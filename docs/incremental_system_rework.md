# Territory Roguelike — Incremental System Rework

> **Goal:** Turn Biomass into a defining progression system that creates rapid number growth, meaningful bottlenecks, chunky decisions, visible physical escalation, and new mechanics that unfold over the run.
>
> **Core rule:** The player should always be **enjoying rapid growth, solving the current bottleneck, or discovering what the ecosystem can do next.**

---

# 1. Why the Original System Needs Reworking

The original system was impactful but too easy to play mindlessly.

Typical loop:

> gain Biomass → click whichever percentage looks useful → repeat

Problems:
- most upgrades were obviously beneficial
- little opportunity cost
- frequent interaction without many interesting decisions
- progression mostly changed numbers
- the system behaved the same way from early to late run

---

# 2. Why the Automatic Metabolism Prototype Failed

The automatic allocation prototype solved repetitive clicking but removed too much interaction.

Observed problem:

> allocate points → watch bars fill

It became less interactive than the original system.

Conclusion:

> **Automation should be earned after the player understands a mechanic, not given from the beginning.**

---

# 3. Research-Derived Structure

Use the following progression loop:

1. Introduce a simple mechanic manually.
2. Let the player understand and optimize it.
3. Create a bottleneck.
4. Give the player a tool to solve that bottleneck.
5. Eventually automate the solved low-level task.
6. Introduce a new higher-order problem.

Avoid:
- passive waiting
- repetitive maintenance
- obvious upgrade spam
- one mandatory build
- showing the entire system at once

---

# 4. Three Progression Timescales

## Every Few Seconds
Dopamine:
- Biomass ticks upward
- production increases
- a purchase becomes affordable
- territory visibly improves

## Every 20–40 Seconds
Meaningful decision:
- buy a chunky upgrade
- solve a bottleneck
- save for a larger purchase
- shore up a weak subsystem

## Every 60–90 Seconds
New mechanic:
- new subsystem
- Breakthrough
- new physical behavior
- earned automation

Exact pacing must come from playtesting.

---

# 5. Keep Biomass as the Main Currency

Do not immediately create multiple currencies.

Biomass should remain:
- easy to understand
- generated by territory
- spent on ecosystem development

New mechanics should usually change what Biomass can do rather than creating another resource.

---

# 6. Territory Is the Economy

Core feedback loop:

> More Green → more Biomass → stronger ecosystem → more Green

The battlefield is the production infrastructure.

Avoid making the economy feel detached from territory.

---

# 7. Keep Simple Repeatable Purchases

Simple purchases are useful for instant satisfaction.

Use three physical development axes.

## Mass
How much territorial material exists.

## Pressure
How aggressively Green moves outward.

## Cohesion
How strongly Green holds together and recovers.

The small purchases are the candy between larger discoveries.

---

# 8. Chunkier Purchase Cadence

Do not require constant clicking.

Initial target:

> one meaningful Biomass purchase every ~20–30 seconds during normal progression

Each purchase should produce:
- visible physical change
- a noticeable production change
- or progress toward a major Breakthrough

---

# 9. Immediate Purchase Feedback

Every purchase should create an instant response.

Examples:
- Green visibly swells
- pressure ripple travels through territory
- damaged Green thickens
- production number jumps
- satisfying sound plays

Avoid invisible upgrades where only a small number changes.

---

# 10. Exponential Costs

Repeatable upgrades should rise strongly in cost.

Conceptual curve:
- 20
- 35
- 60
- 105
- 185
- 325
- 570

Purpose:

> High specialization eventually becomes expensive enough that neglected systems become attractive.

This creates tension between:
- broad efficiency
- deep specialization

---

# 11. Affordable Does Not Mean Correct

A player should sometimes be able to afford an upgrade that is not the best current choice.

Examples:
- too much Pressure can stretch thin Green
- too much Mass can create sluggish territory
- too much Cohesion can overinvest in defense

The economy needs recognizable bottlenecks.

---

# 12. Physical Development Axes

## Mass
Controls:
- thickness
- territory volume
- satellite patch survival
- ability to fill empty space

## Pressure
Controls:
- outward expansion
- frontier force
- corridor penetration
- connection of separated regions

## Cohesion
Controls:
- resistance to tearing
- fragmentation
- recovery
- deformation behavior

These should be visible through the simulation.

---

# 13. Physical Tradeoffs

## High Mass / Low Pressure
> Thick but sluggish.

## Low Mass / High Pressure
> Aggressive but stretched thin.

## High Cohesion
> Small but hard to dismantle.

## Balanced
> Stable all-purpose ecosystem.

No configuration should become universally correct.

---

# 14. Breakthroughs

Every few normal levels, unlock a major behavioral choice.

Prototype:

> Breakthrough at levels 5, 10, 15

Breakthroughs should change the physics, not simply add percentages.

---

# 15. Mass Breakthrough Example

## Rapid Cycling
Generate Mass continuously in many small pulses.

Result:
- smoother growth
- reliable recovery

## Storage Organs
Accumulate Mass and release it in larger periodic bursts.

Result:
- pulsing, explosive growth
- stronger timing interactions

---

# 16. Pressure Breakthrough Example

## Apical Dominance
Pressure concentrates into protrusions.

Result:
- tendrils
- long narrow pushes
- stronger corridor penetration

## Lateral Growth
Pressure distributes across a wide frontier.

Result:
- broad expansion
- less concentrated penetration

---

# 17. Cohesion Breakthrough Example

## Flexible Tissue
Green deforms heavily before breaking and rebounds afterward.

## Woody Tissue
Green resists deformation but fractures more sharply when overloaded.

The player should visibly recognize their choice.

---

# 18. The System Must Unfold

Biomass should gain new capabilities during the run.

Do not reveal everything at the start.

Possible sequence:

## Stage 1
Biomass generation + Mass purchases.

## Stage 2
Pressure system appears.

## Stage 3
Cohesion system appears.

## Stage 4
Transport/connectivity mechanic appears.

## Stage 5
Overflow or another advanced metabolic state appears.

The exact order is tunable.

---

# 19. Example Unlock — Meristem

Early progression unlock:

# MERISTEM DISCOVERED

New capability:

> Spend Biomass to increase territorial Pressure.

Initially manual.

Later, basic Pressure maintenance may become partially automated.

---

# 20. Example Unlock — Cambium

Later:

# CAMBIUM DISCOVERED

New capability:

> Biomass can reinforce and repair Green.

New bottleneck:

> Rapid expansion can now leave the ecosystem structurally weak.

---

# 21. Potential Later Unlock — Vascular Transport

Possible advanced mechanic:

> Nutrient support moves through connected Green rather than acting as if all territory were equally supplied.

Potential consequences:
- isolated patches receive less support
- narrow connections matter
- Mature Green becomes better infrastructure
- Root gains strong interactions

Do not implement until the basic economy is already fun.

---

# 22. Earned Automation

Automation removes solved maintenance.

Example:

## Early
Player manually invests in basic Growth.

## Later Unlock
> Automatically invest 10% of excess Biomass into Mass when Reserve exceeds a chosen threshold.

The reward is:

> You solved this problem. Stop babysitting it.

Then a new decision replaces it.

---

# 23. Automation Must Not Remove Strategy

Automate:
- repetitive low-level spending
- solved maintenance

Do not automate:
- Breakthrough choices
- major build direction
- emergency spending
- reserve timing
- bottleneck solving

---

# 24. Biomass Reserve Must Matter

The player should sometimes choose to save.

Reasons to save:
- upcoming boss
- Breakthrough cost
- emergency recovery
- large cash-out action
- safety buffer

Avoid a system where optimal play is always immediate spending.

---

# 25. Overgrow

Retain one large instant-dopamine action.

# OVERGROW

Prototype:

> Spend a large percentage of current Reserve to inject Mass and Pressure into the territory simulation.

Do not directly paint a fixed amount of Green.

Let physics determine the outcome.

---

# 26. Overgrow Outcomes

## Open Ground
Broad territorial swelling.

## Corridor
Pressure funnels into a jet.

## Damaged Territory
Mass fills holes and reconnects Green.

## Hostile Front
Green compresses against hostile material and seeks weak points.

## Bloom Satellite Patches
Additional Mass helps isolated splats survive and connect.

---

# 27. Overgrow Reflects the Build

## High Mass
Thicker expansion.

## High Pressure
More violent outward surge.

## High Cohesion
More repair and stabilization.

The same button should look different between builds.

---

# 28. Production Milestones

Celebrate major production thresholds.

Prototype:
- 10 Biomass/sec
- 25/sec
- 50/sec
- 100/sec
- 250/sec
- 500/sec

On first reaching one:
- large brief notification
- satisfying sound
- small ecosystem pulse
- stronger UI feedback

Mostly dopamine, not strategy.

---

# 29. Number Escalation

Target obvious acceleration.

Example feeling:

Early:
> +2.8 Biomass/sec

Mid:
> +31/sec

Late:
> +280/sec

Very late:
> +1.2K/sec

Exact values can differ.

---

# 30. Every Order of Magnitude Should Look Different

Do not let economic escalation exist only in the HUD.

Early:
> Green creeps.

Later:
> Green grows.

Then:
> Green pushes.

Then:
> Green flows.

Then:
> Green splatters and reconnects.

Then:
> Green becomes difficult to contain.

The physics simulation is the game's visual version of "number go up."

---

# 31. Optional Later Addition — Critical Harvest

Rare production ticks create a large payout.

Example:

```text
CRITICAL HARVEST
+240 BIOMASS
```

Use mainly for dopamine.

Do not make core builds depend heavily on randomness.

Clover may manipulate these odds later.

---

# 32. Optional Later Addition — Biomass Overflow

If storage reaches capacity:
- excess production is not wasted
- extra Biomass becomes small automatic physical pulses

Fantasy:

> The ecosystem is producing more energy than it can contain.

Do not add until the core economy works.

---

# 33. Solvable Bottlenecks

The economy should regularly create problems the player can diagnose.

## Too Little Mass
Pressure stretches territory too thin.

## Too Little Pressure
Large territory fails to expand aggressively.

## Too Little Cohesion
Enemies shred newly claimed Green.

## Poor Connectivity
Remote territory receives weak support.

## Low Reserve
Player lacks emergency response capacity.

The player should be able to identify and solve these problems.

---

# 34. Progress Walls Must Not Mean Waiting

If the player stalls, the answer should usually be:

> change something

not:

> wait longer

Possible solutions:
- invest in a different axis
- save for Overgrow
- reach a Breakthrough
- improve connectivity
- leverage a Path interaction

Pure waiting should rarely be optimal in a 12-minute action game.

---

# 35. Avoid Mandatory Builds

Specialization should create:
- strengths
- weaknesses
- different physical behavior

It should not create:
- one correct economy
- several dead economies

Suboptimal should mean slower or riskier, not impossible.

---

# 36. Keep Incremental Complexity Below Path Complexity

The game already contains:
- Paths
- Evolutions
- relics
- enemies
- objectives
- physics

Do not add:
- giant Biomass skill trees
- one Biomass tree per Path
- multiple currencies per subsystem

Depth should come from interaction between a small number of systems.

---

# 37. Paths Should Modify the Economy Indirectly

Avoid duplicating Path upgrades inside Biomass.

Bad:
> Spend Biomass for +10% Seed Production.

Better:
> Spore changes how newly created territory interacts with the economy.

Possible examples:

## Spore
Newly germinated Green temporarily improves Mass efficiency.

## Bloom
Pressure-heavy economies help fresh splats spread.

## Bramble
Cohesion investment performs better in heavily controlled Green.

## Root
Mature Green improves production or transport efficiency.

## Pruning
Harvesting sacrifices territorial capital and forces the economy to rebuild.

---

# 38. Physics Is the Output Layer

Biomass choices should alter:
- Mass
- Pressure
- Cohesion
- recovery
- transport
- material state

The simulation decides:
- where Green moves
- where it breaks
- how it reconnects
- what survives

---

# 39. Incremental UI

Persistent HUD:

```text
BIOMASS
428       +24.6/s
```

Detailed bottom drawer:
- upgrade costs
- subsystem levels
- Breakthrough progress
- exact economy information

Do not dedicate a large permanent right-side section to the entire incremental system.

---

# 40. Upgrade Feedback

When buying a normal level:

1. Deduct Biomass.
2. Play a short sound.
3. Trigger a visible world response.
4. Flash the upgraded value briefly.
5. Update the next cost.

The battlefield feedback matters more than the card animation.

---

# 41. Breakthrough Feedback

When reaching a Breakthrough:

1. Pause or slow combat.
2. Show two clear behavioral choices.
3. Explain the physical difference.
4. Select one.
5. Demonstrate the result immediately when possible.

The player should think:

> My ecosystem behaves differently now.

---

# 42. System Unlock Feedback

When a new mechanic unfolds:

1. Brief announcement.
2. One-sentence explanation.
3. Add the control.
4. Let the player use it immediately.

Avoid long tutorials.

---

# 44. Suggested Prototype Unlock Order

Possible 12-minute test sequence:

## Start
Biomass + Mass upgrades.

## ~1:30
Pressure unlocked.

## ~3:00
Cohesion unlocked.

## ~4:00–5:00
First Breakthrough likely.

## ~6:00
Overgrow unlocked or strongly upgraded.

## ~8:00+
Optional second layer of incremental mechanics.

Exact timing must be determined through playtesting.

---

# 45. Playtest Questions

After each run ask:

1. Did I ever wait for Biomass while already knowing exactly what I would buy?
2. Did any purchase change how I played?
3. Could I identify my current bottleneck?
4. Did one upgrade feel mandatory?
5. Did I forget the incremental system existed?
6. Did the economy demand annoying maintenance?
7. Could I visually see my investments in the physics?
8. Did production growth feel dramatic?
9. Did Breakthroughs arrive at satisfying intervals?
10. Did the system become more interesting over time?

---

# 46. Instrumentation

Log:
- Biomass generated
- Biomass spent
- average Reserve
- time near zero Reserve
- time sitting on very high unused Reserve
- purchase timing
- purchase order
- Mass/Pressure/Cohesion levels
- Breakthrough choices
- production milestones reached
- Overgrow uses
- territory gained after purchases
- territory lost after purchases
- time between meaningful Biomass decisions

---

# 47. Warning Signs

## Too Passive
- player forgets Biomass exists
- long gaps between decisions
- automation solves everything

## Too Busy
- purchases every few seconds
- constant sidebar attention
- management feels like maintenance

## Too Obvious
- same upgrade order every run
- always spend immediately
- one axis dominates

## Too Opaque
- player cannot explain why growth stalled
- upgrade effects are hard to predict
- physics response feels random

## Too Weak
- investment choices are not visibly reflected in the simulation

---

# 48. Success Criteria

The incremental system works when:
- the player cares about Biomass/sec
- large purchases feel exciting
- bottlenecks are recognizable
- saving is sometimes correct
- Breakthroughs change physical behavior
- repetitive tasks can eventually be automated
- new systems appear before old ones become boring
- late-game territory looks physically transformed by the economic build

---

# 49. Final Incremental Standard

The system should not feel like:

> a shop attached to a survivor game.

It should feel like:

> **the player is progressively engineering the physical properties of a living ecosystem while the run is happening.**

Ideal loop:

> **grow quickly → hit a bottleneck → identify why → make a chunky investment → watch the simulation transform → production explodes → discover a new mechanic → repeat.**
