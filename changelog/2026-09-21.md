# 2026-09-21

Three flags on the balance bench, what they found, and a rework of Pruning that came out of
it. `--boss` holds a single tough target instead of a crowd, because every number in the pair
sweep so far comes from a sixty-Hunter siege and two Pruning decisions were explicitly parked
on a board that could not read them. `--with` holds one Adaptation at full ranks under an
isolation sweep, because the sweep could not state a single Adaptation fully bought and so had
no baseline for a pair. `--coverage` grows a board to a share of the arena, because the two
existing boards are extremes and each silently zeroes a different class of effect.

What the first of those found was that Pruning's ring contributes 0.01% of the path's damage,
which turned a measurement day into a design one.

Both of those decisions now have an answer. Neither answer is the one the parking note
expected, and the recurring theme is the same one as the last three days: **the instrument
was wrong in a way that left every individual number looking plausible.** Today's count is
six — three in the new mode before it produced a single row, one in the seed pin that has
been wrong the whole time, and two missing rows that had already produced two wrong
conclusions in this document before the row that disproves them existed.

## `--boss`, and what it reports

One boss, replaced the instant it falls, entering at 90px because the walk in is not the
measurement. `--boss queen` is the Hive Queen and `--boss elite` the Rootbreaker.

The headline is deliberately not damage. Damage applied against a lone 780-point body is
nearly quantised by that body's health, while damage *dealt* keeps counting, so `BOSS_ROW`
reports targets felled, mean seconds under fire each, applied, dealt, **wasted %** and mean
spikes. Waste is the eight-target ring cap stated as a number, which is the whole thing a
crowd hides.

Three defects had to be closed before it reported anything:

**The bench was about to pay itself rewards.** A boss dying in a run opens a relic, an
Evolution and a Branch slot, and `RunRoot._on_boss_defeated` calls `unlock_branch_slot()`
*before* anything checks whether a run is in progress. A bench replacing a boss every few
seconds would have handed each row a third Adaptation halfway through being measured — and
the drain would have queued panels on top. `elite_defeated`, `boss_defeated` and
`hive_queen_defeated` are now disconnected from the run. The bench is not a run and takes
no milestones.

**Time-to-kill charged the survivor's seconds to the kills that happened.** Measuring from
spawn also charges the approach, which every row pays alike; measuring to the end of the
bench charges seconds that bought no kill. It now accumulates seconds a boss spent alive
*having already been hit*, closed on death. Cane-only read 39.13s before that and 23.47s
after, so this was most of a build's apparent speed.

**`_sample_control` excluded bosses**, which is correct with twenty-two bodies on the board
and absurd when the boss is the only body: it would have reported slow, tangle and pinned
as 0.000 for every row, and a Bramble or Root row against a single target is paid in
nothing else.

## The patch board reads territory Adaptations as absent against a boss

All four Bramble lines measured *byte-identical* to the Cane alone against the Burrower —
same 1,308 applied, same single kill, the same 540 taken, control columns at exactly 0.000
across six rows. On `--island` the same rows read slow 0.03–0.07, tangle 0.17–0.33, and 540
taken falling to 444 under Repulsion.

So the zero was the board. Six per cent of Green has nothing around the hole a boss stands
in to keep coverage under its feet, and `_update_enemy_bramble_contact` returns early the
moment contact strength hits zero. This is the same defect `--island` was added to the crowd
bench to fix, and it is now noted on the `--boss` flag: **pair the two.** Not forced,
because the gap between the two boards is itself a reading.

Unresolved: whether that hole is the Burrower's own eruption or plain board scarcity. Both
fit the evidence.

## What rooted Pruning actually kills with

The rooted row holds a ring 76.8% of the bench with a mean of **0.17 spikes in it**, and
kills eleven bosses. Walking fills the ring to 5.63 and kills seven. More spikes, fewer
kills — so the spikes are not the weapon.

`_release_ring` is. It takes every orbiting spike, multiplies its damage by `payout`, and
fires it. `orbit_lone_spike_damage_share = 0.06` scales the *aura* — the thicket biting what
is pressed against it — and nothing scales the launch, so a ring holding one spike launches
one full-damage projectile. Rooted Pruning harvests the cell underfoot, raises a spike,
runs its own footing dry, tears, and fires: **93 times in 40 seconds**, one every 0.43s. The
0.17 mean is not a ring failing to work, it is a ring being spent as fast as it is built.

The isolation confirms which line owns it. Pruning alone, one line at a time, against the
Adaptation with nothing bought:

| line | rooted applied | vs base | walking applied | vs base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 2,560 | — | 2,330 | — |
| Charge | 2,572 | **+0.5%** | 2,448 | **+5%** |
| Harvest Rate | 3,132 | +22% | 2,796 | +20% |
| Cutting Yield | 3,204 | +25% | 2,856 | +23% |
| Cutting Edge | 3,450 | +35% | 4,445 | **+91%** |

This is why the path resisted every nerf. `orbit_contact_damage`, `orbit_capacity`, the
lone-spike share and the shed timer all govern the aura; rooted single-target output is the
launch-on-tear stream, which none of them touch. `cutting_damage` does, and it moved in the
same 26 to 12 cut — so the cut landed on the real lever for a reason it was not argued on.

## `torn_ring_charge_share` is a wrong model, not a wrong magnitude

Measured at 0.00, 0.20 and 1.00, with the value restored from backup after each pass:

| share | rooted applied (felled) | walking applied (felled) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 8,717 (11) | 5,964 (7) |
| **0.20 — live** | 8,694 (11) | 5,825 (7) |
| 1.00 | 8,808 (11) | **8,580 (11)** |

Rooted is flat across the parameter's entire range, inside 1.3%, with tear count and mean
spikes identical to three decimals. The arithmetic accounts for it exactly: `payout = 1 +
launch_charge_damage_bonus * release_charge * share`, `_charge` fills over `charge_seconds`
(8.0, about 4.8s at rank 3 through the Charge line), and a ring tearing every 0.43s has
banked roughly 9% of it. The share multiplies a bank the tear has already spent — which is
also why the Charge line is worth +0.5% in the table above.

Walking, 0.20 is indistinguishable from switching the mechanic off: 5,825 against 5,964 at
0.00, with 0.00 marginally *ahead*. The only setting that registers is 1.00, and what it
does is take walking from seven kills to exactly rooted's eleven — erasing the distinction
the parameter exists to draw. A tear paying out like an intentional discharge makes running
dry as good as choosing to fire.

**Left at 0.20 and flagged as the wrong instrument.** If running dry should cost something,
the quantity to scale is the spikes or the launch damage, not a charge multiplier that a
starved ring never has. Yesterday's note called this untested rather than confirmed; it is
now tested, and the finding is about the model.

## `--seed` is not a variance control, and never was

The bench's own comment on `DEFAULT_BENCH_SEED` said the pin fixed each row's critical hits,
Thistle placements and spawn jitter, and that without it the spread was wide enough to put
two Thistle lines below the Adaptation with nothing bought. It pins none of those, because
none of them are random.

- `CombatDirector` holds no gameplay RNG at all. Its repeated choices run off deterministic
  counters — `_briar_trap_sequence`, `_root_eruption_sequence`, `_fruiting_body_sequence` —
  and its one `RandomNumberGenerator` is seeded for audio.
- `SporeDirector` hardcodes `_spore_random.seed = 0x5EED10`. `RelicDirector` hardcodes
  `0x0FF1CE5`. The run seed reaches neither.
- What it does reach: Progression's draft and relic draws, which a bench writing
  `_upgrade_levels` directly never performs; the wave director, which this loop does not
  run; the Canker director; and `AdaptationDirector._random`, whose rolls are Apical and
  Wisteria — Evolution and relic features, inactive without `--evolved`.

Measured: two seeds, byte-identical rows. 11,887 damage, 423 kills, 105 Seeds sown, every
column to the digit, in two separate processes.

That cuts both ways and the comment now says so. No row needs a second seed to be trusted.
But no row can be *checked* for seed-dependence either, so the "two seeds agreeing is a
result; one is an anecdote" rule the sweep documents invoke cannot be exercised with this
tool as it stands. A reading is one sample of one configuration. Every number above is one
such sample, including the walking 1.00 row — an attempted second-seed confirmation of it
reproduced the first exactly, which confirmed nothing and is how the seed pin came to be
examined at all.

## Holding the ring is the wrong play, and every row says so

Walking against a boss, **every single Vine line beats all four of them together**:

| row | applied | felled | tears | spikes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apical Dominance only | **6,328** | 8 | 84 | 2.69 |
| Lignification only | 6,240 | 8 | 65 | 2.47 |
| Turgor only | 6,212 | 7 | 80 | 2.72 |
| Elongation only | 6,167 | 7 | 82 | 2.82 |
| all four Vine lines | 5,961 | 7 | 50 | 5.56 |
| Pruning alone | 4,976 | 6 | 60 | 4.43 |

The column that tracks it is tears, and spikes move the opposite way. It holds across every
row in every table today: **fewer spikes held and more tears means more damage.** Rooted is
the extreme — 0.17 spikes, 93 tears, 8,785 — and it is the strongest row benched.

The mechanism is the one from the section above. A tear launches full-damage projectiles;
the aura it replaces is scaled by `orbit_lone_spike_damage_share`. So the lone-spike scaling
that was added to stop the ring's damage being binary has made *building* a ring a mistake.
The path's stated identity is a thicket she builds by cutting fresh ground and loses by
standing on ground she has already cut. The measurement says the optimal play is to thrash
it: raise one spike, tear, repeat, and never hold anything.

This is confined to the single-target case. On siege the four lines stack normally and
nearly double Pruning, because there the aura has eight bodies to bite and the ring is worth
holding. It is the lone-boss fight where the incentive inverts.

`--isolate` bought one line at a time; `--pair` and `--build` bought every line of two or

baseline every pair figure in the sweep documents is implicitly read against. `--with <path>`

Both wrong conclusions were mine, earlier today, and both came from using the bare mechanic
as the baseline because the real one did not exist:

- **"Vine triples Pruning's tear rate, 36 to 93."** It does not. Pruning *fully bought*

The open question in yesterday's notes about whether roughly thirty tears in forty seconds

## Pruning, reworked

Everything above is the diagnosis. Apy's reading of it was that the ring should be the
main source of the path's damage, and then -- on seeing that a ring lethal enough to be
that would kill a 42-point Hunter on contact and leave its own interior unreachable --
that the whole design was flawed. Both were right, and the second one is the useful one.

### The mechanism, and why no lever could have fixed it

Pruning had one source of damage and it was an error branch.
`_advance_gathering_cutting` handles a Cutting that finishes spiralling in when she has no
Green underfoot: there is no ring for it to join, so it flew. At full damage, never
entering the ring, never passing the release -- so nothing that governs the ring touched
it. And the harvest takes the cell she is standing on, which makes being off her own
footing the *ordinary* state of a hold: 58% of a rooted bench.

So the positional cost was also the damage engine, and failure was the optimal play.
Measured against a boss, zeroing the ring's damage cost a developed Pruning **one point
out of 7,595**; zeroing the Cuttings cost it **78%**.

That explains the whole nerf history. `orbit_contact_damage`, `orbit_capacity`, the
lone-spike share, the shed timer, `torn_ring_charge_share`, the Charge line -- every lever
ever reached for governs the ring, and the ring was not the weapon. Yesterday's 26 -> 12
cut was tuning a term worth 0.01%.

**And it could not be fixed by pricing the launch down.** At a 30% share, `Harvest Rate`
measured *below* the bare Adaptation: the harvest's output in that state is launches and
nothing else, so cheaper launches means a worthless harvest line. The launch payout and the
upgrade tree are the same pipe. Confirmation came from removing the payout outright --
every row of Pruning collapsed onto the bare Cane, +0.3% for the whole developed
Adaptation, damage taken identical at 540. A path with no mechanism.

### What changed

**Failure loses leaves instead of firing them.** Both unchosen routes -- a Cutting that
gathers with no footing, and a ring that tears rather than being spent -- now drop what
they hold. Only a deliberate discharge fires, so the hold-bank-spend burst survives intact
while the exploit stops existing rather than becoming weak.

**The harvest spares a standing pad.** `upkeep_inner_radius_pixels` 0 -> 20. This was the
closed loop: harvesting destroyed the footing that let a harvested leaf join the ring.
Working an annulus keeps her footing while stillness still ends the ring -- she strips the
reachable ground, the harvest finds nothing, and leaves wilt one at a time. Same cost, paid
in a ring that recedes instead of one that fires.

**A wilting leaf plants Green where it falls.** Apy's idea, and the one that did the most
work. The failure mode becomes generative: running dry replants the ground that refills the
ring, so the loop corrects itself instead of needing to be prevented from closing. Lossy on
purpose -- a leaf costs ~4.5 cells and a Seed grows after it lands, so a generous share
would make Pruning a net producer of territory and turn the ground cost into a profit.
Fresh growth rather than a Bloom, or it would manufacture exactly what Root amplifies from.

**The ring's damage is a budget divided by what stands in it.**
`orbit_maximum_targets_per_tick` was written to bound an aura whose output is per-enemy
damage times enemy count, and it does not: it caps bites per *frame*, and sixty enemies on
0.35s cooldowns demand ~171 a second against a cap permitting 480. Ring output was fully
proportional to density, which is why it was simultaneously feeble against a boss and, once
the radius grew, absurd against a crowd -- a flat 20-point aura measured **151,732** on
siege against a Cane-only 15,248. Dividing by bodies in reach makes total output constant at
about `2.86 * budget`, whatever it faces.

That also produces the path's arc without anyone imposing it: four enemies take a quarter
of the budget each and die, sixty take a sixtieth and shrug, so the ring's worth moves from
killing to covering as a run goes on. Which is the role change Apy asked for, falling out of
the mechanic rather than being written into a table.

**Growth became the investment.** `orbit_radius_growth_pixels` 26 -> 110, so a full ring
reaches 154px -- a place rather than a halo -- and a starved one recedes to 44. Safe to
scale now that growth buys area and never damage.

**The ring recedes to a floor rather than dying.** `minimum_orbit_leaves` 1, with
`wilt_warning_seconds` 0.50 flaring the leaves before the first one goes. Stillness costs
*area*, which is the currency the path trades in, paid continuously and visibly. A binary
loss would be worse: regrowing needs unharvested Green that is not always adjacent, so a
ring that died outright would take longer to rebuild than the pause that killed it, and the
cost would be a tax on pausing rather than a decision about where to stand.

**Leaves arrive one at a time.** `leaf_raise_interval_seconds` 0.45, held slightly slower
than the 0.40s wilt so a ring is lost marginally faster than it is built. Unthrottled, a
developed harvest raises thirteen leaves a second -- a full ring in a little over one,
appearing faster than it can be read.

### The upgrade lines, remapped

Three of four had nothing to push on once the ring was the output, so base Pruning and
fully-developed Pruning measured within 3%. Each line now owns one axis:

- **Cutting Yield -> capacity.** `orbit_capacity` 16 -> 8 base, the line earning the rest
  back. Fullness is measured against a separate fixed `orbit_reference_leaves`, because
  dividing by capacity means raising capacity raises the denominator and the upgrade buys
  nothing -- eight of eight and sixteen of sixteen are both a full ring.
- **Cutting Edge -> the ring's budget.** It scaled only loose Cuttings and the volley, so
  once those were demoted the path's damage line stopped touching the path's damage.
- **Harvest Rate -> the wilt interval,** on top of the cells it already bought.
- **Charge** unchanged, and finally worth something: it was +0.5% against a boss because a
  ring tearing every 0.43s never banked any.

## Three corrections, and what caught them

Each of these was mine, and each was caught by measuring one change at a time. They are
recorded because a future session would otherwise repeat them.

**Three coupled changes at once, unattributable.** Growth, capacity and the tear share went
in together; walking rose 25%, rooted fell 8%, and `Harvest Rate` came out *below* the bare
mechanic on siege. No way to tell which change did what. Reverted to baseline and redone one
at a time -- which is the session's own lesson, ignored while applying it to everything else.

**Raising capacity was inert by construction.** Both damage and radius divided by
`orbit_capacity`, so the upgrade raised its own denominator. Fixed with a separate fixed
reference.

**The leaf throttle silently killed Harvest Rate.** Capping leaves at one per interval means
a faster harvest banks debt that cannot be spent, so the line measured +0.0% -- identical to
the digit. Three wirings later it still reads ~0, and the likeliest reason is that
`--isolate` buys one line: with one line bought the ring holds 1.1 leaves, so cadence
rebuilds a ring already at its floor and resilience protects nothing. A line that only pays
off in combination cannot be seen by a sweep that buys lines alone.

## Two more instrument gaps, closed

**`--with <path>`, an `all lines` row, and a `<partner> alone` row.** `--isolate` bought one
line; `--pair` and `--build` bought every line of two or three Adaptations. Nothing could
state **one Adaptation at full ranks** -- the baseline every pair figure in the sweep
documents is implicitly read against. It had already produced two wrong conclusions in this
very document: that Vine triples Pruning's tear rate (Pruning fully bought tears 96 by
itself; Vine takes it to 93) and that Vine triples Pruning in the pair (it adds 15.7%;
Pruning fully bought alone is 7,595 of the pair's 8,785). Both came from using the bare
mechanic as a baseline because the real one did not exist.

**`--coverage N`.** The two boards were extremes and neither is a run: the patch settles near
6% and reads every territory Adaptation as absent against a boss, the island reaches 91% and
reads anything paid for in *losing* ground as worthless -- Pruning holds a ring 100% of a
bench there and tears none. It grows the board to a coverage rather than for a duration.
Its own limit is documented on the flag: natural spread takes a 30% board to ~90% within
forty seconds, so it buys a sparse opening that develops, not a board held at a coverage.

## The harvest was a closed circuit, and the smoke suite is what found it

The rework above measured well and was broken in ways no damage column can show. The
integration suite caught five failures; three were real defects and two were contracts the
rework had deliberately changed. Fixing each exposed the next, so five became six.

**The bench measures magnitudes. The suite measures promises.** A mechanism rework needs
both, and hours of careful measurement today would have shipped a Pruning whose payout never
fired, whose evolution did nothing, and which opened every run on a stale timer -- none of
which moves a number the bench reads.

### The deliberate discharge had stopped firing

A tear loses its leaves now, and a discharge she chose still throws them. The two are told
apart at the call site, and the proxy it used -- Pruned Ground underfoot means the harvest
ate it while she stood there, bare ground means she walked off and meant it -- cannot tell
them apart after a harvest, because then all the ground around her is Pruned. The note at
that site records a previous session trying this exact change and reverting it for exactly
that reason. I read `_release_ring` and never the eighty lines above it that decide what to
pass in.

Half that note no longer applies: it reverted on a measurement of "4% either way", taken
when the ring did not exist and the damage came from the launch stream. In a working ring the
same change removes 78%, so the magnitude argument is dead while the classification argument
stands. Solving the classification was therefore the right response rather than reverting.

Three attempts, and the failure of each says something:

1. **Pruned ground underfoot** -- the original. Cannot distinguish stepping aside from
   starving in place.
2. **Whether she is moving at release time** -- mine. The release is `footing_grace_seconds`
   later, over a second, and by then she is usually still either way. Reads the wrong moment.
3. **Whether she was moving when the footing went**, latched on that frame. Correct, and it
   is why the proxy existed at all: the code needed to know about a moment it had already
   left, and ground state was the only thing that persisted. A flag does the same job
   without the ambiguity.

### A throttle timer leaked across resets

`_leaf_raise_remaining` was added without being added to `reset_director`, so a stale gate
survived into the next run and its first harvest cycle raised nothing. Caught by the
Coppicing test, which resets the director and expects one cycle to produce Cuttings.

### Deadfall had been silently voided

Its thorn yield banked against a gate it did not know about. Quickening the interval was
tried first and cannot work -- the cap inside one cycle is one leaf either way, so the extra
yield never arrives and the evolution measures identical to not owning it. Deadfall's
contract is taking the ring "from shedding a thorn occasionally to firing them continuously,
which is exactly what base Pruning must not do on its own", so the gate that makes base
Pruning legible is the thing the evolution buys past, not a number it tunes.

That is the first instalment of the rework's relic bill, and it sets the pattern for the
remaining four: re-express each against the ring, using its own existing parameter where it
has one.

### And then the real one: standing still was free

The rewritten stillness test failed with **"peaked at 8 leaves, ended at 8, launched 0"**.
Sixty seconds of standing perfectly still ended with the ring completely full, having wilted
not one leaf.

`drain_territory_in_radius` filters candidates on claimable, ownership, maturity band and
distance. It sets `_pruned_ground` when it cuts and **never reads it when choosing what to
cut next**. So a cut cell drops to zero control, `_pruned_regrowth_per_second` -- which exists
to make cut ground recover faster than ordinary ground -- pushes it back over the ownership
threshold, and it is eligible again. The harvest re-ate the same ring of cells indefinitely.

Two systems each doing their stated job, composing into a circuit with no scarcity in it. The
escalating drain is documented as "what gives every hold an end" and could never do it,
because the supply it drained refilled itself behind it.

**This also explains the original design.** Eating her own footing was not really the
positional cost; it was the only thing in the path creating scarcity at all. Remove it -- as
the spared pad did -- and nothing was scarce anywhere, which is why the ring became
permanently stable the moment the loop opened, and why that was three times misattributed to
the bench board. Excluding pruned ground from the cut separates the two: the pad can spare her
footing while the ground itself stays finite.

The same test now reads **"peaked at 5 leaves, ended at 1, launched 0"**, and the suite is
green.

### What the scarcity changed

Pruning alone, all four lines, boss on `--coverage 30`:

| | rooted | walking |
|---|---|---|
| applied | 1,560 (2 felled) | **4,680 (6 felled)** |
| mean leaves | 1.10 | 10.94 |
| ring held | 62.3% | 95.2% |

Walking is three times rooted. This morning rooted measured 7,595 against walking's 4,976 --
the exact inverse -- and "the ring is maintained by walking it onto fresh Green, not by
parking on a good spot" has been in the file the whole time describing something that was not

The lines, walking, against a base of 2,265: Charge +8%, Cutting Yield +21%, **Harvest Rate

### Two tests rewritten, and why that is not laundering

Both asserted contracts the rework changed on purpose, and both were rewritten as positive
statements of the new behaviour rather than inversions of the old:

- "the harvest cuts the ground out from under the player" became "the harvest works a ring
  around the player and leaves her footing standing", checking the inner radius is non-zero,
  that no cut lands inside it, and that the cell under her stays owned.
- "a ring held on one patch of Green burns its own footing out and fires" became "withers
  instead of firing", checking that leaves peak above the floor, end below that peak, and
  that **nothing is launched**. That last clause is the one that matters: the old test
  asserted the volley, so a rewrite that only checked the leaf count would have let the
  reward-for-failure return unnoticed.

Both also stopped reading `orbit_capacity` directly, since it is now the base rather than the
effective value.

## Not done

The rework's own bill first, largest to smallest:

- **Four relics and evolutions are stranded.** Espalier's fan, Brush Pile, Splinterstorm and
  Stubble are written against the launch stream that no longer exists in sustained play, so
  they are dead picks right now -- a balance regression introduced by the rework. Deadfall is
  done and sets the pattern for the rest: re-express each against the ring, using its own
  existing parameter where it has one.
- **The pair sweep is stale.** Every balance figure in the sweep documents predates the
  rework, and Pruning+Vine was the game's top pair. Nothing here has measured Pruning
  against another Adaptation, so *whether the balance improved is unanswered* -- only whether
  the mechanism works, which it now does. Re-run the 28 pairs on `--coverage 30` before
  acting on any of it.
- ~~The lines are unevenly weighted~~ and ~~whether Harvest Rate is worthless~~ both closed
  once the harvest had a finite supply: walking, the four lines measure +8%, +21%, +22% and
  +32% over a bare Adaptation. Harvest Rate was unmeasurable rather than worthless -- it
  needed a ring to work on and a scarcity to protect it from. Left recorded because three
  wirings were tried against a rigged board before the board was the suspect.
- The siege row on `--coverage 30` after the scarcity fix was never captured; the run was
  stopped on system memory pressure. Boss rooted and walking are measured, siege is not.
- **Every number is a placeholder.** Budget 16, growth 110, leaf share 0.35, cadence 0.45,
  wilt floor 1, warning 0.50 -- calibrated against each other and nothing else.
- Whether the stillness pressure is *right*, now that it exists: a held ring peaks at five
  leaves and withers to one. Whether that reads as pressure or as nagging is the question
  yesterday parked, and it still needs a played run rather than a measurement.
- Whether Pruning needs a slow at all, and therefore whether Bramble must move off slow.
  Damage taken is 192 walking against the Cane's 192 and 480 rooted against 540, with no slow
  whatsoever -- so the defensive contribution is now mostly positional, and this is less
  urgent than it looked. Which is the right outcome for a question that was going to be
  decided blind.

Carried over, unchanged:

- Whether Vine's four lines anti-stacking against a lone walking target is acceptable as a
  target-count property or is a bug in how spike count gates the aura.
- Vine's decomposition is done on two boards but not with `--evolved`, which is the
  configuration where `AdaptationDirector._random` starts rolling and the seed stops being
  inert.
- Whether the Bramble hole under a boss is the eruption or board scarcity.
- Bramble's Grasping Thorns and Repulsion measure byte-identical to the bare Adaptation in
  every crowd-bench column but `pinned`. Expected for two control lines in a damage table,
  or another inert lever; not chased.
- `--boss queen` and `--boss elite` are implemented and unrun. The Queen summons, so she is
  deliberately not a single-target reading.
- Territory Trials (Phase 9), by instruction.
- **Nobody has played any of this**, and three separate design questions today came down to
  it: whether the warning flash reads as pressure or nagging, whether a grinding ring is
  satisfying or passive, and whether a safe interior reads as intentional or as a bug when a
  boss is standing on her. None are measurable from here.
