# 2026-09-19

Phases 1 through 8 of `docs/campaign_pacing_balance_rework.md`, in one pass. Campaign is
twenty minutes in four acts, a wave is a threat budget rather than a spawn rate, the player
can call the next wave in early and pay for it permanently, and an Evolution is no longer
decided by which two Adaptations a run happened to take first.

Phase 9 (Territory Trials) is deliberately not here: the rework says to prototype it only
after the base system is fun, and nothing has played the base system yet.

## The thing the rework is actually about

The measured Campaign reached Evolution rank 3 around minute thirteen and then ran for
another twelve. Half of it was spent after the build had finished becoming itself, and the
only lever the wave table had left for those twelve minutes was density — which in this
game is an *input* to a mature build. More bodies make a Vine sweep, a Burr ricochet and a
Pruning ring more efficient, not less. The old Wave 25 requested roughly five hundred
enemies across its minute and the 124-actor reinforcement cap threw most of them away,
which is what "harder" had come to mean.

So: twenty waves, and a wave is a budget.

## Campaign structure

`CampaignWaves` is twenty rows in four acts — Germination, Encroachment, Infestation,
Blight — at 1200 seconds. Each row carries a **threat budget** and a list of **encounter
recipes** it may buy, in place of a pack interval, a pack pattern and a table of role
weights. The six pressure curves keep their authored endpoints and reach them across
twenty waves instead of twenty-five, which is what makes the last quarter the hardest part
of the run rather than its longest plateau.

Milestones moved with the structure: the Elite at wave 5, the Burrower at 10, the Hive
Queen at 15, the finale at 20. Eight wave relics plus three milestone relics is eleven
relic opportunities, which is the same number the twenty-five-wave table produced.

## The Threat Director

`EncounterRecipes` prices every role and names thirteen packs. `ThreatDirector` releases a
wave's budget across it on an authored rhythm — establish, build, peak, recover — and hands
back a purchase when the released credit covers its price.

**Recipe first, then price**, which is the part that matters. Buying whatever the credit
currently affords sounds equivalent and is not: the cheap recipes are always affordable, so
credit never accumulates and a wave with a large budget buys nothing but Probes. Choosing
the pack and *then* saving for it is what makes a big budget read as bigger encounters.

Each recipe names a minimum act, and that is the enemy pool gate: a wave cannot buy a Siege
Push in Act I however badly it is going. `SPLIT_PRESSURE` sends its second half to the
opposite sector. `CANKER_REINFORCEMENT` asks the Canker director for an entrance and is
simply not bought when the run holds no Knot — the Director never makes the Canker it comes
out of.

The four-phase rhythm exists to create the recovery tail. Without a quiet ending a wave has
nothing to give up, and Provocation is the mechanic that spends it.

## Provocation, Blight, Surge, Growth

`E` calls the next wave in, and the HUD says once per wave when the window opens — this is
the Campaign's main difficulty dial and the player chooses it, so being told it exists is
part of the mechanic rather than polish on top of it. Everything on the board stays on it,
the recovery is skipped, and the wave arrives with a Surge — credit injected at its front,
proportional to the seconds given up.

The schedule moves; the clock does not. `elapsed_seconds` is the Campaign's *position* and
Provocation advances it, while `real_elapsed_seconds` is what the player actually played.
A provoked run therefore finishes Wave 20 in seventeen minutes and records seventeen
minutes, which is the number a result screen is about.

Total compression is capped at four minutes across the run. Uncapped, nineteen calls at
twenty-odd seconds each would take a twenty-minute Campaign under thirteen; the structure
needs time to breathe however far ahead of it the player is. The cap is all-or-nothing per
call — a shortened skip would charge a Blight level and leave the run stranded in the
middle of the wave it was trying to end — so as the budget runs down only later, smaller
calls still fit, and Provocation tapers rather than stopping dead.

Availability has two doors: forty seconds into the wave, or most of the wave's released
threat already cleared off the board. The second reads threat the Director spent and
enemies that are no longer standing — never Grandma's damage, her build or her Paths. The
Campaign responds to what the player has *done*.

Each rewarded Provocation raises **Blight** one level, permanently, to a cap of V. Nearly
all of it is Director budget (+8%/level) with health and damage moving at a fraction of
that (+4% and +2%), because the difficulty a player chose should arrive as harder
encounters rather than as the same encounter with a longer time-to-kill. It also discounts
the expensive recipes slightly, so a Blight V wave meets the costly compositions more often
out of the same authored list.

Surviving a provoked wave pays a **Growth**: one of three universal stats, capped per stat,
additive, and worth less in total than one Adaptation. Damage lands at
`CombatDirector._damage_enemy` and area at `blast_enemies_in_radius`, which are the single
funnels every point of damage and every area effect in the game pass through — a universal
reward applied per-caller would be a different number for each Path and would silently miss
whichever effect somebody forgot. Earlier calls buy Surge, never more Growth: paying more
for an earlier call is the feedback loop the whole rework exists to avoid.

## Three Adaptations, and Evolutions that are not locked in advance

Campaign owns three Adaptations rather than four, and hands all three out on its wave table
— waves 1, 3 and 5 — through a dedicated Adaptation draft. Ordinary level-ups in Campaign
can no longer commit a new Path at all. The measured pacing showed early draft ordering
deciding whether a build functioned, and randomness is supposed to shape how a build
develops, not whether the player is allowed to have one. Quick keeps four Paths and its
milestone-driven Branches, unchanged.

`Evolutions` now holds a **set**. Any owned pair with five owned ordinary ranks in each half
is eligible, a Path may take part in more than one, and claiming one spends neither of
them. The reward is its own panel opened at waves 8, 14 and 18 — never a card inside a
level-up — offering up to three of whatever the build has actually developed.

`get_evolution_upgrade_level()` was the dangerous part of this. While a run could hold only
one Evolution, "the Evolution's rank" and "Deadfall's rank" were the same number and every
caller asked for the first; with two claimed at once they are not. Every mechanical caller
now names its Evolution through `get_evolution_rank(id)`, and the one place that already
read two Evolutions' ranks off one number — Briar Payload and Ancient Fury sharing
`evolution_rank` in `_apply_progression_modifiers` — was a latent bug that only a single
Evolution was hiding.

## Cankers

Unchanged in what makes one: territory failure, and nothing else. What changed is how much
room the Campaign gives them. Act I is Canker-free — the arena opens with a hostile basin
the run has not been given the tools to contest, and a Knot there is a timer rather than a
consequence — and the cap climbs with the act: one interior front in Act II, two in Act
III, three in Act IV. A run at Blight III or higher gets one more, which is the Director
and the territory system talking without merging: Blight does not create a Knot, it widens
the room for ground the player is already failing to hold.

## Known outliers

**Thornwhip** is fixed as specified. A mounted head fires one thorn at double damage instead
of a full Clustering volley, which removes an entire multiplicative axis — the Evolution was
multiplying mounted heads, Clustering count, Burr ricochets, Needlepoint crits and Vine
cadence all at once. Its advantage over an unevolved Thistle + Vine drops from roughly
+50/70/115% at ranks 1/3/5 to +16/36/67%.

Deliberately **not** a Thistle damage cut. Base Thistle is not dominant in the measured pair
sweep; one Evolution's interaction is, and the fix belongs where the interaction is rather
than on every Thistle run that never took it.

**Burr ricochet falloff and Vine Elongation are untouched**, as the rework asks. Vine's four
per-rank values are now exported instead of written into `_refresh_vine_modifiers`, because
answering which Vine line is carrying its dominance means benchmarking them one at a time
and a benchmark that needs a recompile between values is a benchmark that does not get run.
**Pruning is untouched** and stays on the watch list.

## Early Paths

**Root** gets a floor. It is an amplifier, so a Root build with nothing yet to amplify
measured at bare-Cane output — and a Path whose first rank does nothing visible is one the
player cannot tell they have taken. Mature ground under Grandma now makes the Cane itself
heavier, at half the share a line she invested in receives. A floor, not a conversion into a
generic damage Path.

**Bramble**'s thorns did 0.4 damage a second before Entanglement, which is less than a
rounding error against anything the run meets; chip damage is up threefold and still chip.
The more important half is the base slow, 15% to 25%: Bramble's rank-1 problem was that an
Adaptation whose whole identity is control produced no control the player could see.

**Spore** and **Bloom** were slow rather than small — five kills a Seed and thirteen seconds
a Bloom meant most of the opening minute with nothing to look at. Four and eleven. Neither
change touches what a Seed or a Bloom *is*, or how either line scales.

These four are the least confident changes in the pass and all of them are exported. The
rework asks for a rank-1 pair sweep afterwards and that has not been run.

## Tests

`wave_cadence_probe` covers Provocation: that wave 1 is not provokable at zero seconds, that
calling it advances the wave without advancing the wall clock, and that it raises Blight.
`campaign_pressure_probe` prints budgets, act gates, recipe prices and the Blight table in
place of the old requested-density column. `campaign_relic_count_probe` counts off the
Campaign's own wave table instead of Quick's repeating cycle — it agreed with the table by
luck at twenty-five waves and stopped agreeing at twenty. `run_mode_probe` checks the
three-Adaptation limit and authored drafts, and that switching back to Quick restores four
and does not leave Campaign's rules behind. `root_new_adaptations_probe` now checks
Lignification *and* Root's Cane floor together, so neither can quietly absorb the other.
The Evolution blocks in `smoke/milestones` and `smoke/evolutions` test an Evolution reward
panel and a Branch that is half of a relationship.

`campaign_density_probe` reads 9 alive at minute 0.5, 45 at minute 7, and pinned at the
126-actor cap from minute 12 to the end. That is the probe's passive player rather than the
budget: it stands still with a Cane and its path directors stop the moment the first
level-up panel it never answers opens, so almost nothing on that board is being killed. It
is a useful ceiling and it is not a measurement of what a played Campaign looks like.

## The measurement pass, and three defects in the instrument

Then the gated half: sections 10, 11 and 33 all say "measure before you change", and the
thing that would do the measuring turned out to have three faults in it. All three biased
the same way — against control kits and against single-target kits — which is the direction
the pair sweep's conclusions ran.

**The bench was unseeded.** `reset_run()` rolls a fresh run seed unless one is authored and
the bench never authored one, so every row carried its own crits, placements and spawn
jitter. Two of Thistle's four lines measured *below* Thistle with nothing bought, which a
line cannot do. Pinned now, with `--seed` to re-check a reading on a second one.

**The board had six per cent Green on it.** One Bloom grown for seven seconds, with the
held population entering from a ring outside it — and Bramble tangles enemies standing *on
Green*, Root amplifies from Mature Green, Spore germinates into it. `--island` grows a 92%
board instead. Bramble+Root at rank 3 goes from slow 0.000 / tangle 0.005 / pinned 0.000 /
24 points of ground lost, to slow 0.409 / tangle 0.574 / pinned 0.239 / one point lost. On
the old board three of Bramble's four lines were bit-identical to buying nothing.

**The damage column was counting kills.** `debug_damage_applied_total` clips each hit to
remaining health, so damage/kills sat at 28–29 in every row of every sweep and any source
landing on targets something else was already killing read as zero. `debug_damage_dealt_total`
now carries the unclipped figure beside it; the gap is overkill. A cane-only row was added
to the isolation mode as well, because on a crowded board the universal Cane is most of
the damage in every row.

Also new: `--build a-b-c` and `--archetypes` for three-Adaptation rosters, `--isolate P`
for one Adaptation's lines one at a time, and four control columns sampled off the live
roster four times a second. `pacing_probe` gained `--build` and `--provoke never|moderate|
aggressive`, and now reports Blight, provocations and Growth per minute.

## Two decisions the measurements settle

**Vine Elongation stays.** Over a cane-only baseline, Vine's four lines contribute +13,456
(Lignification), +5,596 (Turgor), +2,988 (Elongation) and +2,640 (Apical) applied damage at
rank 3. Reach is the second weakest, at about a fifth of Lignification — same ordering on
both boards, both metrics, both seeds. Section 11 makes the cut conditional on reach being
a primary source of Vine's dominance and it plainly is not. Vine is strong because
Lignification multiplies a weapon every build already owns.

**Burr keeps its ricochets.** Thistle at rank 3 with its best line is about eight per cent
over the Cane, and Burr is +840 of that — its *strongest* line, measured under siege
geometry, which is the best case a ricochet will ever see. Cutting it would only deepen the
hole. Section 10 asks for Thistle to be evaluated independently first; independently it is
carrying nothing, and whatever made Thistle+Vine the top pair was Vine and Thornwhip.

(Re-measured after the pause defect below. On the broken bench Burr read second at +676 and
Needlepoint read exactly zero; corrected, Burr is first and Needlepoint contributes +232.
The decision is the same either way.)

## A change this pass reversed

The early-Path buff to Bramble's chip damage (0.18 → 0.55) was made on the patch-board
reading. Measured properly, on a board Bramble has ground on:

| Bramble, no lines, rank 3, siege | applied | kills | over Cane | slow | tangle | pinned |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.18 chip | 18,771 | 656 | +3,523 | 0.049 | 0.389 | 0.012 |
| 0.55 chip | 25,950 | 912 | +10,702 | 0.040 | 0.315 | 0.009 |

It tripled Bramble's contribution and *lowered* its slow, Entanglement and pin — bodies
that die faster never live long enough to tangle. That turns the control Adaptation into a
damage Adaptation, which is the one thing the line is documented not to do.

Chip is back to 0.18 and the max-health share to 0.0005. **The base slow increase stays**
(0.15 → 0.25): it is visible at rank 1, costs no kill throughput, and is the axis section 7
asked for.

Worth stating plainly, because it is the argument for having done the measurement pass at
all: Bramble's base mechanic with nothing bought adds real damage over the Cane on
developed ground — +18% applied once the pause defect below was also fixed — from the
Adaptation the sweep called near-worthless.

One correction to that, from the re-measurement: Bramble *alone* barely improves survival
(336 taken against the Cane's 363). The zero-damage lockdown belongs to Bramble **+ Root** —
Entanglement scaled by Mature ground through the Network multiplier. The pair is the
mechanism, which is what a pair relationship is supposed to mean. Also:
Repulsion is bit-identical to base in all eleven columns and appears to do nothing
measurable — flagged, not chased, since it is not a rework item.

## Two of the four early-Path changes had never run at all

The measurement confirming the Bramble revert came back bit-identical to the pre-change
run in all eleven columns — including the slow column that the same pass had supposedly
raised. `run.tscn` sets `base_slow_fraction = 0.1` and `bloom_interval_seconds = 15.0`, and
a scene value beats a script default. The early-Path pass edited the defaults. The game was
running 0.1 slow and 15-second Blooms the whole time.

Exports are this project's Inspector design surface, so the scene is authoritative for
anything it sets and a tuning pass that edits script defaults is tuning a fallback nobody
reads. Both defaults now say so in a comment.

- **Bramble base slow is now 0.25 in `run.tscn`**, where it takes effect.
- **Bloom's default goes back to 13.0 and the scene is left at 15.0** — no Bloom change
  lands. Re-applying it in the scene would be a new unmeasured change on the same biased
  reading that produced the chip mistake, so the rank-1 Bloom item stays open instead of
  being guessed at twice.

The upside: it makes §9.6's finding cleaner. Both chip rows were taken at 0.1 slow, so the
control drop came entirely from bodies dying before they could tangle.

With the slow applied in the scene where it counts, Bramble base at 0.18 chip goes from
slow 0.049 / 656 kills to **slow 0.111** / 635 kills — measured control more than doubled
for about 2.6% of kill throughput. That is the exact inverse of the chip buff's shape, and
it is the one early-Path change in this pass that is now evidenced rather than assumed.

## A fifth instrument defect, and the worst of them: the bench paused itself

Spore looked inert — identical to owning nothing, at rank 1 and rank 3. It was not. Seeds
were sown correctly all along (36 from 144 kills, 48 with a rank of Seed Production). None
of them ever opened.

`_refresh_simulation_pause()` sets `effects_paused` on the overlay and `simulation_paused`
on the field and the player, fires on any heavy hit, and is only ever cleared by
`RunRoot._process` — which a bench that steps everything by hand never calls. One heavy hit
froze those three systems for the rest of the row.

| Spore, no lines, rank 1 | sown | germinated | green | biomass | taken |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| paused | 36 | 0 | 5.92 | 8.7 | 6 |
| fixed | 36 | 33 | 9.33 | 11.2 | 330 |

The last column is the tell. The sweep document's §1 diagnosed "the same 6 points of damage
for every pair" correctly and added `player._physics_process` to fix it; that call was
being swallowed by the early return on `simulation_paused`, so the symptom never went away.

The bench now forces all three flags false every step and reports `sown`, `seeds` and
`blooms`, so a row that reads as nothing can say whether the mechanic fired and did nothing
or never fired.

**What changed.** Rank 1 is a different table: nothing is inert, the weakest pair is +12%
over the bare Cane rather than +0%, and Pruning — previously reading as zero at rank 1 — is
the strongest rank-1 Adaptation. Rank 3 barely moved. **The Bramble+Root result survives and
is stronger**: zero damage taken before and after, against a baseline that went from 6 to
363.

The real rank-1 story is damage taken. Pruning and Vine openings take 6–28 over forty
seconds; everything else takes 200–330. That is where a weak opening actually loses, on a
column that was broken until now.
