# Changelog: 2026-08-26

## Root Potency was amplifying almost nothing it claimed to

Root Potency is sold as the line that makes every other path stronger on Mature
ground. Tracing what actually read it found a narrower mechanic than advertised, and
one piece of it that was wired to nothing at all.

- `get_network_bonus_at()` folds in the local Root Potency that Annual Rings and
  Seedbed add on top of the path's own amplification. Nothing called it. Every
  consumer read the plain `get_network_bonus()` instead, so **both of those relics
  computed a bonus that was never applied to anything.**
- The rule now lives in one place. `get_network_multiplier_at(position, maturity)`
  folds the local relic bonus together with the ground-age scaling every consumer was
  writing out by hand, and all five sites call it. Annual Rings and Seedbed amplify
  something for the first time.
- The Bramble sites could not reach the relic component: it is positional, and Bramble
  pushes a single scalar into the combat director. `CombatDirector` now takes an
  optional `root_director` and `_get_root_network_multiplier` takes the position,
  falling back to the pushed scalar when the reference is absent.
- **Pruning read it nowhere.** Cutting damage now rides it, measured over the harvest
  rather than at a point: a cut is a patch, so its Root maturity is the Mature share
  of the cells taken, not the maturity left behind — the harvest has already removed
  that. Damage rather than yield, because Cuttings-from-Mature-ground is COPPICING's
  identity and stacking a second multiplier on it would blur the Evolution.
- Root Potency also drives germination speed and Bloom cadence now. A Seed buried in
  old ground breaks open sooner, and a Bloom drawn out of old ground comes back around
  sooner — the same amplification spent on the wait instead of on the mass.

The draft screen and the upgrade hover both carry a `ROOTED 1.74x -> ...` row under the
base number for every line the Network touches, resolved through the same values the
systems use. It is absent for a run without Root and for every line the Network does
not reach, so it is never permanent furniture.

## Spore is an epidemic now, not a damage-over-time line

Two new upgrade lines, and the outbreak loop that makes them scale into dense packs
instead of falling behind them.

- **Sporulation** (+25% accumulation per rank). Enemies standing on owned Green
  accumulate Spore, 5%/sec at rank zero — the mechanic runs for any active Spore run,
  the line only multiplies it. Spore never decays. At 100% the enemy is **Overgrown**
  and loses 25% of max HP per second, dying in four.
- Overgrown is uniform across every role. No boss cap, no elite resistance: the gate
  on the path is reaching 100%, not the damage after it.
- The propagation is the point. A dying Overgrown enemy emits a **Contagion Aura**
  (10% Spore/sec in 60px, so 40% across its four-second death) and bursts for **60%
  Spore in 80px** when it dies. Forty plus sixty is a hundred — an enemy that stays
  beside an Overgrown one becomes Overgrown itself, with no Seed and no Fruiting Body
  involved.
- Both epidemic terms are deliberately **exempt from Root Potency**. Multiplying a
  self-propagating loop's own feedback term makes it impossible to tune.
- The aura rides the Overgrown damage clock rather than the frame: two radius scans a
  second per infected enemy instead of sixty, which is what makes a pack full of them
  affordable at exactly the moment the arena is busiest.
- **Fruiting Bodies** (+10pp per rank, to 50%). A Seed germinating on ground the run
  already owns can raise a colony: a 30% Spore cloud in 60px at 3s, 6s and 9s, then a
  75% rupture in 80px at 12s that scatters two Seeds. The entity pool of 20 *is* the
  cap, so no counter can drift from the number of colonies that exist.
- Overgrown enemies flash a subtle green, driven by the contagion clock rather than by
  wall time — what pulses is the infection actually spreading.

### SEED BANK became a burst-clear Evolution

Germination Season stopped being a storage threshold and became a clock: every 8
seconds it spends whatever the Bank holds, and claiming the Evolution fires a free
6-Seed Season immediately so the power spike lands on the claim.

- Season Seeds are placed **on enemies standing in owned Green**, densest cluster
  first, skipping ground a burst this Season is already covering. A Season opening on
  empty Green was the failure this rework exists to fix.
- Each one **Rootbursts** as it opens: 70px, damage worth 35% of a standard enemy's
  max HP, 50% Spore, both amplified by Root Potency. Written as a share of a standard
  enemy at the current wave so "35%" keeps meaning "a third of a normal mob" as the
  waves harden, and lands as a fraction of a boss rather than as a boss-killer.
- **Fungal Collapse**: anything a Rootburst pushes to 100% Spore dies on the spot —
  and because it is Overgrown when it does, the death burst still fires and carries
  the outbreak onward. Elites take 50% max HP and turn Overgrown instead, bosses 15%.
- Seed Reserve rank 3 is now Rootburst (+40% damage, +20% radius) and rank 4 is Fungal
  Reserve (every 3 Seeds released raise a guaranteed colony that clouds at once).
  Mast Year fires a second full-strength wave 1.5s later, every Season.
- Overgrown kills no longer feed the kill-to-Seed counter. That enemy has already been
  shedding Seeds; letting the kill also count doubles the path's income exactly when
  it is strongest.
- BRIAR PODS' Mass Entanglement seeds the pinned mob with 35% Spore. Holding a pack
  still is only half of it; the other half is that standing still is now fatal.

Three tuning knobs went dead with the rework and were removed rather than left to rot:
`seed_reserve_maturity_inheritance`, `seed_reserve_mast_cadence`, `seed_reserve_mast_share`.

## Bloom leaves a tempo window behind it

Two new Bloom lines, one four-second window seen from two sides.

- **Aftergrowth** (+8 temporary control per rank). Every Bloom lays a disposable
  control buffer over the Green it touches. It is stored **apart from real control**,
  which is the whole design: consuming it is never a territory loss, never costs
  maturity, never changes ownership, and everything asking what the run actually holds
  still sees the true value underneath.
- **Suppression** (-8pp per rank). Enemies the Bloom struck do less damage to
  Territory. Hooked at the single point every territory behaviour reads its strength
  from, so grazing, eruption, hostile conversion, spread and a Saboteur's cut are all
  weakened by one rule — and it structurally cannot leak into player damage, movement,
  attack speed or durability.
- Damage resolves **suppress -> shield -> fortified Green**. The shield absorbs at face
  value before resistance; Mature Strength applies only to what gets through. A hit the
  shield eats produces no control loss, no maturity loss and no thorn disturbance.
- Aftergrowth is queued as a command rather than written directly, so it is stamped
  after the Bloom's own conversion in the same step and covers ground the Bloom has
  just taken as well as ground already held.

## Bloom's splatter bounces off walls

The old response was not a bounce at all: it killed the normal component, damped
everything by one factor, then accelerated the glob *back into* the face for most of
a second. Paint running down a wall.

- Proper impulse resolution on two axes, because they are two different physical
  things. Normal reverses and scales by restitution (0.45, about a fifth of the
  incoming energy returned); tangential is scrubbed by Coulomb friction (0.35) limited
  by the normal impulse that produced it. The old uniform damping meant a square-on hit
  and a graze lost the same share of their sideways speed.
- Verified over 20,000 randomised collisions: never gains speed, never leaves still
  moving into the wall, friction never reverses the tangent, and the normal ratio
  equals e exactly. At e=1 with no friction it reproduces `v - 2(v.n)n` to 1e-9.
- **Fixed a tunnelling bug that bouncing would have made much worse.** Globs launch at
  620px/s and a cell is 8.57px, so one frame of travel is longer than the thinnest
  basalt on the board — a fast glob passed straight through walls. Motion now
  integrates in sub-steps capped at half a cell, re-reading velocity each sub-step so
  travel after a bounce follows the new heading.

Expect wall hits to paint noticeably less territory: the old cling laid a continuous
streak down the face, which was most of what a wall contact claimed. A bounce leaves
one impact splat and carries the rest elsewhere.

## Art, and the board it sits on

- **Painted Seed and Bloom.** `_draw_catalyst_art()` positions art by its *content box*
  rather than its image, because the seed painting sits +12.5, +25.5px off centre in
  its canvas — drawn naively it would hang below and right of the point the Seed
  occupies, with every ring, arc and bar drawn at that point sitting off its face.
  Verified both land at (0.000, 0.000) from the catalyst centre.
- The Bloom art was recoloured to teal. Its dark petals were hue 288 and the Elite
  enemy is hue 285.6 — the same colour. A player catalyst and the most dangerous
  ordinary enemy would have been near-indistinguishable on a busy screen. Only the
  250-340 hue band was rotated, so the yellow core and the black outlines survive.
- Seeds carry a per-Seed facing off the golden angle, so a scattered field of them is
  not one sprite stamped over and over. It pivots on the content centre, so the art
  spins on its own middle rather than orbiting a point beside it.
- **Obstacles read as terrain instead of holes.** Walls `#3B4035`, rocks `#51483A`,
  shading mixing toward `#292A24` rather than subtracting toward black. The old rock
  sat at luminance 0.099 against a 0.098 background — indistinguishable. The order is
  now board -> obstacles -> enemies -> Green, which is the reading order the art wants.
  Walls and rocks are told apart from position, reproducing `_is_border_cell`.
- The sine line textures are gone. 215 bands crossed by a diagonal hatch at 310 is a
  stripe every four pixels — moire, not stone. Replaced with cloud mottling off the
  board's own edge noise, remapped on the texture's measured distribution so the dark
  side still reaches the shadow colour.
- **A `TerritoryObstacleLayer` repaints basalt above everything.** Every layer that
  stretches a coarse per-cell mask over the arena bleeds a little past the frontier it
  was stamped against; clipping each one individually means solving it again for every
  layer added later. Rock that occludes is one rule that cannot be forgotten.
- The blue rectangle around the arena is gone. The board's outer contour is torn by the
  same noise as every other edge, so the ground ends the way parchment ends. Threshold
  checked in simulation: 13.3px of wall left on average, never below 10.4.
- The wager highlight is torn by that same noise too, and so is the new Aftergrowth
  sheet, which additionally derives the Green's own paint coverage line-for-line from
  the territory shader and multiplies by it — so it cannot paint a pixel the Green does
  not, and never appears on hostile ground.

## Sound

`lever_pull`, `reel_click` and `jackpot` are wired to the slot machine. The jackpot
sound plays once per spin from `_on_spin_resolved` rather than from the jackpot
handlers, because a Triple Clover raises one `clover_jackpot` and five `path_jackpot`
signals and would have fired the same sound six times. Reel clicks are polyphonic —
three reels stop close together and the third would otherwise cut the second off.

All three sit on the SFX bus, which is where `sfx_max_linear_volume` caps output at
20%.

## A title screen, and settings that persist

- **Sporekeeper** has a home screen: Play, Settings, Quit, framed in Kenney fantasy
  borders. `skip_home_screen` on the Inspector jumps straight into the run, gated on
  `OS.is_debug_build()` so a release can never ship past its own title screen.
- Buttons use one border art lit three ways — normal slightly under full brightness,
  hover lit, pressed darkened with its content nudged down a pixel. The frame never
  changes shape between states; a press reads as the same button moving.
- The 9-slice margins were measured wrong at first and produced visibly smeared
  corners. The margin has to cover the corner *ornament*, not the point where the
  hollow interior begins — 24px for border 005, not 12. That correction forced every
  framed button to at least 48px tall, since a 96px texture with 24px margins squashes
  its own corners below that.
- Presentation options moved off the pause panel onto their own page behind a SETTINGS
  button, shown in place of the panel rather than over it. Pausing mid-fight was a wall
  of rows to read past to reach Resume.
- The pause panel is centred. Its offsets were asymmetric — `-265..197` hung it 68px
  left of its own centre anchor.
- **`DisplaySettings` persists to `user://settings.cfg`**, keyed by the enum's
  names so a reordered enum cannot silently reassign saved choices. Without this the
  home screen's settings button would have been a menu that changed nothing: the run
  builds its own settings node and would have started from the authored defaults again.
  Volumes persist in the same file and the run restores them at startup.
- Both volume sliders now use the slot machine's slider, without ticks.
- `dev_controls_enabled` on the run's Inspector switches the debug keys and the DEV
  CONTROLS card together. One switch for both, because a build that lists controls it
  will not answer — or answers controls it does not list — is the only confusing
  outcome. Reset and the Biomass drawer stay live either way: both are advertised on
  the interface itself, so pressing them is using the game rather than inspecting it.

## Notes

Nothing in this session was compiled or run — there is no Godot binary on the
development machine. Numeric claims above were verified by simulation against the real
assets and data; the visual results were not seen. Two parse-level bugs have already
been found and fixed this way (`TerritoryCommand.conversion`, which never existed, and
an invented `31_panel_style` resource id); more should be expected on first run,
particularly around the Spore and Bloom work, which added the most new call paths.

`docs/upgrade_paths_reference.md` is current with all of the above.
`docs/spore_new_paths_implementation_guide.md` was deleted — it described a superseded
