# 2026-09-20

The measurement pass the Campaign rework gates its remaining decisions on, continued from
yesterday. Two balance decisions confirmed, one implementation gap in Blight found and
fixed, and — the recurring theme — **six defects in the instruments**, every one of which
made a *comparison* invalid while leaving each individual number looking plausible.

## The instruments were the story

Yesterday found three: an unseeded bench, a board with six per cent Green on it, and a
damage column that was really kill-throughput. Today found three more.

**Script defaults the scene overrides.** `run.tscn` sets `base_slow_fraction = 0.1` and
`bloom_interval_seconds = 15.0`. Two of the four early-Path changes edited the script
defaults and therefore did nothing at all. Bramble's slow is now set in the scene, where it
takes effect; Bloom's change was withdrawn rather than re-applied, because re-making an
unmeasured change on a reading already known to be biased is how the Bramble chip mistake
happened the first time.

**The bench paused itself.** `_refresh_simulation_pause()` sets `effects_paused` on the
overlay and `simulation_paused` on the field and the player. It fires on any heavy hit and
is only ever cleared by `RunRoot._process`, which a bench that steps every system by hand
never calls. One heavy hit froze those three systems for the rest of the row.

Spore looked completely inert because of it — 36 Seeds sown from 144 kills, none of them
ever opening. The sweep document's own §1 had diagnosed the resulting "same 6 points of
damage for every pair" correctly and added `player._physics_process` to fix it; that call
was being swallowed by the early return, so the symptom it was written for had never gone
away. The bench now asserts all three flags every step and reports `sown`, `seeds` and
`blooms`, so a row reading as nothing can say whether the mechanic fired and did nothing or
never fired.

It changed the rank-1 table completely. Nothing is inert; the weakest pair is +12% over the
bare Cane rather than +0%; and Pruning, which had read as contributing *nothing* at rank 1,
is the strongest rank-1 Adaptation there is.

**`pacing_probe` was unseeded too.** The same roster with the same flags came back holding
73% of the board and 37% of it. Pinned now, with `--seed`, plus `--build`, `--provoke` and
`--blight` for forcing a level.

## Blight was made of density, which is the one thing it must not be

Section 20 names four levers — Director budget, composition quality, specialist access,
elite affordability — and only the first was wired. "Composition quality" existed as a
price discount on expensive recipes, but `ThreatDirector` picks a recipe *uniformly* and
then saves for its price, so a discount only meant packs bought sooner. Blight was the
budget knob twice over.

Measured, forced to Blight V from wave 1, two seeds:

| | kills | ground @10 | ground @end |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Blight 0 | ~1,750 | ~54% | ~45% |
| Blight V, before | **+66%** | ~50% | −5 to −10 points |
| Blight V, after | **+58%** | **~20%** | **~10%** |

+66% bodies for five to ten points of ground is exactly the escalation this rework exists
to stop using. `BLIGHT_QUALITY_BIAS_PER_LEVEL` now tilts the *draw*: uniform at Blight 0,
weight-by-price at V, where an Elite Spearhead at 75 goes from as likely as a Probe at 10 to
roughly nine times as likely. Fewer bodies than before, far more pressure. §34's "Blight V
is materially harder than Blight 0" is met on both seeds.

**The strength of the tilt is a guess.** 0.22 per level is the first value tried, measured
from a forced-V-at-wave-1 start that overstates what a real climb to V costs.

## Provocation cannot be characterised yet, and that is the finding

Four full runs, same roster, seeded:

| | seed A | seed B |
|---|---:|---:|
| never — ground at end | 48.4% | 47.6% |
| aggressive — ground at end | **99.8%** | **6.8%** |

Never-provoke is remarkably stable. Aggressive is not: one run ends holding almost the whole
board, the other with 6.8%, from the same build, the same nineteen calls and the same Blight
V. **The spread between seeds dwarfs the difference between policies**, so nothing about
whether Provocation is a good trade can be said from two of them.

Six aggressive runs, ground held at minute 15, sorted: **5.6, 6.4, 26.0, 29.3, 44.6, 99.6**.
Against never-provoke's 52.7 and 53.4.

Not bimodal — a wide continuous spread. The median aggressive run holds about **28%** of the
board against never-provoke's **53%**, so the typical cost of provoking through the run is
roughly half the garden. But the spread around that median runs from near-collapse to
total dominance, from the same build and the same nineteen calls.

So Provocation's real effect is to convert a predictable run into a high-variance one. That
may be exactly right for a mechanic whose fantasy is daring the infestation to escalate —
but the rework does not discuss variance anywhere, and a 5.6%-to-99.6% range is not a
difficulty dial, it is a gamble. Whether that is the intended texture is a design question
rather than a measurement one.

One measurement note: the runs that report "played 960s" are the ones that *won* at the
compressed schedule's end. A run still clearing leftovers keeps playing, and its wall clock
keeps counting — so runtime under Provocation is "16:00 if you can close it out", not
16:00 flat.

## Trials, prototyped

Phase 9, deferred until now because the rework says to prototype it only once the base
system is fun. Built as a prototype rather than a feature: one Trial at a time, no opt-in,
one kind of reward.

**Written as constraints rather than objectives**, which is the one design decision here
worth arguing about. Section 27 sketches Trials as goals laid on a wave — reclaim this much
Rot, kill that elite. The trouble is that a goal is still something a stronger build
reaches sooner, so it becomes another thing damage solves, and §2.4 exists because damage
already solves everything. Die in the Dungeon's challenges are restrictions on how you play
the fight you are already in, and a restriction is the thing output cannot buy its way past.

| Trial | Rule | Judged |
|---|---|---|
| ROOTED | Do not step off your own Green | continuous |
| UNTOUCHED | Take no damage | on event |
| PATIENCE | Kill no more than 45 enemies | tally |
| HIGH WATER | Never let Green fall below 25% of the arena | continuous |
| RECLAMATION | Finish holding 6 points more Green than you started | at the end |
| LAST GARDEN | Keep the marked ground above 50% Green | continuous |

Four constraints and two goals. The goals are §27's and are kept because a run that only
ever meets restrictions never gets asked to go and take something back. PATIENCE is the
bluntest and probably the most useful: for sixty seconds every Adaptation that slows, pins,
pushes or reclaims is the good one.

A persistent card top-right carries the name, the rule as one imperative sentence with its
number already in it, and a live progress line — `31 of 45`, `+2.4 of +6 points`, `68% of
the marked ground`. Border colour carries state. A rule the player cannot see is a rule
they are not playing around.

One Trial per act after Germination, on waves 6, 11 and 16 — waves that owe nothing else,
and none in Act I because a constraint on a build needs there to be a build. Held pays a
relic; **broken pays nothing and costs nothing**, which `trial_probe` asserts directly
because it is the property a later change is most likely to break quietly.

Left out on purpose: Into the Breach's best idea, where a mission carrying more bonus
objectives is itself made harder so the objectives double as a difficulty dial the player
reads before committing. That is the better shape and it needs the wave table to know about
it. Also left out: simultaneous Trials, declining one, and §27's other four reward kinds.

`TrialDirector` has `capture_state`/`apply_state` written but **not wired into
`RunSnapshot`**, so a Trial does not survive a save yet.

## Pruning: two correct changes that did not fix it

Following the genre reference, the orbiting ring was taken out of the stat economy — it no
longer multiplies by Cutting Damage, which buys *launched* Cuttings — and given a per-tick
target cap of 8, nearest first. Vampire Survivors' Garlic bounds an aura's per-enemy damage
(15 a second at max level, earning its place through knockback and freeze-resistance
stripping); this ring was doing ~250 and bounding neither term.

Both changes are right and neither fixed the problem. Dealt damage fell hard — Pruning+
Thistle 255k to 155k — while applied damage and kills barely moved, and Pruning+Vine still
tops the rank-3 table at 79,940 with 17 damage taken against the Cane's 363.

What that proves is the **ring was massively overkilling**: throwing 1.9x damage at bodies
already dying. The changes made it less wasteful, not less dominant. So Pruning's power is
not the disc's damage magnitude, and the next place to look is the continuous launched-
Cutting stream from ring turnover rather than the disc itself. Not chased yet.

## A seventh instrument defect, and the largest: the player never moved

`balance_bench` now has `--walk`, which drifts Grandma on a slow circuit instead of rooting
her to one spot. It is the oldest unexamined assumption in the instrument and it is not
neutral: turrets, sweeps, auras and Fruit all follow her, so standing still costs them
nothing, while Pruning's entire contract is that it eats the ground beneath her and dies if
she steps off. The bench was never charging the one Adaptation with a positional cost.
Section 12 said so and it had never been tested.

Rank 1, siege:

| | rooted | walking |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pruning+Vine | 67,760 (taken **17**) | 36,815 (taken 127) |
| Vine pairs | ~41,000 (taken ~320) | ~30,500 (taken ~250) |
| cane only | 15,248 (taken 363) | 16,052 (taken 374) |

Rooted, Pruning pairs held all seven top slots at 4.4x the Cane with a twentyfold
survivability edge. Walking, the top is 2.3x the Cane and the top ten mixes Pruning, Vine,
Spore, Root, Bramble, Bloom, Thistle and Fruit. Walking costs the Cane nothing and costs
Pruning 46%.

**Pruning was not dominant. It was the only Adaptation never being charged.** Every Pruning
number in this session and in the original sweep was taken against a player who does not
move.

## Pruning: ground freshness

The ring's bite now scales with how recently the ground feeding it was cut -- full on
untouched Green, a quarter on ground just stripped, recovering across `pruned_ground_seconds`.
Charge and freshness pull against each other, which is the positional question the path is
supposed to ask: hold for power, or move to keep the thicket fed.

Two wrong versions preceded it, both caught by measuring rather than reasoning:

- **Sampled under her feet.** The drain spares an inner disc on purpose so the footing
  survives and the hole opens *around* her, so that is the one cell never cut. It read zero
  forever. Now sampled at eight points around the harvest ring.
- **Applied to spike yield.** A rank-3 harvest fills all sixteen spikes almost instantly and
  keeps them full, and surplus Cuttings are capped and discarded -- so throttling yield by
  three quarters still produced a full ring and changed nothing a target could feel. Moved
  onto the ring's damage.

It now bites: `dealt` falls 9% rooted, and the Harvest Rate line falls 17% because stripping
ground faster puts you in spent ground sooner. That line had been strictly good and now
carries a positional cost. Walking is untouched (30,616 to 29,980), which is the intended
shape -- the rule charges standing still, not the Adaptation.

**It does not reduce rooted kills**, because the ring overkills by about 1.5x and still
kills everything it touches. If rooted Pruning is to be weaker in kill throughput, the lever
is base damage or the hit cooldown, not another multiplier. That decision should come from a
played run now that the bench's rooted condition is known to be unrepresentative.

## The harvest cuts the ground out from under her

> "It should be harvesting directly where grandma is standing so that she is always
> encouraged to move around to maintain the ring."

`upkeep_inner_radius_pixels` is now `0.0`. The harvest used to spare a small disc under the
Gardener, because the harvest is gated on standing on your own Green and cutting the cell
underfoot switches off the thing that is running it. That gate is what changed: the ring is
now given `footing_grace_seconds` (1.10) off Green before it tears loose, so standing still
eats the footing, the debt runs up, and the ring fires. Moving onto Green pays the debt back
at `footing_recovery_rate` (4.0x), so a player who relocates keeps the ring and a player who
parks does not.

The debt is a running total rather than a flag, which took three corrections:

- **It reset on any single frame of Green.** Cut ground regrows under her within a second,
  so a reset-on-touch timer never expired: measured at 82% of the bench spent with no Green
  underfoot and the ring never once tearing. It now *drains* rather than clearing.
- **It reset when the ring tore.** That handed her a fresh grace the instant she lost the
  old one, so bare ground produced an endless refill-and-tear cycle instead of a path that
  had run out of ground. Firing the ring does not put the Green back, so the debt survives
  the discharge. Capped at the grace so a long walk over bare ground is not a debt she then
  has to stand still to clear.
- **Charge was gated on a clear debt.** A third tax on the same decision, and it put the
  discharge -- the path's whole payout -- out of reach of a short hold. Charge now builds
  for as long as the ring is up.

### Yesterday's spent-ground multiplier is retired

`spent_ground_yield_floor` and `get_ground_freshness_multiplier()` are gone. That rule was a
*proxy* for a positional cost the path did not have; the footing cut models the same cost
directly, and keeping both taxed one decision twice. Measured, the proxy held the ring to
**six spikes of sixteen** while she was grazing properly, which quietly retired
`orbit_capacity` and the shed timer built around it.

It also had a defect the footing cut exposed: the multiplier was read *after* the drain, so
it was reading the harvest's own footprint and came back at the floor on every cycle,
pinning the path to a quarter yield permanently rather than only when she doubled back.

### Where it lands

Rank 3, isolated, siege, island board, 40s:

| | damage | over cane | no Green underfoot | rings torn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rooted, before today | 56,140 | 3.68x | -- | -- |
| rooted, now | 48,481 | 3.18x | 73.8% | 8 |
| relocating (r=120) | 31,666 | 2.09x | 45.8% | 5 |
| relocating (r=260) | 19,536 | 2.01x | 53.7% | 14 |

The pressure is real: standing still tears the ring about every five seconds, and moving
moderately cuts both the starvation and the tearing by a third.

**The bench cannot say whether moving *pays*, only what it costs.** `--siege` pins its
targets at the arena centre, so every step away from them costs contact for every build
alike -- the cane-only baseline falls just as hard. Movement's upside is invisible to it.
That is why rooted still tops the table and why that ordering should not be read as a
balance finding. `--walk-radius` and `--ring` were added to get even this much.

### Two bugs found on the way

- **`natural_release` leaked across the Cutting pool.** The shed path and the off-Green
  gather path launch a recycled spike without clearing it, so a spike reused from an earlier
  discharge still claimed Green Manure credit, Skimming Cut redirection and Watersprouts.
  `_launch_cutting` now sets the flag from its own `outward` argument. Latent before today;
  the old fixture read one frame after a release and never saw a recycled spike.
- **`find_open_ground_position` disagreed with the run.** It checked raw cell control while
  the director samples `get_control_at_local`, which interpolates neighbours -- so every
  fixture that stepped off the Green to drop a ring was handed a cell that still read as
  Green.

### Fixtures

The pruning smoke and the charge-tell probe pinned the player in place. Under the old rule
that was harmless; it is now the one thing the path forbids, so they graze. `graze_owned_ground`
sweeps a lap wider than `upkeep_radius_pixels` (a tighter lap re-treads its own cut and
starves exactly as standing still does) and swings its heading until the step lands on owned
ground, because `stand_on_owned_ground` returns the field corner and most of a lap around
that is off the field. Two hold fixtures moved to `find_owned_cell_with_clearance`.

## Pruning nerf, and why every single lever failed first

> "I think it is still too strong."

Correct, and the rank-3 pair table said so in a column I had not been reading. Pruning was
not merely top of the damage table -- it was taking **no damage at all**.

| build | damage | taken | control invested |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root+Vine | 73,976 | 110 | none |
| Pruning+Vine | 72,716 | **0** | none |
| Root+Pruning | 55,524 | **0** | none |
| Thistle+Vine | 56,492 | 198 | none |
| Bramble+Root | 22,836 | 0 | slow .62 / tangle .69 / pin .32 |
| cane only | 15,248 | 363 | none |

Every Pruning pair took 0-11 damage in forty seconds against sixty held enemies. The only
other build at zero was Bramble+Root, which buys it with a full control kit and sits near
the bottom for damage. Pruning had tank-tier defence for free at second place in damage.

### Four levers that did nothing

Each of these was measured on the isolated rank-3 siege bench, against a 48,791 baseline:

| change | damage | taken |
|---|---|---|
| `orbit_contact_damage` 26 -> 18, cooldown 0.35 -> 0.55 | 69,859 (pair) | 6 |
| torn ring pays 20% of banked Charge instead of 100% | unchanged | 6 |
| `cuttings_per_cell` 0.22 -> 0.10 | 44,443 | 28 |
| `orbit_maximum_targets_per_tick` 8 -> 2 | 48,936 | 6 |

The seven-times discharge bonus is the clearest case. Cutting it dropped `dealt` by 17% and
moved **applied damage and kills not at all** -- the spikes were already killing what they
hit and the whole bonus was overkill.

### Why: the two damage sources are not additive

| zeroed | damage lost | taken |
|---|---|---|
| ring alone (`orbit_contact_damage` = 1) | 5,174 | 0 |
| Cuttings alone (`cutting_damage` = 1) | 2,255 | 0 |
| **both** | **23,556** | **369** |

Whichever source is left takes over the kills. The same holds for the defence: either one
alone is enough to keep damage taken at zero, and only removing both lets anything reach
her. Any lever pulled by itself is absorbed by the other, which is why four consecutive
nerfs registered as noise -- and, in hindsight, why yesterday's spent-ground multiplier
also measured as nothing.

A fifth finding came with it: the ring was up **99.2%** of the bench. A torn ring re-formed
on the first frame of Green, so coverage was never actually interrupted. The path's output
is its coverage, not its damage numbers.

### What was changed

- `orbit_contact_damage` and `cutting_damage` both 26.0 -> **12.0**. They move together or
  they do not move; the export comments now say so.
- `footing_recovery_rate` 4.0 -> **1.50**. At 4.0 a torn ring paid its footing debt off in
  under a third of a second.
- A torn ring must pay the debt off **in full** before it re-forms (`_torn`), rather than
  resuming on the first frame of Green.
- An involuntary tear pays **20%** of the banked Charge payout (`torn_ring_charge_share`),
  not 100%. The two cases are told apart by the ground underfoot: Pruned Ground means the
  harvest ate her footing while she stood there, bare ground means she walked off her own
  garden and meant it. This is invisible on this bench because the bonus was overkill here,
  and it is kept on design grounds -- the discharge is priced as a decision, and cutting the
  ground out from under her had made it fire itself every few seconds for free. Against a
  target the volley does not already overkill, it is the change that matters most.

Isolated at rank 3, island, siege:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pruning alone, before | 48,791 | 3.20x | 0 | 99.2% | 8 |
| Pruning alone, after | 34,016 | 2.23x | 176 | 86.8% | 31 |

### The caveat this tuning was chosen around

The bench holds sixty Hunters, so nearly all Pruning damage above the kill threshold is
overkill, and the response to a damage cut is a **threshold rather than a slope**: 26 -> 13
costs 10% of output, while 13 -> 3 costs another 34%. Tuning to the knee would have meant
an 80% damage cut, which reads as -80% against anything the volley does not already overkill
-- a boss, an Elite, a Hive Queen. 12.0 is a 54% cut chosen to move the `taken` column into
the band the other paths occupy without pricing the path against a single tough target.

**So this needs checking against a boss wave, not another siege bench.** The siege bench
cannot tell a strong Adaptation from an absurd one; it can only tell which one is still
standing at the end.

## The ring's damage was binary, which is why nothing worked

> "I essentially want the gameplay for pruning to be so that grandma has to constantly
> be moving around otherwise the cuttings wont be able to build up or maintain their
> damage. Also I dont want it to be so much damage that it is the obvious choice every
> run."

`_apply_orbit_damage` applied `orbit_contact_damage` flat the moment a single spike
existed. **One spike bit exactly as hard as sixteen.** The ring's damage was a boolean --
up, or down -- and building it up was cosmetic.

That is the root cause of every dead lever in this document. Halving `cuttings_per_cell`
changed nothing because the ring was at full damage on its first spike. `orbit_capacity`
and `full_ring_replacement_seconds` were decorative. Dropping the target cap from eight to
two did nothing. The only number that moved output was how much of the run the ring existed
*at all*, which was 99.2% of it -- so the path's output was its uptime, and its uptime was
very nearly total.

It also explains the non-additivity in section 9.13 from the other side: with the ring
pinned at full damage regardless of size, the Cuttings only ever mattered as a second
always-on source, never as the thing the ring was made of.

### Two changes

- **Ring damage is linear in the spike count** (`orbit_lone_spike_damage_share`, 0.06).
  A full ring deals what it always did; a ring of one deals a sixteenth.
- **Unfed spikes wilt** (`orbit_wilt_interval_seconds`, 0.40). A harvest cycle that finds
  no ground drops the ring's oldest spike. Every cell the drain returns is fresh by
  construction -- Pruned Ground sits at zero control and the drain only takes ground she
  still owns -- so finding any ground at all is the signal that she has moved onto
  something worth cutting, and it resets the clock.

  Spikes **wilt rather than launch**. A starving ring is not a discharge, and
  `_launch_cutting` would have handed her the damage anyway, which would pay her a volley
  for standing still -- the exact thing this is here to discourage.

Together they make the ring the object it looks like: a thicket she builds by cutting fresh
ground and loses by standing on ground she has already cut.

### What it does

Isolated, rank 3, island, siege:

| | damage | taken | mean spikes of 16 | tears | ring up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rooted | 22,753 | 363 | **0.69** | 44 | 81.2% |
| walking (r=120) | 23,902 | 149 | **8.54** | 9 | 92.1% |

Standing still collapses the ring to essentially nothing and she takes 363 -- which is
precisely what the cane-only baseline takes, so a rooted Pruning player is carrying no
defence at all. Moving builds the ring to over half and halves the damage taken.

### The half of the request this does not deliver

Movement now drives the ring and drives survival. It does **not** drive damage.

| isolated, rank 3 | damage | taken |
|---|---|---|
| rooted | 21,839 | 363 |
| walking | 22,869 | 132 |

Five percent on damage against 2.7x on damage taken, and the same shape at a 22-enemy
population (1.43x vs 1.42x over cane, damage taken 292 against 66). The ring is not where
Pruning's damage comes from; the Cuttings are, and the Cutting stream is set by the harvest
rate, which a rooted player still gets. Standing in one place is a sustainable farm: strip
the disc, tear, stop holding, Pruned Ground regrows because suppression only applies while a
ring is up, re-form, strip it again. She is off Green 53% of the bench and harvesting the
other 47%.

An attempt to close it by **withering** a torn ring rather than launching it -- so that
running out of ground paid nothing -- moved rooted and walking output by the same 4% and did
not widen the gap at all. It was reverted. It also broke the discriminator between a
deliberate release and a starved one: after a harvest the ground all around her is Pruned,
so stepping aside on purpose is indistinguishable from starving in place.

If damage is to follow movement, the lever is the **regrowth loop**, not the ring: Pruned
Ground coming back fast enough to re-feed a stationary player is what makes standing still
pay. That is a change to `pruned_regrowth_per_second` and to when suppression applies, and
it touches the "a burn is a loan" rule that Coppicing and Second Harvest are built on, so it
should be a decision rather than a tuning pass.

**Root+Vine is now the obvious choice**: first in damage by 29% over second place, and the

## Root+Vine: the stacked axis, unstacked

> "Its okay for root + vine to be incredibly strong, but not THAT strong."

Root alone measures at 1.00x the cane baseline -- it deals no damage of its own at all --
and Vine alone at 1.42x. Together they were 75,460, which is 4.95x. That is not two things
adding up.

Section 35 of the rework says to ask what *multiplicative interaction* is producing the
result before touching damage values, and there was one. `_root_amplifies_line` amplifies
**every owned upgrade line** by the full Root Network multiplier, and a line's bonus is
already `rank x per-rank`. So a rank-3 Vine damage line was being multiplied by a rank-3
Root Potency -- two investments the run made, compounding on the same axis. At rank 3 on
the island board that multiplier is `1 + 1.05 x 0.90 = 1.945x`.

Zeroing the Network confirmed the size of it: Root+Vine fell from 71,596 taking 121 to
50,456 taking 253. A third of the damage and most of the defence was that one
multiplication.

### Why not simply cut Root Potency

The obvious lever is `network_bonus_per_level` (0.25 in `run.tscn`). Cutting it to 0.15
produces almost exactly the same damage result -- Root+Vine 63,168 -- and costs nothing
measurable in Root's other pairs:

| pair | 0.25 | 0.15 |
|---|---|---|
| Root+Fruit | 43,652 | 42,448 |
| Root+Thistle | 36,180 | 36,284 |
| Spore+Root | 29,425 | 29,292 |
| Bramble+Root | 22,836 | 22,973 |

It looks free. It is not, because Bramble+Root's value is not in its damage column:

| Bramble+Root control | slow | tangle | pinned |
|---|---|---|---|
| `network_bonus_per_level` 0.25 | 0.621 | 0.691 | 0.323 |
| `network_bonus_per_level` 0.15 | 0.525 | 0.652 | 0.238 |

That is a 15% and 26% cut to the one thing the second-weakest build in the game is for, paid
to fix an interaction it has no part in.

### What was changed instead

`root_line_network_share`, 0.50: the share of the Network that reaches an upgrade line's
per-rank bonus. The Cane has always taken a share rather than the whole multiplier
(`root_cane_network_share`); the lines took all of it. Now they take half.

This is targeted because of *where* each consumer resolves the Network. Line bonuses go
through `AdaptationDirector._root_bonus_scale`, which is where Vine's damage lives. Bramble's
entanglement, slow and tick damage, and Spore's sporulation, resolve it in `CombatDirector`
instead, and are untouched.

| build | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Root+Vine | 71,596, taken 121 | **62,776, taken 176** |
| Root+Fruit | 43,652, taken 204 | 41,384, taken 242 |
| **Bramble+Root** | 22,836, control .621/.691/.323 | **22,836, control .621/.691/.323** |

The same reduction to Root+Vine as the blunt lever, and Bramble's control is bit-identical.

### A measurement note

`--seed` does not vary this bench for a `--build` run: three seeds returned identical
numbers. So the non-monotonic result found while sweeping (`per_level` 0.15 giving 63,168
and 0.10 giving 69,356) is deterministic chaos in kill order, not sampling noise, and small
differences between candidates here cannot be trusted. Single-build runs also read about 5%
lower than the same build inside a full pair-table run, so the two are not comparable and
the tables above only compare like with like.

## The damage cut, re-examined and kept

The 26 -> 12 cut on `orbit_contact_damage` and `cutting_damage` was chosen from the damage
threshold curve *before* the spike-count scaling existed. Once damage also scales with ring
fullness the two stack, so the path took two independent cuts for one problem -- and the
siege bench cannot see the cost, because sixty Hunters absorb it as overkill. Against a boss
it is roughly a 75% reduction in single-target output, on a path whose stated identity is a
glass cannon.

So 18.0 was measured against it:

| | 12.0 | 18.0 |
|---|---|---|
| rooted | 22,753, taken 363, 0.69 spikes | 25,745, taken 363, 0.77 spikes |
| walking | 23,902, taken 149, 8.54 spikes | 27,548, taken **110**, 9.12 spikes |
| Pruning+Vine | 57,237, taken 105 | 63,084, taken **61** |

**Kept at 12.0.** The movement gradient on defence survives either way -- rooted takes the
full 363 at both values -- but 18.0 puts Pruning+Vine at 63,084 against Root+Vine's 63,588,
which is the top-two tie that started this, and hands it the lowest damage taken in the
entire table at 61. Raising the flat value buys back single-target damage *and* the crowd
clearing *and* the free defence, and only one of those was wanted.

The boss concern is real and remains open, but a flat damage raise is measurably the wrong
instrument for it. If Pruning proves too weak against a single tough target, the fix belongs
where that problem lives -- the per-target hit cooldown, or the eight-target cap that wastes
most of the ring's capacity on a lone boss -- not on a value that also governs how it
handles a crowd.
