# Changelog: 2026-08-22

## Lock icon

- Rendered `assets/icons/lock.svg` at 4x on import, so the locked-slot icon is a
  96px source drawn into the slot instead of a 24px one.
- Fixed the icon sitting low and right of centre. It was positioned and sized by
  hand, which a later layout pass re-resolved against the texture's own minimum

## Plant director split into per-path components

- Replaced `PlantDirector` with `SporeDirector` and `BloomDirector`, matching how
  `KudzuDirector` already owned its path. Each path's tuning, its evolutions, and
  the signals it listens to now live together.
- Spore owns kills into Seeds, germination growth, Volatile Germination, Briar
  Pods and Tangleburst, and the Seed Bank with its Germination Seasons. Bloom owns
  the Bloom cadence, detonation, Super Bloom's chain, Flowering Season with its
  Echo Blooms, and Thornburst's harvest into Briar Traps.
- Super Bloom spans both paths, so chain depth and the chained telegraph stay on
  the Bloom director and a germination asks it to arm the next generation through
  `plant_chained_bloom`. The Seed side no longer keeps a second copy of the
  Bloom's numbers.
- Moved the boss eruption pulse to the run controller, where it belongs: it is
  arena feedback rather than anything a Plant path does.
- Split the authored Inspector values to the component that now reads each one,
  and updated the smoke suite, both probes, and the architecture docs.

## Slot row is editable in the editor

- Made `UpgradeSlots` a tool script, so the row builds itself in the editor
  instead of only at run time. Selecting the node now shows real slots.
- Every visual property rebuilds the row the moment it changes, and the set is
  wider than before: slot count, size, spacing, corner rounding, border width,
  both font sizes, the three fill colours, the three border colours, the lock
  texture, its share of the slot, and its tint.
- The editor fills the row with sample contents, so a filled, an empty, and a
  locked slot are all on screen while they are being styled. Play overwrites every
  slot on the first HUD refresh, so the preview cannot leak into a run.
- The generated slots are added as internal children, so they render and preview
  but are never written into the scene: the row is always rebuilt from the
  properties above, which stay the single place its look is defined.

## Every path is a component, and they live together

- Added `BrambleDirector` and `RootDirector`, so all five Plant paths now have
  their own component. Neither owns entities or a cadence - Bramble is resolved
  per enemy inside the combat loop and Root is a set of field rules - so each owns
  its path's tuning and derived values and applies them to the systems that
  execute them.
- Moved every Bramble and Root number out of `Progression` and onto those
  components, along with the Siege Thorns relic values. `Progression` now keeps
  what belongs to the run rather than to a path: levels, drafts, rerolls, path
  commitment, relics, evolution status, and the Biomass economy.
- Root's Biomass yield reaches the economy as an argument to `tick_biomass`
  instead of `Progression` reaching into a path for it.
- Where one path amplifies another, the component says so directly: Spore and
  Bloom hold a reference to `RootDirector` for the Root Network, and Bloom holds
  one to `BrambleDirector` for Entanglement potency.
- Took the Bramble and Root wiring out of the run controller. It now asks each
  path component to apply itself rather than knowing how any of them work.
- Grouped all five components under one `Paths` parent in the scene, so the Plant
  side reads as one place in the tree. `Paths` is a `Node2D` so the Kudzu
  director's arena-space drawing is unchanged.
- Preserved every authored Inspector value through the move, including the Bramble
  overrides that were sitting on `Progression`.

## Kudzu could never hold a foothold

Playtest report: several Kudzu upgrades and nothing visibly happening. Measured a
four-minute run and the path was genuinely broken, in two ways.

- Crowns were being killed by the ground they were planted in. A rooting Crown
  took double damage from hostile control, and remote ground is hostile ground
  almost by definition, so a Crown died in about two seconds against a six-second
  rooting period. Nothing could ever establish.
- Once that was fixed, Crowns established and were then worn down by the same
  hostile ground within seconds: sixteen established and sixteen destroyed across
  four minutes, which is exactly the "nothing is happening" the report described.
- Terrain no longer damages Crowns at all. A Crown is taken by the enemies
  standing on it, in both states, which is what "vulnerable while rooting" was
  always meant to mean: the player defends a foothold rather than being denied
  one by the map.
- An established Crown now holds its own ground, feeding a small amount of growth
  into its foothold on a cadence, regenerates while it stands on its own Green,
  and gets a short settling grace after establishing.
- Measured again over the same four minutes: three Crowns established, none lost,
  and Green rising rather than falling.
- Added visible cluster progress. A forming foothold draws one pip per banked kill
  where it is forming, so fighting out in the open reads as doing something
  instead of nothing.
- The smoke check now follows the besieged Crown itself rather than a count, and
  asserts both halves: it holds against hostile ground, and falls to enemies.

## Kudzu colonies actually grow

Follow-up playtest: the cluster pips appear, but a Crown still did nothing
visible. Measured again, this time driving the player at enemies the way a person
plays rather than leaving it on spawn.

- Crowns form and survive now, but each colony was a 16 to 24 pixel dot that never
  grew. Runners were firing on schedule and their growth was being reconverted by
  the hostile ground around it before it could spread, so a Crown produced a
  speck and nothing else.
- Strengthened the runners, which are the entire point of the path: they reach
  farther, arrive more often, stamp more mass over a wider radius, and keep more
  of that mass when pushing into hostile ground.
- Measured over four minutes of played-out run: colony reach went from 24 pixels
  to 88, and the run's Green went from falling to 2.1% to holding at 6.2%.
- Added block-reason counters to the director, so a full cluster that is going
  nowhere can say whether capacity, remoteness, or Crown spacing stopped it
  rather than failing silently.
- Covered by test: a Crown's colony must be visibly larger after its runners have
  had time to work, not merely present.

## Kudzu foothold placement is now the player's decision

Playtest note: the player cannot choose where a Thornshot lands, so requiring
several kills inside one small area made Crown placement essentially random.

- Remote kills now bank a foothold rather than siting one. Kills decide when a
  Crown is available; the player decides where it goes by being there.
- A banked foothold takes hold where the player is standing, provided that ground
  is remote. A player who has already fallen back gets it at the last remote kill
  instead, so the mechanic still resolves rather than stalling.
- The bank lapses if the player fights elsewhere for long enough, so a foothold is
  still something committed to rather than accumulated forever.
- The pips are drawn on the ground the foothold would actually claim, and turn
  amber when the bank is full but the player is standing somewhere it cannot take,
  which says "walk back out" rather than nothing.
- Replaced the whole kill-clustering structure with the bank, so there is one
  place a foothold's progress lives instead of six drifting candidate sites.

## Kudzu switched itself off on a healthy run

Playtest note: no Crowns forming at all. Reproduced it by growing the ecosystem
first, which is the state the previous probes never reached.

- With 72.5% Green held, a four-minute run produced zero remote kills and zero
  Crowns. Remoteness was measured as 150 pixels from any owned Green, so the
  better a run was doing, the less ground qualified, until the path stopped
  working entirely. Kudzu was strongest when the run was weakest and dead when it
  was strong, which is exactly backwards.
- Remoteness is now about being outside the territory rather than far from it: the
  margin drops from 150 pixels to 56. Measured again at the same 72.5% Green, the
  run banks footholds and establishes a Crown.
- The banked foothold now lapses one kill at a time instead of resetting to
  nothing, so scattered remote kills still accumulate between fights.
- Covered by test: a grown ecosystem must still leave ground Kudzu can colonise.
  The previous Kudzu tests all ran against a small starting territory, which is
  why none of them caught this.

## Kudzu reworked: the player is the vector

The Crown model asked the player to hold a second place. This arena gives them one
body against a horde that follows them, so a remote asset was something the game
could never let them defend, and every fix so far had been a patch on that
premise. Playtesting agreed: Crowns died faster than they could be replaced, and
replacing them was hardest exactly when enemies were thickest.

- Replaced Crowns entirely. Walking on ground the ecosystem does not own now lays
  a runner behind the player, thin and short-lived on its own. Closing that runner
  into a loop, by meeting it again or by returning to the border it started from,
  claims everything it encircles at once.
- Nothing in the path can be destroyed, because nothing in it is held. Remoteness
  became a route the player draws instead of a place they garrison, and the payoff
  lands the moment the loop closes rather than minutes later.
- Enclosed ground is claimed weakly and capped per loop, so the spectacle stays
  without a single lucky lap of the arena ending a run.
- Added `stamp_enclosed_region` to the field, which owns what "inside" means: the
  caller hands over the outline it drew and gets back how much ground it was worth.
- The upgrade lines became Trail Persistence, Runner Vigour, and Reach, alongside
  the unchanged Invasion Strength. Each one moves a number the runner is actually
  made of.
- Repointed all four pairings at the runner: Sporulation drops Seeds along the
  line, Wildflower Network opens Blooms around a closed loop, Briar Bastions thorns
  the live runner so the drawn line tangles what crosses it, and Rhizome Network
  brings enclosed ground up already settled.
- The drawn trail fades with its own life, marks the head the player is drawing
  from, and rings the head when the loop can be closed, which is the only prompt
  the mechanic needs.
- Rebuilt the Kudzu tests around walking the player rather than emitting kills:
  laying, closing, the per-loop cap, withering, the upgrade lines, all four
  pairings, and that a grown ecosystem still leaves open ground worth crossing.

## Pruning

Implements sections 14 to 20 of `docs/new_changes.md`, including all five pairings.
Pruning is the sixth Plant path and the only one that spends the resource the run
is trying to protect.

- Added the path, its four authored lines (Pruning Frequency, Cutting Yield,
  Cutting Damage, Regrowth), a development switch, and `PruningDirector`
  under `Paths`.
- A Pruning Cycle cuts a fixed share of the whole claimable arena rather than a
  share of current Green, so the cost stays predictable however well a run is
  doing, and takes it from the youngest ground inward: Fresh, then Established,
  and Mature only when nothing else remains.
- No upgrade raises how much a cycle sacrifices. Every line buys efficiency, which
  is what stops a sacrifice that scales with its own reward from running away, and
  a test asserts the cut stays the same size with all four lines maxed.
- Cycles pause below a territory floor, so the path can never spend the last of
  the ground the run is standing on.
- Cuttings spiral in toward the player before launching at what is nearest, pierce
  a couple of enemies with reduced damage on each pass, and are drawn as plant
  matter gathering and then flying.
- Added Pruned Ground to the field: cut cells remember they were owned and Green
  retakes them far faster than ordinary neutral, so a cycle is a loan rather than
  a loss. The field owns that rule; Pruning decides how strong it is.
- Added the field commands the path needed: a whole-ecosystem cut that reports what
  it took, a radial cut for a Bloom collapsing Green into itself, and a claimable
  cell count for the fixed cost to measure against.
- Coppice seeds the cut ground as it is taken and germinates it together a moment
  later. Rebound collapses Green into a detonating Bloom and pays it back as Force
  only, capped. Deadfall turns torn-out thorns into extra Cuttings. Coppicing pays
  out on Mature ground and drives the regrowth much harder. Vegetative Propagation
  sends a capped few Cuttings out as living propagules that try to root a Crown.
- Added `damage_first_enemy_in_radius` to combat, so a piercing projectile owned
  outside combat can strike one enemy at a time and decline to strike it twice.

## Pruning did nothing in a real run

Playtest report: nothing about Pruning worked. Measured a five-minute run and it
was gated off entirely, for the same reason Kudzu had been.

- The safety floor was a territory percentage, set at 27% from the design note.
  Runs in this build hold two to four percent, so `is_above_safety_floor` was false
  for the entire run and no cycle ever fired. The floor is now counted in cells:
  what it is actually for is refusing to spend the last of the ecosystem, and that
  is a small absolute amount rather than a share of the arena.
- The fixed arena share was also sized for a much larger ecosystem. At four percent
  Green it would have taken nearly half the run's territory in one cycle, so a
  cycle now takes whichever is smaller: the fixed arena slice, or a bounded share
  of the Green actually held.
- With cycles firing, Green then bled away: 4.0% down to 0.0% by four minutes,
  against 3.0% held by the same run with Pruning switched off. Pruned Ground only
  regrew where a Green neighbour could reach it, so cut ground with nothing beside
  it never came back and the path drank its own run dry.
- Pruned Ground now grows itself back independently of its neighbours, which is
  what makes a cycle a loan instead of a bleed. Hostile control still stops the
  recovery, so the enemy can take the ground the run gave up.
- Measured again over the same run: 36 cycles, 162 Cuttings, and Green holding at
  3.1% against the 3.0% control. The sacrifice is real moment to moment and pays
  for itself over the cycle, which is the loop the path is supposed to have.
- Covered by three new checks: a run on its starting foothold can still prune, the
  floor still refuses when the ecosystem is nearly gone, and cut ground measurably
  grows back on its own.
- Pinned the two newest path switches in the smoke suite alongside the other four,
  since adding a path changed how many options a milestone offers.

## Pruning becomes the ring: no cycle timer, a real burn, and lines to match

- The orbit did not occur in a played-out run: the player stood on their own Green
  1.6% of the time, and a Cutting only joined the ring if the player was on Green
  at the moment its gather finished. The same class of failure as the earlier Kudzu
  remoteness gate — a condition calibrated to a game state this build rarely
  reaches. Pruning no longer waits for that moment; standing on Green *is* the path.
- Removed the cycle interval entirely, on request. There is no cooldown, no first
  cycle delay, and no arena-share cut. The harvest runs continuously for exactly as
  long as the player stands on their own ground, and the ground it takes comes
  straight back up as spikes for the ring.
- The drain was invisible because Pruned Ground reclaimed almost instantly: at 1.3
  control per second against an ownership threshold of 0.18, a drained cell was
  Green again inside a fifth of a second. Reclamation is now suppressed for as long
  as the ring is turning, and resumes the moment it fires. The Green under the
  player visibly recedes, and firing the ring is what lets the garden come back.
- The ecosystem pays for spikes, not for time. While the ring has room the harvest
  runs at 9 cells per second, rising to 18 at full Charge; once it is full it drops
  to a maintenance drain that tapers to nothing at zero Charge. A first pass without
  that distinction stripped the run from 4.9% to 0.4% inside a minute and it never
  recovered — a death spiral, not a glass cannon.
- Ring damage tuned up for the glass-cannon feel the path is aiming at: 26 per bite
  against 7, a 0.35s per-enemy cooldown against 0.55, and up to +80% at full Charge.
  The launch still pays out far more for a hold (+250%) than the orbit does.
- The four upgrade lines were still describing the old loop, so they were rebuilt
  around the new one. Pruning Frequency and Regrowth are gone; Harvest Rate and
  Charge take their places, alongside Cutting Yield and Cutting Edge. Three buy
  efficiency out of the same ground; Harvest Rate is the only line that spends more,
  and it buys spikes per second and burn per second in the same rank.
- Coppice now buries a Seed every few cells harvested and erupts in batches, since
  there is no longer a cycle to hang a single eruption off. Vegetative Propagation
  sends a share of the harvest out as propagules rather than a per-cycle cap.
- Measured over a five-minute run holding the ring continuously: a full 16-spike
  ring at maximum Charge, 790 cells burned, and Green at 1.9% against 2.7% for the
  same run without Pruning. The cost is visible and survivable rather than fatal.
- Test fixtures that stood still had to learn to graze. Standing in one place burns
  your own footing out and fires the ring, which is the loop working correctly, so
  a fixture that holds still measures an empty ring.
- All five suites pass.

## The thicket, and a discharge worth firing

- The ring only damaged a band either side of the rim, so an enemy crossed it, took
  a few bites, and was then safe standing on the player: contact range sits well
  inside the hoop. It behaved like a tripwire rather than like the Garlic-style aura
  the path is aiming at. It is now a disc — everything the hoop encloses is caught.
- Drawn to match, and to stay a plant rather than a magic circle: the spikes are tied
  together by severed runners with visible slack, and cut leaf litter turns over the
  ground inside. Both brighten and speed up with Charge.
- The discharge was badly underpriced. At full Charge it dealt about 2,400 damage
  while a held ring did roughly 1,070 per second against a crowd of eight, so firing
  bought about two seconds of grinding and cost a quarter of the ecosystem plus eight
  seconds to rebuild. That made camping the correct play and inverted the loop.
- Launch payout raised from +250% to +600% at full Charge, and discharged spikes now
  gain up to four extra pierce and keep 88% of their damage through each enemy rather
  than 65%. A full-Charge burst is now the largest number the path produces; an
  unearned one at zero Charge stays small, which is what keeps Charge meaningful.
- The burst also fires radially outward instead of homing. Sixteen spikes seeking the
  nearest enemy overkilled one target while the crowd walked past, and it did not
  read as a ring coming apart. Ordinary Cuttings still home; only a discharge bursts.
- Four new checks: the thicket catches an enemy at contact range, a discharge bursts
  outward rather than converging, discharged spikes drive further through a crowd,
  and the discharge pays out far more for Charge than holding does.
- All five suites pass.

## The hold gets an end: an escalating drain

- A single patch of Green fed a ring indefinitely. The maintenance drain was tied to
  Charge, and Charge caps after eight seconds, so past that point holding cost a flat
  1.5 cells per second while ordinary spread refilled the ground faster than that.
  Standing still was sustainable forever, which is the opposite of the intent.
- The drain now climbs by 0.8 cells per second for every second the ring has gone
  without firing, on top of the Charge-scaled rate, and that escalation does not cap.
  Maintenance also starts higher: 3 cells per second at zero Charge rising to 9 at
  full, against a flat 1.5 before. The escalation resets when the ring fires.
- Measured over five minutes with the player pinned to their own Green and never
  moving: the longest hold is 26.9s and the drain peaks at 30.6 cells per second,
  against a hold that previously never ended at all.
- The honest cost of holding a ring continuously is that Green sits at 0.7% against
  2.7% for the same run without Pruning, which is the safety floor. That is the
  intended glass-cannon shape rather than a bug, but it does mean a run that never
  stops holding lives permanently at its floor. A real run fights off its own ground
  a good deal of the time; if this proves too punishing in play,
  `upkeep_escalation_per_second` is the one knob.
- Test fixtures had to start stepping the territory field alongside the director.
  With the faster drain a fixture that only advanced Pruning burned a hole under its
  own feet that never grew back, so the ring starved on ground of its own making.
- New check: a ring held on one patch of Green burns its own footing out and fires,
  with the drain measurably higher late in the hold than at the moment it filled.
- All five suites pass.

## Authored Pruning tuning, and a check that was asserting a number

- Retuned in the inspector after play: the fill rate goes from 9 to 15 cells per
  second, and the escalation from 0.8 to 5.0 cells per second per second held. Both
  are scene overrides on PruningDirector; the script defaults are unchanged.
- The effect is a much shorter, punchier hold. The escalation now reaches the 60
  cells per second ceiling about nine seconds in, so a ring fills fast and gives out
  fast instead of settling into a long grind.
- Measured over five minutes against the authored values. Standing on one patch and
  never moving: longest hold 16.3s, peak drain at the 60/s ceiling, full Charge still
  reachable. Grazing across owned ground: longest hold 13.0s. Both leave Green at
  0.5-0.7% against 2.7% for the same run with Pruning switched off, which is the
  safety floor: a run that holds a ring continuously lives at its floor.
- Worth watching in play rather than acting on yet: mean hold length across a whole
  run is short (about a second grazing) because once the run is pinned at the floor
  the ring forms and gives out repeatedly. Long holds happen early, while there is
  still an ecosystem to spend.
- One smoke check had to be repointed. It compared the two Charge-scaled drain rates
  to prove upkeep outgrows the ring's damage, and the new tuning brings those two
  numbers close together, so it failed on values that are working as intended. That
  property now lives in the escalation, so the check asserts the rule where it
  actually lives: the escalation is positive, the drain ceiling outgrows the orbit's
  Charge bonus, and the launch still pays out more than holding. The authored numbers
  were left exactly as tuned.
- All five suites pass.


# Summary of the day

Interface work, then Kudzu, then a full redesign of Pruning.

**Interface and structure.** The upgrade tray got the lock icon, bare rank numbers
instead of "3/5", and a slot row that is editable in the editor and whose authored
caption now survives into the running game. Relic slots replaced the field signal
readout. A reroll that sometimes produced the same three options now steps until the
offer genuinely differs. The single plant director was split into one component per
path, all six gathered under a shared `Paths` node, with each path owning its own
tuning and derived values and `Progression` keeping run-level state only.

**Kudzu, five attempts and no answer.** Crowns could not hold a foothold, then could
not grow, then could not be placed where the player wanted, then switched themselves
off entirely on a healthy run because a remoteness gate assumed far more territory
than this build produces. A rework making the player the vector was built and
rejected, and the Crown model was restored. The path functions and its four
Evolutions work, but it is parked: it needs a rework of its fantasy rather than more
tuning, and that has not been attempted.

**Pruning, redesigned from a cycle into a ring.** It began as a timed cycle that cut
territory and threw it outward. That never fired in a real run, because its safety
floor and its orbit condition were both calibrated to territory levels runs never
reach — the same failure as Kudzu's remoteness gate, and the recurring lesson of the
day: thresholds written as percentages of the arena assume an ecosystem this build
does not grow.

What replaced it has no timer at all. Standing on your own Green harvests it
continuously and the harvest comes back as spikes that orbit the player, grinding
everything the ring encloses rather than only what crosses its rim. Holding banks
Charge; the drain rises with Charge and then rises again for every second the ring
goes unfired, so a hold always ends. Leaving the Green bursts the hoop outward with
the Charge behind it. Along the way the discharge was repriced from roughly two
seconds of grinding into the largest number the path produces, the four upgrade
lines were rebuilt around the new loop as Harvest Rate, Cutting Yield, Cutting Edge
and Charge, and reclamation was made to pause while a ring turns so the burn is
visible instead of healing within a fifth of a second. Final tuning was authored in
the inspector: a faster fill and a much steeper escalation, giving short punchy holds
of around thirteen to sixteen seconds.

**Method.** Every claim about Pruning in this file was measured by playing a run out
headless and comparing against the same run with the path switched off, because unit
tests call directors directly on grown fixtures and cannot see a gate that never
opens in practice. Two fixture bugs came from the same blind spot: test loops that
stood still burned their own footing out, and loops that advanced the director
without stepping the field starved the ring on a hole that could never regrow.

## Carnivory replaces Kudzu

- Removed `KudzuDirector` and the Kudzu path, and added `CarnivoryDirector` in
  its place. Carnivory adds new function to territory the run already owns
  instead of being a second way to expand it.
- Added a Prey Pressure channel to `TerritoryField`. Enemies standing on owned
  Green deposit enemy-seconds into the ground beneath them, cell by cell; the
  channel decays where nothing is feeding and is cleared outright on ground the
  run loses. It is local by construction, so a dense late invasion banks pressure
  faster simply because more bodies are standing still, and no arena-wide
  percentage is consulted anywhere.
- Where enough pressure collects, a Maw Bud emerges, visibly grows, and becomes a
  Maw that detects prey, opens with a Jawspan-wide telegraph, snaps, and digests.
  Budding a Maw consumes the pressure that produced it.
- A Bite spends one total damage budget across everything it caught rather than
  dealing full damage to each, softened slightly so a full mouth still beats a
  perfect division. More prey buys coverage, never unlimited damage.
- Maws are planted, not placed: losing the Green under one uproots it. Capacity
  stays hard — two by default, up to six with Budding — and at capacity the
  ecosystem recycles its oldest predator rather than refusing to grow.
- Four normal lines: Bite Force, Jawspan, Digestion, and Budding. Budding buys
  Prey Pressure conversion first and Maw Capacity only from rank three, so the
  early ranks make Maws arrive sooner and the late ones let more stand.
- Replaced the five Kudzu pairings with the five Carnivory ones: Snap Pods,
  Feeding Frenzy, Strangletrap, Ancient Hunger, and Uprooting. Uprooting drives
  the current active Pruning ring, not the obsolete timed cycle — it tears Maws
  out into the ring as snapping heads that bite along the route and may replant
  where the ring ends. Pruning's `propagule` Cutting flag is gone with it.

## Biomass becomes Ecosystem Metabolism

- Deleted the five repeatable percentage purchases and the whole
  `BiomassUpgradeId` shop. Progression still owns the Biomass currency; what
  allocating it does now belongs to the new `Metabolism` component under
  `GameplayComponents`.
- Three universal systems: Photosynthesis is Mass, Meristem is Pressure, Cambium
  is Cohesion. The player distributes eight discrete allocation points across
  them — left click a system to allocate, right click to release — and allocated
  points draw Biomass out of the Reserve at a fixed rate per point.
- Level bars fill themselves on an exponential curve, so the decision is between
  cheaply developing a neglected system and paying heavily to specialise an
  advanced one. Reallocating changes the future investment rate only; levels
  already earned are never refunded.
- The Reserve is a genuine buffer. An allocation the economy cannot pay for is
  not blocked: it burns the Reserve and then throttles back to whatever income
  covers, which is what makes running an unsustainable configuration a real and
  temporary choice.
- Every fifth level offers a Breakthrough through the existing paused choice
  panel, and each one changes the simulation rather than a number. Rapid Cycling
  and Storage Organs shape how Photosynthesis releases Mass; Apical Dominance and
  Lateral Growth decide whether Pressure concentrates or spreads; Flexible Tissue
  and Woody Tissue move pressure advection and resistance in opposite directions,
  so Green either bends and rebounds or holds and then fails suddenly.
- The side panel reads Biomass as a running economy — Reserve, Production,
  Consumption, Net — and turns amber when the run is spending down its reserves.
- Added OVERGROW. It spends half the Reserve and converts it into an injection of
  Mass, Pressure, and Cohesion along the living frontier, split in the proportions
  the run's own allocation is. It grants no predetermined slice of territory: the
  new `TerritoryField.overgrow()` derives each frontier cell's outward normal from
  the Plant behind it, so open ground spreads, a gap funnels the same budget into
  a jet, and a torn ecosystem spends the repair share sealing itself.
- Added production milestones, which announce themselves once, play a cue, and
  grant a small free ecosystem pulse.

## Bloom becomes a splatter

- One Bloom no longer stamps one circle. It resolves into a central surge plus a
  small number of thrown splats — globs, satellites, and directional streaks —
  each its own field command with its own Mass, Force, and radius, via a new
  `queue_splat` command kind.
- Kept Bloom Mass and Bloom Force physically distinct. Mass rides Photosynthesis
  and decides how much material is thrown, how thick the resulting territory is,
  and whether an isolated patch survives; Force rides Meristem and the Bloom Force
  line and decides distance, elongation, and shove. High Mass with low Force is a
  thick splash near the source; low Mass with high Force is a thin far-reaching
  spray.
- Thrown splats colonise rather than displace, so a glob that clears a wall can
  establish detached Green that then has to survive fragment decay, reconnect,
  mature, and accumulate Prey Pressure on its own.
- Where the splatter can go is decided by ray clearance against arena geometry, so
  a wall spreads the impact sideways, a corridor funnels a jet, and a pillar
  leaves two clear rays around it. None of those cases is special-cased.
- Roughly a dozen splats reach the simulation; dozens of microdroplets are drawn
  by the overlay and never become field objects.

## Physical presentation for Bramble, Root, and Pruning

- Bramble now publishes drawable tethers: vines rooted in the Green beneath an
  Entangled enemy, more and thicker strands as Entanglement deepens, stretched by
  a moving enemy and snapping back visibly when the restraint ends. The movement
  logic stays deterministic in the combat loop — nothing simulates rope.
- Root gained the material properties the path is supposed to be about: lower
  fragmentation, better pressure transmission through Rooted ground, and one
  cohesion figure shared with the field, so a Bloom out of heavily Rooted
  territory physically behaves differently from one out of Fresh Green.
- The field now exposes cohesion and mass directly, and takes a cohesion
  multiplier that Metabolism and Root both feed.

- `queue_maturity_support` was a silent no-op: the queue clamped its strength to
  zero even though the stamp reads a negative suppression as support. The command
  is signed now, which is what Ancient Hunger and Rhizome-style feeding need.
- Moved the relics row clear of the evolution readout in the side panel; the two

## Relic system rebuilt around the new catalog

- Added `RelicCatalog`, the authored relic table: identity, rarity, pool,
  mechanic requirements, and player text, as pure data with no scene
  dependencies. Drafting, the HUD, and the tests all read the same rows.
- Added `RelicDirector` under `GameplayComponents`, which owns the runtime
  of every triggered relic: counters, cooldowns, per-wave flags, and the
  persistent objects a relic leaves standing in the arena. It subscribes to
  domain events and never polls the scene tree.
- Relics that are only a rule parameter are not simulated there at all. They are
  pushed once, when the relic set changes, into whichever component already owns
  that rule: Core Weapon rules into combat, the damage-absorption rule onto the
  player, the regrowth layer into the field, and Seed shields onto the overlay.
- The catalog is 48 relics: 12 Universal, 6 Plant Element, 3 per Path across all
  six Paths, and 8 curated synergies. The four relics that predate it are kept
  and folded into the Plant and Bramble pools rather than retired, which puts
  Plant slightly over the authored six-relic element target.
- Relic rewards are now choose-one-of-three, weighted by rarity, drawn without
  replacement from a run's eligible pool. The offer names each relic's rarity and
  tints the button by it. Previously the panel simply listed every relic the run
  did not already hold, which only worked while the pool was four entries long.
- Added relic rerolls, separate from upgrade rerolls, now that an offer is a
  sample rather than the whole pool. This is also what makes Recycling Bin mean
  anything: it guarantees one replacement at least as rare as what it replaced.
- Eligibility is expressed as required Mechanics rather than path pairs, so a
  synergy relic unlocks the moment the run can actually do both things it needs,
  whether that came from the initial pair, the support path, or an Evolution.
  Insurance Policy requires the optional-objective layer, which does not exist
  yet, so it is authored but never offered and will switch itself on when that
  layer lands.
- The relic row now shows what the run holds, in the order it was taken, with the
  trigger counter drawn on the slot itself: Predator's Eye reads `8 / 10`,
  Overpopulation reads `PRIMED`, The Hedge reads its cooldown.

## Recursion limiters

Every loop where one relic can feed another carries a limiter, and each one is
covered by a test:

- An Overpopulation blast carries the `RELIC` death source, so it can neither
  spend the next primed charge nor count toward it.
- Encore skips Legendary triggers, its repeats never advance its own counter, and
  it does not repeat relics that create permanent objects.
- Cross-Pollination and Pollinator can only advance a Seed that is off its
  receiving cooldown.
- A Feeding Ring free Bite is reduced and does not reset normal Digestion.
- The Hedge, Superbloom, and the Shared Hunger relic each hold a global
  cooldown; Gleaning Hook caps what it can bank in one hold.

## Carnivory could never bud a Maw in a real run

- Measured a full run with Carnivory as the primary path, engine frames and all,
  out past the boss: zero Buds, zero Maws, zero Bites in 390 seconds. The peak
  Prey Pressure on any single cell reached 0.76 against a threshold of 10.
- The cause was where the enemy-seconds were credited, not the numbers. Pressure
  was banked only by enemies standing on ground the run owns, but enemies erode
  the frontier from the hostile side: sampled every 30s, the best control under
  any enemy was −1.00 in most samples and 0–5 enemies were ever on Green at once.
- Enemies eating the frontier now bank a reduced share (`frontier_pressure_share`,
  0.75) into the nearest owned frontier cell within `frontier_feed_radius_pixels`
  (30). The deposit lands on the Green rather than on the hostile ground the
  attacker stands on, because the field only banks pressure on owned cells.
- `TerritoryField.find_player_frontier_near_local` returns that cell. It rejects a
  frontier flag on a cell whose control has already gone: the mask is written by
  the same step that resolves control, so a cell lost on that step can still be
  flagged, and a deposit aimed at it evaporates.
- Retuned what the fixed rule exposed: Prey Pressure decay 0.55/s → 0.05/s,
  deposit radius 3 → 4.5 cells, and the budding threshold 10 → 2.5 enemy-seconds.
  Pressure now banks across a wave instead of bleeding out between contacts.
- Same measured run after the fix: first Maw at 142.5s, 8 Buds and 49 Bites
  over the run, with a Maw standing for most of it. The smoke suite now covers a
  frontier attacker banking pressure into the Green it is eating.
- Added F11, which prints and shows the Carnivory readout the path never had:
  whether the path is held, peak Prey Pressure against the budding threshold the
  scan actually uses, total pressure, Buds, and Maws against capacity. Prey
  Pressure is field data with no display, so a run that never buds gave no way to
  tell whether the metric was climbing or flat.

## Maws live on their ground, not on hit points

Playtest model: strip Maw HP entirely and let the territory carry the predator.

- A Bud is the whole of Carnivory's vulnerability. It needs Green directly under
  it, a Saboteur within reach cuts it down (`bud_durability` 3.0 against
  `saboteur_bud_damage_per_second` 2.5), and it now takes 1.6s to emerge rather
  than 2.6s.
- Emerging grants 1 second of root-support immunity, so a Maw that opens on a
  contested frontier is not pulled straight back under. If prey are already
  standing in the Jawspan it bites on emergence instead of owing them a telegraph.
- A grown Maw has no ordinary HP and normal enemies cannot destroy it. It stands
  while its root zone (`root_radius_pixels`, 76) holds enough Green: below 30% of
  the root mass it emerged with it retracts, above 60% of that mass it rises
  again, and if the ground does not come back within `root_recovery_seconds` (6)
  it dies and frees its capacity slot. A retracted Maw keeps its slot while it
  waits, and is drawn as a closed socket with its remaining time draining round
  the rim.
- The 30%/60% are shares of that Maw's own required mass, not of a fully owned
  disc. Measured against a full disc, a Maw budded on a frontier reads 0.19
  support and would retract the instant its protection lapsed — the hostile half
  of its radius is ground it can never own. `root_minimum_baseline` (0.25) keeps a
  Maw that emerged on a sliver from asking nothing of the ground thereafter.
- Nearby prey accelerate Bite readiness: +35% per extra body in the Jawspan, to a
  3x ceiling.
- Same scripted run as before, kiting player: first Maw at 90s, both capacity
  slots held from 120s onward, and 113 Bites against 40 under the old model. The
  predator persists instead of churning.
- Covered: retract on losing the root zone, re-emergence when the Green closes
  back over it, death and slot release after the recovery window, the Saboteur
  cutting a Bud, the Bite on emergence, and the density ceiling.

## Biomass becomes a bought economy

Implementing the incremental rework's recommended prototype.

- Automatic allocation is gone. Allocation points, the per-point investment rate,
  the throughput split, and the free-points readout are deleted: feeding the
  systems from a pool turned the economy into "allocate, then watch bars fill",
  which was less interactive than the purchases it replaced.
- Levels are now bought outright on three physical axes — Mass, Pressure,
  Cohesion — at 20, 35, 61, 107, 188, 328, 574 Biomass. Nothing levels with time.
- The economy unfolds on the run clock instead of arriving whole: Mass from the

  immediately.

  shows. A purchase that only moved a number would not be a purchase worth making.

  get visibly different results.
- Breakthroughs at level 5 per axis, production milestones, and Overgrow are
  unchanged. Vascular Transport, Critical Harvest, Overflow and automation stay
  unimplemented, per the doc's own scope.

## Slot contents can be nudged by hand

## Carnivory is scrapped; Man-Eater is the relic it should have been

As a Path, Maws had to support a whole twelve-minute build, which forced answers
to how many there are, how durable, how they reposition, what happens when they
are swarmed, and how four upgrade lines and five Evolutions scale them. None of
that made the fantasy better. The fantasy was one moment: a giant plant erupts out
of the Green and eats a mob.

- Deleted the Carnivory Path: `PathId.CARNIVORY`, its four upgrade lines, its five
  pair Evolutions (Snap Pods, Feeding Frenzy, Strangletrap, Ancient Hunger,
  Uprooting), the 1,372-line director, and the scene node. Five Paths remain, and
  Pruning now pairs into four Evolutions rather than five.
- Retired the six relics built on persistent Maws: Trapdoor, Shared Hunger, Apex
  Predator, Carrion Garden, Feeding Bloom, Feeding Ring. Nothing in the catalog
  refers to a Maw that no longer exists.
- Added **Man-Eater**, a Plant Legendary. Enemies standing on Green build Hunger at
  one enemy-second each, capped per second so a late horde stays readable: one
  enemy needs the whole 30, five need six seconds, fifteen need two. When it fills,
  the densest cluster on Green is chosen, the ground bulges for 0.7s — an event
  enemies can walk out of, not invisible damage — and the jaws close on up to
  eight. Normal enemies are swallowed, elites take 55% of the Bite, a boss takes
  30% and is never eaten. The meal comes back as a Bloom command at the site, so
  the enemies become more Plant. Then it retracts completely.
- No Maw HP, no Capacity, no Digestion, no Budding, no placement problem, and
  nothing standing on the field for an enemy to kill before it does anything.
- Deleted the field's Prey Pressure channel outright — the per-cell buffer, its
  decay pass, the peak search, the frontier-feeding added earlier today, and the
  root-support query. Hunger is one scalar counted off enemy positions, so the
  simulation carries nothing for it between eruptions.
- F11 now reports Man-Eater's Hunger against its threshold, meals, swallowed
  count, and lockout, replacing the Carnivory readout.
- Covered: Hunger scaling with the crowd and staying at zero without the relic, the
  telegraph firing before anything is damaged, the swallow and the Hunger reset
  behind its lockout, the Maw leaving nothing behind, and a boss being bitten but
  never eaten.
- The rule this establishes, worth keeping: when a mechanic has one fantastic
  moment but is not deep enough to sustain a Path, turn that moment into a relic
  rather than throwing it away.

## Pruning was a homing-volley launcher wearing a ring

- Reported as "the Bramble + Pruning evolution is always on". It is not: measured
  side by side, a run with no Bramble and a run with Bramble but no Evolution both
  raise 18 spikes from 84 harvested cells, and Deadfall adds 11 more only when the
  run has actually evolved into it. The gate was never leaking.
- The real fault was the ring turning over. `_trim_orbit` launches the oldest spike
  whenever the ring exceeds capacity, and the harvest kept raising new spikes while
  the ring was already full, so every harvested cutting joined the disc and ejected
  an older one as a homing thorn. Measured: 78 spikes raised in a 10-second hold,
  62 of them fired off as free homing volleys — about six a second, for as long as
  the player held the ring. The disc, which is meant to be the path's identity and
  its damage, was incidental.
- A full ring now sheds on an authored interval rather than on arrival:
  `full_ring_replacement_seconds`, 1.5s by default. Between sheds the harvest banks
  toward the next one and never past a single spike, so a long hold cannot save up
  a volley to dump the moment the timer elapses.
- Same 10-second hold after the fix: 20 spikes raised, 4 of them shed. The ring
  fills, stays full, and turns over occasionally. It still eats the same 358 cells,
  so holding a finished ring costs exactly as much territory as before.
- Deadfall keeps the rapid fire, because that is what the Evolution should have
  been all along: `deadfall_replacement_seconds` (0.15s) replaces the base interval
  while the run holds it, so the ring goes from shedding a thorn now and then to
  throwing them off continuously. Measured over a six-second hold on a full ring:
  0.7 shed per second on base Pruning, 5.7 per second with Deadfall — the same feel
  the bug had, now as the payoff for taking the pairing.
- Deadfall also still converts the torn-out thorns into extra Cuttings, so the
  Evolution raises more spikes and throws them faster.
- Covered: a full ring sheds within its authored interval and keeps paying for the
  ground it stands on, and Deadfall's interval is a fraction of the base one.

## Evolutions get their own node, and paths get their own numbers

Progression was five jobs in one node: XP and drafting, upgrade levels *and* every
path's effect numbers, path selection, the whole Evolution system, relics, and the
Biomass currency. On top of that, each path director carried the export groups for
the Evolutions its path takes part in, so Super Bloom's tuning sat on Spore and on
Bloom at once and Deadfall's sat in the middle of Pruning's own numbers. Reading
what one Evolution did meant opening three Inspectors.

- New `Evolutions` node under GameplayComponents owns the lot: the pairing
  table, readiness and the grant flow, names and descriptions, the optional
  upgrade line each Evolution unlocks, its rank descriptions, and every number —
  the 66 lines that were on Progression plus the 41 exported values pulled off the
  Spore, Bloom and Pruning directors. Twelve export groups, one Inspector panel.
- The behaviour stayed with the paths, deliberately. Deadfall still adds Cuttings
  inside Pruning's harvest and Coppice still buries Seeds where the ring cut,
  because the harvest and the ring are Pruning's. A director asks the node whether
  the run holds an Evolution and what its numbers are, then does its own work;
  nothing reaches into a director to act on its behalf.
- Each path's effect numbers moved to the path: Spore took the Seed kill
  thresholds and growth mass, Bloom took Force and interval, Pruning took its four.
  They read the level from Progression and apply their own number, which is what
  Bramble and Root already did — those two were the pattern, and now every path
  follows it.
- Progression keeps the shared vocabulary (PathId, UpgradeId, EvolutionId), the
  draft, paths, relics and Biomass, and delegates the Evolution questions so the
  sixty-odd call sites that ask it about Evolutions still can.
- Progression 1,600 lines to 1,255. Its Inspector went from nine export groups to
  six, and none of them are another system's tuning.
- One wrinkle worth recording: the two scripts reference each other at runtime, so
  Progression's reference is typed `Node` rather than `Evolutions`. Declaring
  the concrete type made the two scripts cyclic at parse time and neither resolved.

- The slot row gained an optional `slot_texture_style`. When set, the art draws the
  square and the palette only tints it, so a filled, empty or locked slot still
  reads as itself; left empty the row draws flat exactly as before.
- The art was resampled before use. At full size a progress-bar corner is 37px
  against an 11px-tall bar, and the ninepatch ballooned into overlapping blobs —
  caught on the first capture. Button, slot and bar art are scaled to 0.20–0.34 so
  their corners are proportionate, and the three bars gained 4px of height.

## Bloom splatter is a thrown object now, not a stamped decal

- The splatter only ever respected basalt. It sampled a ray in each direction,
  found how far it could travel before a wall, and stamped its globs, satellites
  and streaks at the far end instantly. Hostile and neutral ground had no say in
  any of it, which is why the paint never looked like it was hitting anything.
- Every piece of thrown material is now a Glob with a position, a velocity and a
  Mass, launched by the eruption and stepped each frame. What it crosses decides
  where it ends up: basalt and the arena edge stop it dead, hostile ground drags
  hard on it and charges Mass per pixel crossed, neutral ground costs a little, and
  the run's own Green barely slows it — paint slides on wet paint.
- The streak is the trail of something moving rather than splats placed along a
  sampled ray: a glob lays a splat every `glob_trail_spacing` pixels out of the
  Mass it is still carrying, so a long throw thins as it goes and a short one lands
  heavy.
- Measured, one glob thrown along a lane of a single owner:

  | ground crossed | travelled | Mass on landing |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | the run's own Green | 410 px | 0.61 of 4.00 |
  | neutral | 205 px | 1.67 |
  | hostile | 72 px | 1.82 |

- Hostile crossing is charged by distance, not by time. A per-second drain barely
  registered — hostile ground stops a glob so fast that it had a tenth of a second
  to act — so it landed with more Mass than a throw across friendly ground.
- The drawn burst is seeded from the material actually in the air, so the spectacle
  and the simulation now agree about where the paint went. The pre-flight ray
  sampler and the two knobs that only fed it are gone.
- Covered: a Bloom throws its material and lays its splats where the globs land,
  and a throw over the run's own Green carries measurably further than one into
  hostile ground.

## Bloom splatter atomises like paint does

- The thrown material was still a handful of equal lumps in three authored
  categories. A bursting liquid does not do that: it atomises into a spectrum,
  mostly fine mist with a few heavy globs, and what happens next is decided by
  each droplet's own size.
- One eruption now breaks into `droplet_count` droplets whose radii are drawn from
  a biased distribution. Mass is volume, so each droplet takes the share of the
  eruption's Mass that its own r-cubed represents and the burst conserves what it
  was given however many pieces it breaks into.
- Air drag acts on cross-section and is resisted by Mass, so deceleration goes as
  speed squared over radius. Terrain drag is scaled by the same ballistic
  coefficient: a droplet is stopped by ground a heavy lump plows through.
- The launch model was wrong first time and the measurement caught it. Giving every
  droplet equal energy made mist the fastest thing on the field and left the heavy
  globs at the origin — backwards. An explosion delivers energy per unit Mass, so
  every droplet leaves at a similar speed and drag does the sorting:

  | radius | mass | launch | travelled |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 3 px | 0.11 | 735 px/s | 24.7 px |
  | 10 px | 4.00 | 592 px/s | 146.9 px |
  | 22 px | 42.59 | 514 px/s | 224.4 px |

- Bloom Force and Meristem Pressure feed the blast as energy rather than as speed,
  so it arrives as a square root: four times the Force is twice the throw, and drag
  eats into the difference from there — 73, 147, 191, 208 px at 0.5x, 1x, 2x, 4x.
- Covered: fine mist is thrown fastest and falls short while heavy globs carry, and
  four times the Force is exactly twice the launch speed.

## The focus ring is a controller affordance now

- The pause menu grabbed focus whenever it opened, so a mouse player got a white
  box parked on Resume with nothing to explain it.
- The last input event decides: a controller button or a stick past its deadzone
  shows the ring, and any mouse movement, mouse button or key takes it away again.
  Opening the menu with a keyboard now focuses nothing at all.
- Covered end to end: no focus on open, focus after a pad button, released again on
  mouse movement, and released again after a key.

## Ecosystem Breakthroughs: two were broken, two were invisible

- Measured all six against the thing each claims to change, with a no-breakthrough
  control. Flexible and Woody Tissue were working all along — advection, cohesion
  and fragment decay all move. The rest were not.
- **Lateral Growth did nothing at all.** Its frontier share was 1.00, which is
  exactly the value an uninvested run already has, so choosing it changed no number
  anywhere. It now spreads wider than the default, and the injection path honours a
  share above one instead of only narrowing.
- The Mass pulses were far too small to see: a pulsing run and a non-pulsing one
  were within a twentieth of a percent of territory after ten seconds. The pulse
  budget per level is up from 5 to 42, which lifts Rapid Cycling clear of the
  control, and a pulse now carries outward Pressure as well as Mass — a pulse of
  pure Mass can only top up cells that are already full.
- `overgrow` reported the Mass it *offered* rather than what the ground accepted,
  which is what made this hard to see. It now reports the accepted amount, and the
  reach of an injection scales with its budget instead of dividing a fixed budget
  across every frontier cell.
- Storage Organs is still the weak half of its pair and I could not close that gap:
  its rare large release lands on a frontier whose cells are already near full, so
  most of the budget is refused. Concentrating the burst made it worse, not better.
  The honest read is that hoarding only pays off with a large border, which may be
  the intent — but at present Rapid Cycling is the better choice at every point I
  measured.

## Splatter lands where it was thrown, and is big enough to see

- Landings were symmetric discs pushing outward in every direction: nothing about a
  splat knew which way the paint had been travelling. A splat now carries its
  impact direction and is stretched along it, its centre sits forward of the impact
  rather than on it, and the Pressure it applies pushes the ground it claims
  downrange instead of radiating. Measured on a clean field: a splat with no
  direction claims 10 cells along by 8 across, and the same splat thrown right
  claims 23 by 8, reaching 15 cells past the impact instead of 4.
- The globs were also simply too small to matter — about 2.6 cells across against a
  central splat of 13.5, so each landing was a fifth the size of the thing it was
  meant to be seen beside. Fewer, larger droplets: count 14 to 9, radius range 3-22
  to 13-58 pixels, and a landing delivers six times its carried Mass rather than
  three.
- Same measured Bloom before and after the two changes: +0.813% territory, then
  +1.460%. The landings now read as separate islands of Green outside the main
  body, on the side they were thrown toward.
- One test threshold had to be loosened afterwards. The mist-versus-glob check
  asserted a 3x range difference, which held only because of the terrain that
  particular run happened to cross; it asserts the ordering now, which is the
  actual claim.

## Written: where real physics fits the remaining paths

- `docs/physics_opportunities.md` surveys every path against the equation that
  describes what it is currently approximating with a multiplier, with the rule
  that an equation earns its place only by producing behaviour we would otherwise
  author case by case.
- It starts by naming what the field already is: the diffusion, sharpening and
  advection terms in the fixed step are Fick's second law plus the Allen-Cahn
  double-well, which makes the territory simulation a phase-field model. That is
  worth knowing because motion by mean curvature is already implied by it and not
  yet used.
- Ranked by behaviour gained against risk: Spore's fragment survival as classical
  nucleation theory and Pruning's ring as angular momentum come first, because each
  deletes authored numbers rather than adding them.
