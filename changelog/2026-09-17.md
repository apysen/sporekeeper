# 2026-09-17

Two things: the mechanics glossary lost its background-systems half, and Cankers went
in. The second is the first mechanic built from `sporekeeper_territory_consequence_rework_implementation.md`
rather than from the GDD, and the doc's sections 14 through 22 are close enough to a
specification that most of this day was implementing them and then fixing the two places

## The glossary lost seven entries it could never reach

Every keyword description is now two sentences or fewer, with Root Network at three.
That was the whole ask, and it was the smaller half of the change.

The larger half came out of checking what the Metabolism cluster is actually attached
to. Metabolism, Biomass, Breakthrough, Cambium, Meristem, Photosynthesis and Cohesion
appear **zero** times in reward text, upgrade text, evolution text, or any UI label -
against twelve uses of Mass, three of Pressure and three of Overgrow. Their only
appearances anywhere are node paths in `run.tscn` and internals in `metabolism.gd`.

That is not just clutter. `show_reward_tooltips` builds cards by scanning reward text
for keywords, so those seven could never fire from the path that produces most glossary
reads. A player reached them only by opening the glossary and following links between
them - a closed loop of seven terms that mostly cross-referenced each other.

They are gone. Two survivors had to be rewritten to stop leaning on the deleted
vocabulary: Green no longer "generates Biomass", and Overgrow no longer "spends part of
the Biomass Reserve" - it spends the growth you have banked, which is a thing the player
can see. `metabolism.gd` still returns "Meristem" and "Cambium" from `display_name`; if
a Metabolism readout is ever built, those names come back and the entries can come with
them.

`mechanics_help_visual_probe` had three dependencies on the deleted terms, including a
word-boundary guard proving "Biomass" does not trigger the Mass card. The guard was
worth keeping, so it now uses "A massive surge of Pressure" instead of being deleted
along with the term it happened to be written against.

## Cankers

A Canker is Rot that has been held long enough to become infrastructure. Once one
forms, a slice of ordinary reinforcements stops entering from the perimeter and arrives
beside the Knot instead.

The rule the whole feature is built around is section 18's: **a Canker changes spawn
topology, never spawn quantity.** Same enemies, same budget, same cap - different door.
`canker_probe` asserts it directly by counting the population across sixty spawn
requests rather than by inspecting the redirect in isolation, because that is the only
form of the check that would notice if a redirect ever became an addition.

Knots have no health. `CankerRules.advance_stage` contains no damage term anywhere, and
the only route from ACTIVE back to INACTIVE is the local Rot ratio falling below the
destroy threshold and staying there. Taking the ground back is the answer, which is the
message the structure exists to deliver.

### Where it lives

- `src/core/rules/territory/canker_rules.gd` - the ladder, hysteresis, candidate
  scoring, the redirect cap, and the telegraph curve. Pure, and driven one transition
  at a time by `tests/unit/canker_rules_probe.gd`.
- `src/core/nodes/territory/canker_director.gd` - the sites, the measurement, the
  paintings, and which spawn requests a Knot may answer.
- `TerritoryField.measure_rot_ratio_in_radius` - hostile cells over *claimable* cells,
  not over every cell in the disc. A circle overlapping a wall would otherwise read as
  contested ground, and a Canker would refuse to grow against a wall it is perfectly
  happy to grow against.
- `TerritoryField.get_playable_bounds_local` - see the clipping section.

The art is four authored paintings in `assets/art/cankers/`. Each Knot draws one at a
random rotation, both fixed when it forms and both replayed by the run seed, following
the pattern Fruit already uses in `adaptation_director.gd`.

### Two places the doc's numbers produced the wrong feel

**A Canker formed twelve seconds into every run.** The arena opens with a hostile basin
already on it, and that basin clears the 65% activation ratio on its own. Following
sections 15 and 16 exactly, the first Knot appeared before the player had done anything
to deserve one - a timer, not a consequence, which is what section 14 asks the system
not to be.

The gate is `first_wave`, default 3. Sites measure from the start and none of them
climbs before that wave. It went in as a stopwatch first and was moved to the wave
clock, which is the better unit for the same reason the doc keeps reaching for player
legibility elsewhere: the wave is the run's own measure of escalation and the only one
on screen. *"Cankers start at wave three"* is a rule a player can hold; *"after
forty-five seconds"* is one they would have to discover. Both modes run sixty-second
waves, so it means the same thing in each, and `get_current_wave_number` is derived from
elapsed time in both rather than being Campaign-only.

**Twenty-eight regions warned and two delivered.** This one was only visible in a
screenshot. Slots were checked at formation, so on a board that had gone fully to Rot
every site entered WARNING on the same tick, drew its ring, and then twenty-six of them
silently reverted when the cap was already spent. A telegraph that fires fourteen times
per Knot is a telegraph the player learns to ignore, so slots are now reserved when a
site *starts* warning, counted against active and warning sites together. Both the
warning grant and the formation grant rank their candidates by
`CankerRules.candidate_score` rather than taking them in grid order - ground goes to Rot
in sheets, and array order would put the Knot in the top-left of the blob instead of the
worst part of the board.

### Nothing paints past the outer wall

A Knot is a 150px painting placed at a point, so a site near the edge put half of it
through the wall and into the HUD.

Clipping is the right answer rather than insetting the sites: ground beside the wall is
exactly the ground a player abandons first, so a Canker has to be able to grow there and
simply be cut off by the boundary, the way the washes already are. Three things were
wrong on the way to that working:

1. A CanvasItem has no draw-time clip rect. `clip_children` masks a node's *children*,
   and every Knot is an immediate draw call on the layer itself, so this goes through
   `RenderingServer.canvas_item_set_clip` plus a custom rect.
2. The custom rect has to be re-applied at the end of every `_draw`. The engine
   recomputes an item's rect from its commands on each redraw, so setting it once in
   `_ready` leaves the layer unclipped from the first frame onward.
3. The clip rect is **not** `arena_size`. The outer wall is built from the field's own
   outermost cells rather than drawn around the outside, so the arena rect includes it
   and a Knot clipped to it still painted over the stonework.
   `get_playable_bounds_local` derives the inside of the wall from the same
   `_is_border_cell` rule the claimable mask is built from, so the two cannot drift.

`canker_visual_probe` puts a Knot in each corner and samples the framebuffer just
outside the wall on each side. It was verified the way these should be, by flipping the
clip off and confirming it fails. That probe also had to convert field-local through the
canvas transform *and* the window stretch to reach a pixel index - a canvas coordinate
used directly reads the wrong part of the screen by exactly the stretch factor, which is
how it first passed against a screenshot that visibly showed the bug.

## Tests

```
powershell -File scripts/run_tests.ps1 -SkipPerformance

45 test(s), 698 check(s), 0 failed.
All tests passed.
```

`green_wash_probe` is worth a note: it failed twice in the middle of this day's work and
passed on either side of that, without the tree changing in any way that touches it.
While it was failing it was *deterministic* - 6036 of 1,762,381 owned pixels, the same
number every run - and the identical failure reproduced on a clean worktree of `343b18b`
with none of today's changes in it, which is how it was cleared. So it is flaky rather
than broken, and the flake is environmental rather than in the tree. The shape that
allows it is `paper_on_owned == 0`: a zero-tolerance threshold on a framebuffer read,
where the observed miss is 0.34%. A small tolerance would make it say what it means.

`canker_rules_probe` (31 checks), `canker_probe` (26) and `canker_visual_probe` (9) are
new. Two bugs in the probes themselves are worth recording, because both produced
*passing-looking* output while measuring nothing:

- **A GDScript lambda captures locals by value.** `director.canker_formed.connect(func(_p): formed = true)`
  sets a copy; the check that followed read the original and reported that no Knot had
  formed, while the knot count in the very next assertion showed two. The flags are
  script members now, with the reason written next to them.
- **`neutralize_cells` only releases ground the *player* holds.** Used to reclaim a
  fully hostile field it is a no-op, so the reclaim test was asserting that a Knot
  survived a reclaim that had never happened. It now takes the ground back with
  `queue_splat` and stepped simulation, the way the game does.

## Still to do

- **The warning has no sound and no localized text.** `canker_warning_started` fires and
  the region pulses, but `_set_status` is a no-op sink in this build, so the doc's
  "ROT TAKING ROOT" reaches nothing. It wants a world-space label near the region, not a
  banner.
- **Cankers are not in the glossary.** Deliberate, for now: the entries added today were
  cut down to what the player meets in reward text, and a Canker is not named in any yet.
- **Nothing is saved.** A Knot does not survive a save and resume. `RunSnapshot` takes
  one dictionary per system and the Canker director does not contribute one, so a
  resumed run starts with a clean board and the grace running again.
- **The tuning is the doc's starting points.** Every number in section 15 and 18 is
  exported and none of it has been played against a real run.
