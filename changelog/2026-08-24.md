# Changelog: 2026-08-24

## The Clover machine now wagers Territory instead of Biomass

The machine in the side panel was still the older design: a Biomass stake, weighted
reels, and a payout table with minor and strong tiers on it. The master guide replaces
all of that, and the reason it replaces it is worth stating, because it decides most of
what follows. Biomass funds Metabolism, and Metabolism develops automatically from the
run's *lifetime* earnings. A machine that spends Biomass therefore cannot really hurt
the player, because it cannot touch the growth that Biomass already bought. Territory
can. So the stake is now a physical region of the organism, and the reward is a Plant
Path unleashed on the battlefield — two different axes, wired to two different systems.

- `CloverSlot` holds the vocabulary and the winning grammar as pure rules with no run
  state: seven faces, four stakes, four outcomes. Everything else reads the grammar from
  there, which is the only way the code that decides whether a line paid stays the same
  code as the one that drew it.
- Clover is a Path-only wild. It completes `Bloom / Clover / Bloom`, it does not complete
  `Bust / Bust / Clover`, and it does not choose between two different Paths, so
  `Bloom / Root / Clover` is worth nothing. Clover always reads as good news or as
  nothing; it is never the symbol that ruined the line.
- Stakes are 10 / 25 / 50 / 100% of currently owned Green, measured at the instant the
  lever moves. A higher notch does not buy a bigger prize — the prize is already a maxed
  Path — it buys better odds and a worse floor.
- Minimum buy-in: 10% of the claimable board must be Established-or-better Green, using
  the field's existing Established threshold rather than a second private definition of
  maturity. It applies to All In too, so a nearly dead run cannot use 100% as a free
  lottery ticket. Below it the machine stays visible and says what is missing.

## The wager is a shape, not a subtraction

- `WagerSelector` grows the region outward from the player with a Dijkstra flood
  over owned cells. Step cost is `1 + 1.5 * (1 - maturity) + 1.0 * exposure`, so mature
  interior tissue is cheap and the exposed rim is expensive. Standing in open Green gives
  something roughly circular; standing at the edge gives a region that deforms inward
  through the organism rather than stamping a radius over ground nobody owns.
- Measured on the starting island: a Medium wager averages 0.060 boundary exposure
  against the board's 0.092, and 0.258 maturity against 0.198. It reaches inward, which
  is the entire point — a bet that only ever costs the fringe is a bet that costs nothing.
- If the player's island cannot fill the stake the flood jumps to the nearest remaining
  Green and continues, so a fragmented organism produces a wager in several pieces. That
  is the honest picture of what a fragmented organism is.
- Ties break on cell index. Without that the preview flickers between identical-looking
  regions while the player stands still, which reads as the machine being unable to make
  up its mind.
- The preview is hover-only and rebuilt at 10 Hz, and only when the seed cell or the
  notch actually changed. It pulses while the bet is still hypothetical and freezes the
  moment the lever goes down: preview is player-relative, commitment is world-relative.

## Outcome-first randomness, and a readout that does not lie about it

- The result is rolled when the lever moves and the three faces are then *built* to
  represent it. Spinning three independent wheels would make drought protection and Bust
  anti-streaking impossible without the symbols quietly misreporting what the machine
  does.
- Drought protection starts after four dry spins at a stake, adds up to +30pp of Path
  jackpot, and takes every point of it out of Whiff. Never out of Bust, which would make
  a losing streak safer, and never out of Triple Clover. Counted per stake, so cheap Low
  spins cannot charge up pity and cash it out on an All In.
- Triple Bust cannot repeat: 0% on the next spin, half on the one after. The removed
  probability becomes Whiff.
- The panel prints the odds this spin will actually roll, after both. The backend may
  protect the player from ugly randomness; the readout may not misreport that it did.
- Whiff lines are built by rejection rather than by construction. Hand-building a
  guaranteed loser means writing the winning grammar down a second time, and a second
  copy of a rule is a copy that will eventually disagree with the first.

## What a jackpot actually does

- A jackpot lends the Path its ceiling. `Progression.get_upgrade_level` now returns
  `max(owned, 5)` for a Path the casino has lit, which means every director fires its
  real mechanic at maxed strength without knowing the casino exists. There is no
  casino-only imitation of Bloom or Root anywhere in the codebase.
- The loan is strictly a loan. Drafting, slot occupancy, upgrade descriptions and
  evolution readiness all moved to a new `get_owned_upgrade_level`, so a jackpot cannot
  fill a growth-line slot or unlock an Evolution on the way past. Ownership itself is
  never granted, so a relic that requires the player to have selected the Path keeps
  requiring it.
- Root 15s, Bramble 10s, Pruning 10s, stacking by duration and never by strength. Level 5
  is the top of the Path and a second win has nowhere above it to go, so a second Root
  jackpot buys another fifteen seconds rather than a Root nobody has numbers for.
- Spore and Bloom resolve as discrete board-wide events through the real entry points,
  scaled by `0.75 + 0.75 * sqrt(owned_fraction)` — large guaranteed spectacle, bent
  upward by Territory rather than driven by it, so a jackpot is enormous even when the
  run owns a corner.
- Pruning's jackpot releases the ring rather than running a harvest cycle: the casino
  supplies the clippings, and a jackpot must not pay for itself out of the player's Green.

## Losing

- Whiff converts the committed region to Neutral. Only cells still Green at resolution —
  ground the enemy took during the spin stays taken rather than being handed back as
  neutral, which would make losing the bet a favour.
- Triple Bust converts the region to Hostile. Neutral in the region goes with it. That is
  the whole difference between an expensive spin and a catastrophe: one takes the tissue,
  the other leaves something that has to be fought back through.
- All In whiff takes every owned cell, including Green grown during the spin, and starts
  a three-second grace period with the ordinary extinction countdown suspended. Reclaim
  5% of the board and the run continues, with nothing granted for surviving. The grace
  controller checks recovery before spending the clock, so hitting the threshold on the
  frame the timer expires is a rescue: the visible number and the deadline behind it have
  to agree, and when they disagree by a frame the player gets the frame.
- All In Bust turns the entire claimable field hostile and ends the run. No grace, no
  threshold. It is the one result in the machine that ends a run by itself.

## The relic row is coloured by rarity

`UpgradeSlots.set_slot` takes an optional accent. Unset it is the row's own palette,
so the growth-line row is untouched; the relic row passes
`RelicCatalog.get_rarity_color`, which already existed and had no caller outside the
draft panel.

The accent takes the border outright and only darkens into the fill, so a rarity reads as
the edge of the slot rather than as a coloured square fighting the abbreviation sitting on
it. The lettering is lifted toward white rather than set to the accent: legendary gold text
on a legendary gold border is a slot with nothing readable in it. And colour is never the
only signal - the tooltip still names the rarity in words, the same rule the reel faces and
the Bust symbol follow.

## The coordinate bug was in eight more places

The wager fix earlier today corrected the Clover machine's calls. The same mistake was in
every other consumer that predates this work: `root_network.advance`,
`overlay.set_root_demand`, `overlay.set_overgrow_aim`, `_overgrow_aim_position`,
`_release_overgrow_at`, and the Root development effects. `player.position` is global,
`TerritoryField` sits at `(24, 104)` inside the scene, and every one of those takes an
arena-local position, so each was off by that much - `set_overgrow_aim` names its parameter
`local_position` and draws it directly, which put the aim ring a dozen cells from the
player. There are now no uses of `player.position` left in the run root.

## The four Clover relics

Two of them could not be built as written, because their text predates the gambling
rework. Both were rebuilt on the shape that survived rather than being dropped, and the
doc records what changed and why.

- **Bent Coin** (Common). Each Triple Bust bends the coin; on the third the next spin
  cannot Bust at all. The printed Bust chance drops to zero for that spin, because the
  readout may never advertise odds the machine is not about to roll. The shield is spent
  by whatever spin follows rather than waiting for a Bust that may never come - a zero
  printed indefinitely stops being information. The original said "no more than one Bust
  symbol", which means nothing under a grammar where a lone Bust is already worthless.
- **Side Pot** (Uncommon). Whiffs build the pot, the next jackpot cashes it into its own
  strength, a Triple Bust takes it. The original ran on Chips accumulating past a full
  Pot and paying "minor symbol rewards" - Chips, the Pot and minor rewards were all
  removed by the master guide, so what was kept is the shape: accumulate on failure, cash
  on a jackpot, lose to a Bust.
- **High Roller** (Rare). Every seventh spin doubles the jackpot and doubles the Bust, and
  the counter is in the readout, because a doubled spin the player only learns about
  afterwards is a coin flip rather than a decision. The doubled Bust lands on how hostile
  the ground becomes rather than on how much is taken, so the wager the player agreed to
  is still the wager they lose.
- **The House Always Loses** (Legendary). Each Triple Bust permanently cuts the machine's
  Bust odds, three times, and the third removal comes with a free spin that stakes no
  territory.

All four are rules about the odds table, so they live on `CloverOdds` where the
readout reads them, rather than being applied quietly at the point of the roll. They are
gated by a new baseline `Mechanic.GAMBLING`: the machine is permanently in the side panel,
so every run can reach them, and they needed no new pool eligibility beyond that. The
catalog is now 44 defined and 44 implemented.

One ordering trap worth recording: `_resolve` noted the outcome before computing the
jackpot scale, which would have cashed a Side Pot that had already been emptied and
doubled the wrong spin. Both are now read first.

## The four Pruning Evolutions finally have upgrade lines

Six of the ten pairings carried a five-rank Evolution line. The four that pair with
Pruning carried none: `get_evolution_upgrade_id` returned -1 for them, so
`get_evolution_upgrade_level` was permanently zero and the draft correctly offered those
runs nothing. A Pruning pairing had strictly less to draft than any other and stayed at
its base effect for the whole run. All twenty ranks are specified in
`docs/new_changes_0821.md` and none of them existed.

- **Second Flush** (Pioneer). Dense Reseeding buries more Seeds per cut; Rapid
  Germination shortens their fuse; Fertile Cut gives any Seed germinating on pruned
  ground more Growth Mass — read from the field's own pruned-ground timer rather than
  from a list Pruning would have to keep, so it works for every Seed that lands there and
  not only the ones the Evolution buried; Blood and Soil banks the ring's kills into
  extra Seeds, capped per cycle; Second Flush follows every third eruption with a
  half-size second wave that costs no further cut.
- **Compression** (Rebound). Efficient Compression I and II raise Force per collapsed
  cell and leave the existing cap alone, so the ranks buy a steeper curve rather than a
  higher ceiling; Elastic Growth widens the crater's accelerated reclaim; Inward Collapse
  drags enemies in before the harvest, so the pull reads as the cause of the collapse
  rather than its echo; Elastic Rebound springs a second wave 1.25s later at 45% Force —
  pressure and damage with the mass set to zero, because the doc is explicit that it
  creates no additional Plant Mass.
- **Splinterstorm** (Deadfall). Thorn Harvest I and II raise the thorn yield; Piercing
  Splinters adds pierce and softens the pierced hit so it widens a volley rather than
  squaring it; Shatter breaks a Cutting into two seeking splinters on a fully Entangled
  enemy, which keeps it as Bramble's payoff instead of a free doubling on every hit;
  Deadfall Volley throws a ring of eight heavy spikes off every cycle, raised outside the
  orbit so it never eats the ring the player is holding.
- **Stool Regrowth** (Coppicing). Mature Harvest I and II raise the Mature yield;
  Vigorous Regrowth drives the cut ground back faster, which is also what makes the last
  rank arrive inside the same fight; Root Memory feeds maturity back so regrowth over a
  Mature cut returns part-grown; Second Harvest watches the ground a cycle took and pays
  a free volley once half of it has returned.

`grant_casino_cuttings` became `grant_free_cuttings` now that two systems want clippings
that cost no territory: the Clover Pruning jackpot and Second Harvest.

## Coppice is now Pioneer

Coppice and Coppicing were one letter apart, and the collision was hiding a naming error
rather than only being hard to read. Coppicing is regrowth from a cut stool, which is
exactly what Root plus Pruning does, so that one keeps the name. Spore plus Pruning
regrows from seed on freshly disturbed ground — textbook pioneer growth — so it took the
new name. Its line is still Second Flush, which still fits.

## Spore Burst

A fourth Spore relic, Uncommon: germination detonates, damaging everything standing in
the growth it creates. The damage rides the Growth Mass the germination already pushes
into the field rather than being a flat number beside it, so it scales with Growth Mass,
the Root Network and Seed Bank's maturity bonus instead of carrying its own ladder.
Sourced as RELIC damage so a kill it causes cannot feed a relic trigger and start a chain
the player cannot see the end of.

Measured at the shipped values: a fresh Hunter goes from 28.0 to 6.7 HP. It softens a
crowd and finishes wounded enemies rather than clearing waves, which is where an Uncommon
should sit, and both numbers are on the Spore director in the Inspector.

The relics doc is updated: Spore's pool is four, the catalog is 44, and the prototype
implements 40 of them.

## The timed jackpots paid out nothing at all

Reported from play: a Bramble jackpot produced no thorns, Pruning produced no ring, and
Root was the same. All three had the same two causes, and the first one is mine.

- **The casino raised levels on mechanics that were switched off.** Bramble, Root and
  Pruning gate every effect on owning the path — `get_slow_fraction` returns 0.0, the
  thorn flag stays false, Pruning's ring refuses to hold — and the loan deliberately does
  not grant ownership. So the jackpot raised numbers nobody was reading. Ownership and
  activity are now two questions: `has_path` stays ownership and still decides drafting,
  which lines may be offered and which relics are eligible, while a new `has_path_active`
  means "this path's mechanics should be running", which a jackpot turns on. The directors
  ask the second; progression's own drafting and relic checks keep asking the first.
- **Bramble and Root never re-read their levels.** Both are push-style: `apply_modifiers`
  writes their numbers into the field and the combat loop once, when the build changes.
  A jackpot changes the build for ten seconds without anything else noticing, so it now
  says so at both ends — `jackpot_started` and `jackpot_ended` both re-push. The way out
  matters as much as the way in: without it a run would stay permanently maxed on
  whatever it last won.
- **Pruning had nothing to fire.** The jackpot released the ring, and at that instant the
  ring was empty. `grant_casino_cuttings` now supplies clippings outright, generated
  around the player rather than cut from the run's Green — a jackpot must not pay for
  itself with the territory it is rewarding.

Measured through the machine's own payout signal, five seconds in: thorns off → on, slow
fraction 0.00 → 0.35, Root maturation 1.00x → 2.10x, Pruning active with sixteen cuttings
orbiting, and every one of those back to baseline when the timers expire.

## A jackpot now stacks on top of what the run owns

The guide's rule was a floor — every line of the Path behaves as Level 5. That reads
badly for the path a run is actually built around: a player who has already maxed Root
hits a Root jackpot and gets handed back exactly what they already had. The loan is now
added instead. Level 5 becomes 10, level 4 becomes 9, and a Path the run does not own
still arrives at a full 5.

Nothing indexes an array by upgrade level, so the higher ranks simply extend the linear
per-level terms. The build readout in the upgrade tooltip shows the owned rank rather than
the borrowed one — it describes what the run has bought — while the "Now:" line beneath it
reads live values and so shows the boosted effect, which is the part that is actually
true for the next ten seconds.

## A colony core could be planted inside a wall, and then nothing could kill it

Reported as a run that could not be won. A Colonizer holding position against a wall can
overlap it, and `_try_establish_colony_core` planted the core at exactly the Colonizer's
position. Every radial damage source tests `is_growth_path_clear` from its centre to the
target, and no line reaches into solid rock, so a core inside a wall takes damage from no
direction at all. Clearing the field is a victory condition, so the run was unwinnable.

Placement now steps outward in rings, nearest first, to the closest spot where the core
fits on claimable ground, and refuses outright past 96px. Refusing is deliberate: a
Colonizer that finds nowhere legal should waste its action rather than plant something
invincible. An unobstructed placement does not move at all.

## The wager highlight is a field, not a grid of lit cells

- First version drew one `draw_rect` per selected cell. The selection is decided per
  cell because that is what a wager is counted in, but drawing it that way makes the
  highlight read as a debug view of the simulation rather than as part of the organism.
- `TerritoryWagerLayer` now uploads the selection as an R8 mask at the field's own
  resolution and reconstructs it in `wager_field.gdshader` with the same B-spline cubic
  filter the territory shader already uses for its border, so the highlight has the same
  kind of edge as the ground under it. One CPU blur pass first: a binary mask
  reconstructed on its own still carries the lattice in its contour, and the threshold is
  symmetric so blurring does not change how much ground the region covers.
- Its own layer between the field and the effects overlay rather than another pass inside
  the overlay, because a CanvasItem carries one material and the overlay has already
  spent its own. The rim is a band measured on the same reconstructed field, so it
  follows the smooth outline instead of the cell grid.

## The preview cost a whole frame, ten times a second

Reported as lag whenever the highlight appeared, and it was: on a mid-run organism of
3,348 owned cells a High-stake rebuild measured **26.5ms**, against a 16.67ms frame. The
algorithm was never the problem. Almost all of it was GDScript call overhead, and it came
off in four steps, each measured on the same board.

- **The field publishes a snapshot instead of answering per cell.** The flood asked
  `is_owned_cell`, `get_cell_maturity` and `get_cell_boundary_exposure` — and that last
  one asks `is_owned_cell` four more times — for every neighbour it considered. Roughly
  seven cross-object calls per neighbour, tens of thousands per rebuild.
  `refresh_ownership_snapshot` now does one pass and publishes maturity per owned cell
  with `-1.0` everywhere else, so a single array read answers both "is this owned" and
  "how mature". **26.5 → 19.0ms.**
- **The heap stopped calling out to compare and swap.** `_is_before` and `_swap` ran a
  dozen times per push across several thousand pushes. Written out inline, and sifted as
  a hole so the entry is written once at the end of the branch rather than swapped down
  it. **19.0 → 8.8ms.**
- **The binary heap became a bucket queue.** Step costs are bounded — one plus the two
  penalties — so the frontier never spans more than a fixed window and Dial's algorithm
  applies: a ring of buckets with O(1) push and pop, sized from the penalties in force so
  a newly pushed cell can never wrap past the bucket being drained. **8.8 → 4.4ms.**
- **Per-cell work is computed once and reused.** A cell's traversal cost is cached the
  first time the flood touches it rather than recomputed for each of the four neighbours
  that reach it, and a cell already queued at a cheaper cost is not queued again, which
  removes most of the duplicate entries the queue was carrying and popping.
  **4.4 → 3.4ms.**

Building the cost for the whole board up front was tried and is not in the build: it read
better but measured worse at every stake below All In, because the flood never sees most
of the board.

The mask upload was a separate fixed 1.6ms, because it blurred all 7,056 cells however
small the region was. It now blurs only the region's bounding box plus a margin — the
clear stays whole because that is a native memset. **1.6 → 0.46ms** at Low, 0.84ms at
High.

Measured end to end, walking with a 1,508-cell High wager on screen: average frame 4.15ms
against 4.00ms with nothing showing, **worst frame 8.23ms**. The same worst frame before
this work was around 32ms, which is a dropped frame every tenth of a second — the lag as
reported. Drawing the layer itself costs nothing per frame; the whole cost is the periodic
rebuild, and a player standing still pays none of it.

## The wager was being seeded a dozen cells from the player

- The flood starts under the player. That is the one thing about the wager the player can
  aim, and it was landing somewhere else entirely.
- `player.position` is global. `TerritoryField` sits at `(24, 104)` inside the scene, so
  everything that takes an arena-local position has to be handed
  `player.get_arena_position()`. Passing the global position seeded the region roughly
  three cells right and twelve cells down — measured at run start, the cell under
  `player.position` is not even owned, so `_find_origin` fell back to the nearest owned
  cell and the region grew from a corner of the island the player never chose.
- Fixed at every call the machine makes: preview rebuild, hover, lever pull, the Bust
  pulse, and the jackpot anchors. The selector itself was always right, which is why the
  new check drives the preview through `_on_slot_hover_changed` as well as calling the
  selector directly — only the second path ever went wrong.

## Reel icons

- The seven faces are white silhouettes and the reel strip is near-white, so in motion
  they read as one smudge. Each face now carries a shader that draws a black ring from
  the silhouette's own alpha and fills it from a `fill_color` uniform: Root brown, Spore
  gold, Bloom pink, Bramble purple, Pruning teal, Clover green, Bust red.
- The colour is a uniform rather than `modulate` because modulating the node would tint
  the outline along with the shape and put the reading problem straight back.
- Two things cost time here and are worth writing down. Passing the built-in `TEXTURE`
  into a helper function does not work on the compatibility renderer, so the eight taps
  are inlined. And the final line was `COLOR = vec4(rgb, alpha) * COLOR`, which looked
  harmless: on entry `COLOR` already holds `texture(TEXTURE, UV) * MODULATE`, so it
  re-applied the silhouette's own alpha and erased the ring everywhere the ring was meant
  to exist — which is precisely outside the silhouette. The outline was correct and
  invisible for three renders.

## One thing I did not touch

- `performance_probe` fails roughly one run in three with a peak of 16.5 to
  17.4ms against a 16.67ms target. It does the same with the wager layer stripped out of
  the scene, so it is the threshold sitting on top of this machine's noise rather than
  anything added today. Average is steady at ~7.0ms.
