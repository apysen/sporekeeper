# 2026-09-22

The pair sweep the rework left stale, and the first half of the relic bill it opened.
Both were on yesterday's "Not done" list and both are answered, one of them in the
opposite direction to how it was written down.

The recurring shape of the day is the same one as yesterday's: **the instrument could
not state the thing, so the thing read as broken.** Yesterday it was a board that
reported every territory Adaptation as absent. Today it is a bench with no hands, which
has never once pressed the only button Pruning has.

## The rework worked, and the pair sweep says so twice

Every balance figure in the sweep documents predated the Pruning rework, and Pruning+Vine
had been the game's top pair. Re-run on `--coverage 30`, both boards, rank 3, 40s:

| | rooted | walking | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bramble+Pruning | 5,985 | 9,134 | **+53%** |
| Spore+Pruning | 6,642 | 8,148 | +23% |
| Pruning+Thistle | 9,534 | 11,301 | +19% |
| Pruning+Fruit | 8,361 | 9,884 | +18% |
| Root+Pruning | 6,446 | 7,576 | +18% |
| Bloom+Pruning | 6,739 | 7,857 | +17% |
| Pruning+Vine | 11,137 | 10,469 | -6% |
| Root+Vine | 12,572 | 9,856 | **-22%** |
| Bloom+Vine | 11,060 | 8,848 | -20% |

Two readings, and they are the two questions that were open.

**Pruning+Vine is no longer the top pair** — third rooted, fourth walking, behind
Root+Vine and Thistle+Vine on one board and Thistle+Vine and Pruning+Thistle on the
other. That was the point of the rework and it is the first measurement that could say
whether it landed.

**Every Pruning pair gains from walking and nearly everything else loses.** "The ring is
maintained by walking it onto fresh Green, not by parking on a good spot" has been in the
file since before the ring existed. It is now a property of the table. The `mature`
column agrees from the other direction: Pruning pairs sit at 76-80 against everyone
else's 85-91, so the ground cost is real and is paid in maturity rather than in coverage.

Vine is still in seven of the top eight rooted, which is yesterday's finding untouched —
it is the only Adaptation that buffs the universal weapon. Walking softens it to four of
eight. It remains the standing balance question and nothing today addressed it.

produced, walking on a 30% board:

was built and trusted. `--discharge` spends the ring the moment it fills — the honest

Five of the six came back. Skimming Cut and Watersprouts are the *two best relics in the

## What was actually dead, and what was only unmeasurable

Three still read zero, and they are three different things.

**Callus is untestable on this bench, not dead.** Its window is 8 seconds and the bench's
walk is a circle at 0.55 rad/s, so a lap takes 11.4 — it cannot return to the wound before
the window shuts. Byte-identical on both boards for a reason that is entirely the
instrument's. Noted on the flag; not touched.

**Green Manure has no headroom, not no effect.** It speeds reclamation, and a 30% board
grows to 96% Green by the end of a bench whatever is holding it, so there is nothing for
faster reclamation to buy. On `--siege`, where the board is contested and ends at 16%, it
measures **+2.7%**. Working as written; the 30% board is the wrong place to read it.

**Gleaning Hook was really diminished, and by the rework.** It banked Charge, and Charge's
only consumer is the payout multiplier on a discharge — the one currency the rework
demoted. It measured -0.6%: the tightest zero in the pool and the only Pruning relic to
come in *below* holding none.

Re-expressed against the ring, per the pattern Deadfall set — and the first attempt was
wrong in a way worth recording, because it is the same wrongness a third time.

Its sentence is "grants Charge without cutting more Green", and what that sentence is
about is the second half: the ring paid in blood instead of in ground. So the first
version bought **leaves** — every 8 held-ring kills raises one, capped at 3 a hold, paid
through `_cutting_debt` so the cadence throttle and the capacity still governed how they
arrived. Measured: **7,761. Byte-identical to holding no relic**, to the digit, on the
same board that had just shown five other relics coming alive.

A walking ring runs at or near capacity, so adding to the harvest's debt adds to a queue
already longer than the shed cadence can spend. That is `orbit_capacity` raising its own
denominator, and `Harvest Rate` inert against a rigged board, and this, three times on one
path: **a lever that pays into a thing already full is worth nothing, however faithful it
is to the words.** Leaves are not the scarce thing.

Ground is, since the scarcity fix, and ground is exactly what the relic's own sentence
says it saves. So a payout cancels harvest debt instead — every 8 held-ring kills spares 4
cells the harvest would have taken, capped at 12 a hold, subtracted before the cut rather
than repaid after and never below zero. The cap is what stops a long hold running its ring
for free, which would take the ground cost out of the path.

Measured, walking, discharging: **7,956 on the 30% board (+2.5%, from -0.6%)** and
**19,702 on siege (+2.3%)**. Green comes in slightly *lower* on both, which is the trade
being legible rather than a defect: a ring that is cheaper to keep is a ring that runs
longer, and a ring that runs longer cuts more ground in total than one that starves.

## Two relics were not dead. They were paying for failure

Found while reading the pool, not while measuring it.

`_release_ring` branches on `intentional` for the leaves — that is the whole rework, a
torn ring drops what it holds and only a chosen discharge fires. **Brush Pile and Callus
were branching on `released`**, which counts the spikes that came off the ring, which a
tear does. So both paid out in full for running the ring dry. The exploit the rework spent
a day closing walked back in through the relic pool, for anyone holding either relic, and
no damage column would ever have shown it: the bench does not hold relics, and now that it
can, it reads them on a board where the tear is not the optimal play anyway.

Brush Pile's own wording settles it — "**release** to fire up to 8 extra Cuttings" — and a
ring that came apart was not released. Both now return before paying if the ring tore.

**Brush Pile was also accruing on a fraction of the cycles it should.** "Continued
harvesting stores bonus Cuttings" was counted at the bottom of `_harvest`, past the early
return that fires on every frame between sheds once the ring is full — which, since the
rework, is most of the time the harvest is actually cutting. Moved up beside
`_upkeep_cells_taken`, where the cutting is counted. Measured on the same fixture: 0
bundles before, 4 — the cap — after.

## Stubble belonged to the cut all along

"Pruned Ground stays sharp for 5s, damaging enemies that cross the wound." It planted its
wound at the moment of release: a relic about the ground Pruning cuts, waiting on an event
that in sustained play happens two or three times a minute. Planted by the harvest
instead, with every parameter it already had.

It is now the strongest relic in the pool and by some distance on a crowd: **+19.3%**
walking on a 30% board, **+55%** on `--siege`, against a pool whose next best is +25% and
+12% respectively. The cause is legible rather than mysterious — `stubble_maximum_wounds`
is 2, sized for wounds arriving two or three times a minute, and a harvest cycle plants
one every fraction of a second, so what follows her around is two permanently fresh damage
zones and a siege board puts sixty bodies through them.

**Answered below**, in "Two ceilings": it should read as a trail she leaves, and the
magnitude was a rate rather than a size.

## Espalier, Splinterstorm and Callus: none of them needed touching

Three names were on the bill. All three came off it without a line of gameplay code,
and the only thing that changed to get them there was the instrument.

**Callus is alive.** It needed `--discharge`, and then it needed the walk to fit inside
its own window: `--walk-lap` sets the lap in seconds rather than deriving it from a fixed
0.55 rad/s, because a lap is a *measurement window* for anything that depends on coming
back to a place. On `--siege` with a 6-second lap it holds **19.17% of the arena against
17.89%** with no relic, and pushes Rot back to -9.51 against -11.18. Damage is 1.6%
*lower*, which is what a reclamation relic should look like in a damage column.

**Espalier is a shape, not a defect.** It reads -5% at rank 1 and -8% at rank 5 on a
22-Hunter board, which is what sent it to the bill. Against a single tough target it is
**+35%** -- 6,338 applied against 4,680, eight Burrowers felled against six, 3.35s under
fire each against 4.39s. A discharging ring bursts outward and each spike keeps its own
heading, so the ring opens into a wall of blades in every direction; Espalier trades that
for a 90-degree fan. On a crowd that wastes what the burst was spreading. On one body
there is nothing to spread *to*, and concentration is the whole point.

Two fixes were written for it before that measurement and both are reverted. The first
paid the fan in damage, and the bench refused it in the clearest terms available: damage
**applied** did not move by a single point at either rank while damage **dealt** rose by
seven thousand -- every extra point was overkill, because a fan aimed at one sector is
several spikes arriving at the same enemy. The second paid it in pierce, and applied
damage came back byte-identical a third time, which is what finally said the launched
volley is too small a share of this board to read at all. The board was wrong, not the
evolution.

**Splinterstorm is the opposite of inert.** Yesterday's note guessed it multiplied a thorn
yield into a capacity-capped ring, the same inert-by-construction shape as Harvest Rate
before the scarcity fix. Bramble+Pruning, walking, discharging, on a 30% board:

| | applied | vs unevolved | taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| no evolution | 6,976 | — | 50 |
| Deadfall rank 1 | 11,084 | **+59%** | 44 |
| rank 4 | 11,990 | +72% | 28 |
| rank 5 | **16,598** | **+138%** | **0** |

The guess was wrong because Deadfall takes the leaf throttle off entirely -- `allowance =
room` -- so the extra yield converts the moment it is banked instead of queueing behind a
cadence. Nothing to adapt.

What it raises instead is the other question: **rank 5 is the strongest thing measured in
two days**, more than double its own unevolved pair and comfortably past the best row in
either pair sweep, while taking zero damage. Answered below, in "Two ceilings".

## The pattern, stated once

Four levers were rewritten or nearly rewritten today for problems they did not have:
Skimming Cut and Watersprouts (the bench never discharged), Callus (the lap was longer
than the window), Espalier (the board had nothing to concentrate on), and Splinterstorm
(the throttle it was supposed to be capped by is the thing its own evolution removes).

Every one of them presented identically -- a row indistinguishable from not owning the
thing. **On this project a zero is a claim about the instrument until proven otherwise**,
and the proof is always a second board, never a closer reading of the first.

## Two ceilings, brought down

Both numbers the day had flagged for a decision got one: **nothing in the run should be
strong enough that the run stops being played.** Each was decomposed before it was tuned,
and in both cases the magnitude turned out to be a symptom of a rate, not of a size.

### Deadfall's volley was on the harvest's clock

`_fire_deadfall_volley` said it outright in its own first line: "every cycle throws a ring
of heavy spikes outward". A harvest cycle runs several times a second on fresh Green, and
faster again with `Harvest Rate` bought -- so rank 5 was eight heavy spikes at 1.6x with
four pierce, several times a second, costing neither ring nor ground, at a rate an upgrade
line sells more of. That is a multiplicative loop with nothing on the other side of it,
and it measured +138% over its own unevolved pair while taking **zero** damage across a
whole bench.

The size was never the problem -- the volley is the evolution's identity. The rate was.
`splinterstorm_volley_interval_seconds` gives it a clock of its own, because Deadfall's
contract is the *ring* firing continuously and a second weapon on top of that does not
also inherit the harvest's cadence.

| interval | applied | vs unevolved | vs rank 4 | taken |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| every cycle | 16,598 | +138% | +38% | 0 |
| **2.0s** | **14,341** | **+106%** | **+20%** | 17 |
| 3.0s | 12,692 | +82% | +6% | 28 |

Left at 2.0. Three seconds makes the capstone rank worth six per cent over the rank below
it, which is not a capstone; two keeps it the strongest thing on the board and stops it
being twice the field.

### Stubble was an aura wearing a relic's words

"Pruned Ground stays sharp." The harvest happens *under her*, several times a second, so
two wounds planted at her own position were re-planted on top of her before either could
be left behind -- a permanent 90px damage field that followed her around, and on a siege
board sixty bodies walked through it. +55%, against a pool whose next best was +12%.

Sharp ground is something you leave behind, so a wound now has to be on ground the last
one is not: one per patch, spaced by its own radius, and standing still refreshes nothing.
`stubble_maximum_wounds` came down 2 -> 1 with it, which is the trail rather than the
carpet.

| | siege | 30% board |
|---|---|---|
| planted at release (before) | — | +13.2% |
| planted by the cut, 2 wounds, unspaced | +55% | +19.3% |
| spaced by radius, 2 wounds | +33% | — |
| **spaced, 1 wound** | **+25.5%** | **+13.1%** |

Which puts it beside Skimming Cut's +25.4% and +12.5% rather than four times past it.
Damage taken went from 127 to 341 on siege in the process: the aura had been a defensive
item as well, and nobody had asked it to be one.

## Vine gives facing back, and trades its damage line for width

Two changes, and they turned out to be one.

**Vine no longer aims itself.** `set_cane_auto_targeting(false)`: the sweep follows where
Grandma is looking, exactly as the bare Cane does. Vine was the only Adaptation that both
improved the weapon every build already carries *and* pointed it -- least asked of the
player, most given -- and that combination is what made the Cane-buffing read as a leak
rather than as a design. Facing-based, Vine's identity is clean: your Cane, grown, and
yours to aim.

It will not move the pair table much on its own and was never going to. The 09-21
decomposition is unambiguous about where the dominance lives -- Vine owned with no lines
is +12.6% on a crowd and 0.0% against a boss, while all four lines together are +89% --
so auto-aim was a slice of the small term.

**Lignification is now Fanning.** That slot held a flat damage multiplier on the Cane,
which is the purest possible expression of the actual problem: `set_cane_modifiers` is
where every Vine line lands, so Vine multiplied the weapon every other build is also
swinging. Width is the honest replacement rather than a smaller damage number, because it
buys the sweep's *shape* instead of its output and therefore cannot multiply anyone else's
Cane.

And it is the same change as the first one. A wider arc is the forgiveness stat -- the
thing auto-aim used to hand over for free, now sold back as something the player chooses
to buy. Neither change makes much sense without the other: facing without width is a
straight nerf to feel, and width without facing buys nothing anyone would notice.

Vine's four lines are now reach, arc, cadence, and damage *conditional on hitting at the
tip*. Two geometry, one tempo, one conditional output, and no unconditional multiplier on
the universal weapon. `MAXIMUM_CANE_ARC_MULTIPLIER` caps it at 2.0 -- a half-circle in
front of her, which is as wide as a facing can mean anything.

The enum member was renamed in place rather than appended, because a save stores an
upgrade's index and this slot keeps its own.

### Three tests moved with it

All three asserted the old contract, and all three were rewritten as positive statements
of the new one rather than as inversions:

- "Vine auto-targets the densest valid sector" became "Vine follows facing like the Cane,
  and Fanning is what reaches the flank" -- one body dead ahead that any sweep reaches, one
  at 55 degrees that sits outside the authored arc and inside the widened one.
- The Clover Vine jackpot asserted `_cane_auto_targets`. Its actual claim is that the Vine
  is *running* the Cane, so it reads `is_vine_driven()` now.
- The Root probe checked Lignification's damage against Root's Cane floor. With no Vine
  damage line left, the floor is the only thing moving Cane damage, which is a cleaner
  statement of the same contract.

`cane_targeting_reference_probe` still passes on all 1,760 cases: the densest-sector
algorithm is untouched, it simply has no caller any more. Turning auto-aim back on is one
line, and the branches that read the flag are the only description the codebase has of
what it would mean.

## Not done

- **An aggressive run reaches Wave 20 in under a minute, and then nothing happens.**
  Measured with `pacing_probe --provoke aggressive` once Provocation lost its limits, at
  one press per wave: nineteen presses, Blight XIX, final wave inside sixty seconds. The
  build keeps up better than expected (level 24 and 23 of 36 ranks by minute two, because
  each press dumps the skipped wave in front of you and a hundred enemies in one place is
  a lot of XP), but Green hits 0.0% at minute two and never comes back.

  Two things left unchased, deliberately, rather than half-chased:
  the run *flatlines* from minute six (488 kills, 4 alive, 229,049 damage, identical for
  fourteen minutes) with four enemies that never die; and 0.0% Green should be a defeat
  condition and was not. Part of the first is the probe, whose loop runs a fixed twenty
  minutes and never reads `RunState` -- but that does not explain four survivors against a
  finished build. **The structure has no answer for a run that outruns it**, which is a
  different problem from difficulty and wants its own session.

- **`get_upkeep_cells_spared` is uncounted by the bench.** Gleaning Hook's whole effect is
  a subtraction from a cost, and the columns print the cost's *result*. The director
  counts it now; nothing reads it.
- **`--relics` cannot reach an Evolution.** Espalier and Splinterstorm were measured by
  hand, one `--evo-rank` at a time. A sweep over an Adaptation's evolutions would have made
  the Splinterstorm table a single command.
- **The discharge policy is one policy.** Spending at a full ring is the loop's own
  intent, and it is not the only thing a player does — holding for a moment, or firing
  early into a pack, are both real and both unmeasured. Every relic figure above is one
  sample of one policy, on top of the `--seed` caveat that a reading is one sample of one
  configuration.
- **The pair sweep is stale again**, by one day and by my own hand: every figure in it
  predates the Vine change. Re-running it is the only thing that says whether trading a
  damage line for width moved the outlier or merely moved Vine down.
- Carried, untouched: whether Vine's four lines
  anti-stack against a lone walking target, the Bramble hole under a boss, `--boss queen`
  and `--boss elite` unrun, and that **nobody has played any of this**.
