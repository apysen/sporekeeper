# Changelog: 2026-08-25

## The clippings were coming off the wrong side of the player

Reported from play: Pruning was cutting territory above the Gardener rather than
underneath. It reads as a coordinate-space bug and it is not one — every call in
the Pruning director already goes through `get_arena_position()`, and every one of
them was correct.

`drain_territory_in_radius` walks the bounding box of its disc row by row and stops
the instant it has its quota. That is fine when the quota is the whole disc, which
is what `prune_territory_in_radius` asks for. The continuous harvest asks for a few
cells per step, so the scan never got past the top rows of the box: the cut opened
in a band above the player and worked its way down over several seconds.

- Candidates are now gathered first and taken nearest-first, ties on cell index so
  a player standing still gets the same cut every step rather than a flicker between
  two equidistant cells. The band ordering is untouched: which ground is *eligible*
  is still youngest-first, and distance only decides which of the eligible ground
  actually goes.
- That immediately broke four ring checks, and the reason is the interesting part.
  The harvest is gated on standing on your own Green, so eating the cell directly
  underfoot switches off the thing that is running it — one step of harvesting and
  the ring stops filling. `upkeep_inner_radius_pixels` (7.0) spares the player's own
  footing, and the hole opens as a ring around them instead of beneath them. It is
  also the more legible version: a hole exactly underneath is hidden by the Gardener
  standing on it.
- Measured on the starting island at a six-cell quota: 2 cells taken above the
  player, 2 below, furthest 12.1px against a 12px radius.

## Root grows visible roots now

Every run has the metabolism's vessel network. Only a run that took the Root path
grows a mat of fine hairs into the ground it is holding.

- `TerritoryField.sample_rooted_ground` strides the board for owned cells past the
  Established threshold and returns each with its own maturity, on the same slow
  clock the vessels already rebuild on. Root hairs are a broad texture, not a
  per-cell readout, and scanning the whole field every frame to draw a few dozen
  tufts would cost the budget for nothing.
- The overlay draws tapering hairs radiating from each anchor, underneath
  everything else including the vessels — the vessels run *through* the mat, so the
  mat goes down first. Hairs are seeded off their anchor's own position, because a
  mat that reshuffles itself between frames reads as static rather than as roots.
- Density and alpha follow both the cell's maturity and `get_path_rank_fraction`,
  which is 0.25 the moment the path is taken and 1.0 with its lines maxed. Never
  zero for a path the run owns: taking Root is itself worth showing, and a fraction
  starting at nothing would hide it until the first draft landed. Read from owned
  ranks rather than borrowed ones, so a Clover jackpot lending Root for ten seconds
  does not repaint the ground as though the run had bought it.
- Measured: 72 anchors on the starting island, strength 0.25 on taking the path and
  0.44 with one line maxed.

## Root Metabolism is the economy now, and the shop is gone

The master doc's Phase 0 kept two economies behind a flag so they could be compared.
The comparison is over. `root_economy_enabled` and `purchase_system_enabled` are
both deleted, along with the surface they were guarding.

What went: Photosynthesis / Meristem / Cambium as things you buy, their unlock
schedule, their exponential price curve, the Breakthrough forks, and the automatic
ladder fed by lifetime earnings that had already replaced the shopping once. Two
economies were tried on top of those three axes and both failed the same way — a
shop where the player clicks whichever number looks useful is a solved order wearing
the costume of a decision, and an automatic ladder is a bar filling itself.

What stayed is the doc's own answer: the player develops **physiology** and the
simulation reads **physical parameters**. So Mass, Push and Hold survive and stop
being a purchase surface. Each is read from the developmental domain that governs
it — Storage feeds Mass, Circulation feeds Push, Anchoring feeds Hold — plus one
rank for every slice of the board the organism has actually rooted. That second
half cannot be bought at all, which is what finally makes the economy follow the
simulation rather than decorate it: a large healthy organism is physically more
massive, pushes harder and holds together better because it is made of more of
itself.

A rank arriving on its own still answers on the battlefield the way a bought one
used to — material on Mass, a shove on Push, a knitting-together on Hold. That
requirement never went away when the shopping did; it just stopped having a button
to hang off.

## Four domains, and each one hands the run a verb

The three prototype developments did their job: expansion, banking and active
control turned out to be three strategies a player would choose between. What they
were not is physiology — three unrelated multipliers with no architecture under
them. They are replaced by the doc's four developmental domains.

- **Rooting** — held ground establishes faster. Unlocks **Deep Root**: drive the
  reserve into the ground around you and everything nearby establishes at once
  instead of over the next minute.
- **Storage** — the network banks more before it overflows. Unlocks **Pressurize**:
  empty the reserve as one shove and nothing else. It creates no Plant material at
  all, so the same Biomass has to be worth more as motion, or the action is a worse
  Overgrow with a longer cooldown.
- **Circulation** — your influence carries further. Unlocks **Translocate**, the one
  exception to a severed colony's Biomass being unreachable. It has to cost
  something or the rule it breaks was never a rule: 65% survives the journey through
  ground the organism does not hold and the rest is gone.
- **Anchoring** — established Green resists erosion and tearing. Unlocks
  **Regenerate**: pure Cohesion, which the field already spends on whatever is
  damaged rather than whatever is furthest forward.

Overgrow is unchanged and never gated — it is the run's first metabolic discharge
and every run has it. All five share one aim flow, one debit path and one cooldown
table, so a sixth verb is an enum entry rather than a fifth copy of the same code.
Pressing a different action while one is already aiming switches the aim rather
than eating the click.

Anchoring is the one domain whose effect is a refusal rather than a growth, so it
cannot be announced by making something bigger. Its rank strikes every component's
heart at once, which reads as the tissue setting rather than spreading.

## Metamorphosis replaces Breakthrough

Layer 7 of the unfolding schedule, and the last thing the economy reveals. Reaching
rank 3 in a domain offers a permanent architectural fork, once, and holds the drawer
until it is answered — buying past an unanswered question would answer it by
accident.

Every old Breakthrough effect found a home rather than being deleted, which is why
the simulation still has all of them:

- **Storage** — Central Taproot concentrates the reserve into one structure (×1.75
  primary capacity, ×1.40 discharge) and leaves a severed colony 35% of what it
  would have held. Distributed Nodules does the reverse. Their pulse behaviour is
  the old Storage Organs and Rapid Cycling: one gathers and releases in a breath,
  the other trickles constantly.
- **Circulation** — Cardiac Pumping withholds the player's support and delivers it
  as periodic surges at 1.65× reach, and takes the old Apical Dominance focus, so a
  pumped release also concentrates into tendrils. Continuous Peristalsis flows
  without pause, carries 22% more, and spreads a release the way Lateral Growth did.
  A pumped circulation that looked identical to a continuous one would be two names
  for one mechanic, so the beat is visible on the battlefield when it lands.
- **Anchoring** — Fibrous Mat is the old Flexible Tissue: a wide shallow organism
  that deforms heavily and springs back, and keeps torn scraps of Green alive far
  longer. Deep Taproots is Woody Tissue: mature ground becomes almost impossible to
  uproot, and young Green is left exactly as fragile as it was.
- **Rooting** — the genuinely new pair. Colonial Roots turns part of a cut-off
  colony's own production into its own establishment, so severed ground survives
  alone. Aggressive Branching roots newly taken frontier 55% faster and gives
  anything cut off nothing.

Neither half of a pair is the better one. Each buys a strength by accepting the
weakness the other does not have, which is the only way a permanent irreversible
choice is a decision rather than a correct answer.

## A late enemy is not an early enemy with more health

Three archetypes from the design notes, none of which had ever been built.

- **Heavy Grazer** — large, slow, eats a lot of ground, and shrugs off a shove.
  `knockback_resistance` is deliberately separate from slowing: the note says it is
  still vulnerable to being slowed and held, and one number governing both would
  make it immune to Bramble rather than immune to being moved. Measured: it takes
  under a fifth of the shove that moves an ordinary Grazer.
- **Rusher** — a fast Hunter. It needed no resistance stat at all. Entanglement
  already ramps with time spent on Green, so something crossing the frontier quickly
  is barely touched by passive Bramble control; the counter is emergent rather than
  authored.
- **Siege Corruptor** — conversion that compounds for as long as it is ignored, to a
  ceiling of six times its base, and one hit resets it to nothing. That makes "if
  ignored" the actual condition rather than a timer.

## Elites that change the rules

Six modifiers, one per elite and never stacked — two rule changes on one body is a
fight the player cannot read.

Unstoppable breaks holds and shoves and stays fully slowable. Blighted sours its
wake at intervals along its path, so it leaves a trail with gaps rather than a smear
whose width is the frame rate. Ravenous shares the Siege Corruptor's escalation, so
"if ignored" is one mechanic with two owners rather than two mechanics that look
alike. Regenerative heals unless the *protagonist* hit it — thorns, Blooms and the
ring keep working, they just do not stop it healing, which is what makes it a change
to a build rather than a larger health pool. Disruptive suppresses the maturity of
the Green around it, and since everything the run reads off its territory is read
off maturity, it weakens Green-territory effects without needing to know what any of
them are. Volatile detonates on death, damaging nearby enemies and the ground it
died on, sourced as RELIC so a kill it causes cannot feed a relic trigger and start a
chain the player cannot see the end of.

The roll is gated behind the run's own escalation rather than the wave number: zero
until minute five, then climbing to 85% at the final invasion. Before that a
modifier is an unexplained stat line, because the player has no build for it to be a
change *to*. Late specialists become eligible alongside the Elite; the body of a
pack never does. Measured at full pressure: 35 of 40 rolls carried one.

## The presentation settings exist

Seven options from the UI guide, none of which had been built, all wired to real
consumers rather than to a stub.

The central rule is that these are not one global opacity dial. Friendly Effects and
Enemy Telegraphs are separate on purpose: a player who turns their own spectacle
down to see the fight must not thereby turn down the thing that tells them the fight
is about to hit them. Both meet in exactly one place — the pulse loop reads the
telegraph setting for a hostile pulse and the friendly setting for a friendly one,
so the same drawing code serves both and neither can quieten the other. High
Contrast raises telegraphs *above* normal rather than merely declining to lower
them, because the option exists to make danger louder.

Territory Detail is cosmetic only, and the check for it is that dropping to Low
moves no field parameter at all. Damage Numbers gets an Important Only tier, where
important means a hit on an elite or a boss, anything carrying a modifier, or a hit
large enough to be an ability landing rather than a tick. Player Highlight defaults
to Outline rather than Off — by the time splatter, vines and an orbiting ring are
all on screen, an outline is the difference between playing and hunting for
yourself. UI Scale scales the interface layer and deliberately not the battlefield.
HUD Detail reveals more of the line Compact already had rather than switching to a
second design.

They live in a second column of the pause panel, one button per row, because every
one of them is a short list of authored presets and a preset list wants a control
that names its current value.

## The gambling guide's remaining four

- **First matching-pair cinematic.** The once-per-run pause and focus now exist.
  The battlefield is held still and the machine is not — everything else advances
  from `advance_run`, which the focus gates, and the machine is advanced by the
  focus itself, so the reels keep turning inside the pause. Its duration is read
  from the machine rather than authored a second time, because a rewind extends the
  third reel by most of a second and a cinematic that ended before the reel it is
  focused on would be the worst possible version of this. Later pairs lean the panel
  in at 1.12× without pausing anything.
- **The rewind Easter egg animates.** The backend already rolled it and reserved its
  timing; what was missing was the reel actually reversing. The winning face crosses
  the payline on an eased-out pass, hangs there long enough to read as a miss, then
  is dragged back on an eased-in snap that reads as a mechanical lock. The face on
  the strip throughout is the one the machine rolled before the lever finished
  moving, so the fakeout cannot change what was already decided — it is an animation,
  never a rig.
- **Bloom jackpot anchors find the crowd.** Spore is planting Seeds, which want
  ground; a Bloom is an explosion, and an explosion placed on empty frontier is a
  firework. Anchors now go on the thickest knots of enemies, scored the same way the
  Man-Eater picks its meal, spaced apart so a big win is spread over the fight rather
  than dropped four times onto one mob, and falling back to the frontier when there
  is nothing to aim at.
- **Telemetry**, one row per spin in the guide's own order, plus both debug views on
  F2 and F3. It records and does not judge: nothing it holds is read by the machine,
  because an instrument that fed back into the thing it measures would stop measuring
  it. Off by default — a shipped run should not pay for a dictionary per spin to
  answer questions nobody is asking. The odds it logs are read from the same call the
  readout prints, so what is logged is what was shown and what was rolled rather than
  a third opinion.

## Fragment survival is nucleation now

The first of the physics proposals, and the one the doc ranks highest because it
deletes an authored number rather than adding one.

A patch of Green under `small_fragment_cell_limit` cells used to decay at a flat
rate. Both the threshold and the rate were numbers we picked. They are replaced by
classical nucleation: a new phase forming inside another pays surface energy and
gains bulk energy, and below the critical radius `r* = 2γ / Δg` the surface term
wins and the nucleus dissolves.

Cohesion is γ. A cohesive ecosystem raises the cost of having a border at all, so it
throws fewer surviving satellites and holds its main body harder — a genuine trade
rather than two upgrades that both say "better". Mass is Δg and lowers r*, so a Mass
build's scatter literally starts taking root, which is the fantasy that axis has
always been selling and only ever approximated. Maturity and what the patch landed
next to both raise Δg, so a satellite that lands beside the organism survives at a
size that would dissolve on open ground.

The support term costs one check per cell. The flood is four-connected, so Green
touching a patch only diagonally is a different component sitting physically against
it — which is exactly what "landed next to friendly Green" means. Decay is now
proportional to how far below r* the region is: a uniform rate made a one-cell scrap
and an almost-viable colony die at the same speed.

## Kudzu and Carnivory are out of the docs

Both were dead spec. Kudzu was replaced by Carnivory, Carnivory was collapsed into
the Man-Eater relic, and the documents still described both as current work.

`new_changes_0821.md` loses its five Kudzu sections and its five Kudzu Evolution
Upgrades; `new_changes_0822.md` loses its five Carnivory sections and the two
implementation phases that served them. Both are renumbered rather than left with

Every surviving reference is to the Man-Eater, which is a real Legendary relic in a
real catalog, or to *why* Carnivory was retired — which is the rule the design now
follows and is load-bearing for the Root promotion that this whole rework rests on.
Those stay. The code comment on `phase_b_man_eater.gd` explaining what the file used
to be also stays: it is accurate history of why that file looks the way it does.

  metamorphosis trades, the elite modifier rates and the new archetypes' stat lines

  numbers most likely to be wrong are the Anchoring multipliers and the nucleation
  surface tension, because both now reach every border in the field.
