# Changelog: 2026-08-25

## The clippings were coming off the wrong side of the player

Reported from play: Pruning was cutting territory above the Gardener rather than
underneath. It reads as a coordinate-space bug and it is not one — every call in
the Pruning director already goes through `get_arena_position()`, and every one of
them was correct.

`drain_territory_in_radius` walks the bounding box of its disc row by row and stops
the instant it has its quota. That is fine when the quota is the whole disc, which
is what `prune_territory_in_radius` asks for. The continuous harvest asks for a few
cells per step, so the scan never got past the top rows of the box: the cut opened
in a band above the player and worked its way down over several seconds.

- Candidates are now gathered first and taken nearest-first, ties on cell index so
  a player standing still gets the same cut every step rather than a flicker between
  two equidistant cells. The band ordering is untouched: which ground is *eligible*
  is still youngest-first, and distance only decides which of the eligible ground
  actually goes.
- That immediately broke four ring checks, and the reason is the interesting part.
  The harvest is gated on standing on your own Green, so eating the cell directly
  underfoot switches off the thing that is running it — one step of harvesting and
  the ring stops filling. `upkeep_inner_radius_pixels` (7.0) spares the player's own
  footing, and the hole opens as a ring around them instead of beneath them. It is
  also the more legible version: a hole exactly underneath is hidden by the Gardener
  standing on it.
- Measured on the starting island at a six-cell quota: 2 cells taken above the
  player, 2 below, furthest 12.1px against a 12px radius.

## Root grows visible roots now

Every run has the metabolism's vessel network. Only a run that took the Root path
grows a mat of fine hairs into the ground it is holding.

- `TerritoryField.sample_rooted_ground` strides the board for owned cells past the
  Established threshold and returns each with its own maturity, on the same slow
  clock the vessels already rebuild on. Root hairs are a broad texture, not a
  per-cell readout, and scanning the whole field every frame to draw a few dozen
  tufts would cost the budget for nothing.
- The overlay draws tapering hairs radiating from each anchor, underneath
  everything else including the vessels — the vessels run *through* the mat, so the
  mat goes down first. Hairs are seeded off their anchor's own position, because a
  mat that reshuffles itself between frames reads as static rather than as roots.
- Density and alpha follow both the cell's maturity and `get_path_rank_fraction`,
  which is 0.25 the moment the path is taken and 1.0 with its lines maxed. Never
  zero for a path the run owns: taking Root is itself worth showing, and a fraction
  starting at nothing would hide it until the first draft landed. Read from owned
  ranks rather than borrowed ones, so a Clover jackpot lending Root for ten seconds
  does not repaint the ground as though the run had bought it.
- Measured: 72 anchors on the starting island, strength 0.25 on taking the path and
  0.44 with one line maxed.

## Root Metabolism is the economy now, and the shop is gone

The master doc's Phase 0 kept two economies behind a flag so they could be compared.
The comparison is over. `root_economy_enabled` and `purchase_system_enabled` are
both deleted, along with the surface they were guarding.

What went: Photosynthesis / Meristem / Cambium as things you buy, their unlock
schedule, their exponential price curve, the Breakthrough forks, and the automatic
ladder fed by lifetime earnings that had already replaced the shopping once. Two
economies were tried on top of those three axes and both failed the same way — a
shop where the player clicks whichever number looks useful is a solved order wearing
the costume of a decision, and an automatic ladder is a bar filling itself.

What stayed is the doc's own answer: the player develops **physiology** and the
simulation reads **physical parameters**. So Mass, Push and Hold survive and stop
being a purchase surface. Each is read from the developmental domain that governs
it — Storage feeds Mass, Circulation feeds Push, Anchoring feeds Hold — plus one
rank for every slice of the board the organism has actually rooted. That second
half cannot be bought at all, which is what finally makes the economy follow the
simulation rather than decorate it: a large healthy organism is physically more
massive, pushes harder and holds together better because it is made of more of
itself.

A rank arriving on its own still answers on the battlefield the way a bought one
used to — material on Mass, a shove on Push, a knitting-together on Hold. That
requirement never went away when the shopping did; it just stopped having a button
to hang off.

## Four domains, and each one hands the run a verb

The three prototype developments did their job: expansion, banking and active
control turned out to be three strategies a player would choose between. What they
were not is physiology — three unrelated multipliers with no architecture under
them. They are replaced by the doc's four developmental domains.

- **Rooting** — held ground establishes faster. Unlocks **Deep Root**: drive the
  reserve into the ground around you and everything nearby establishes at once
  instead of over the next minute.
- **Storage** — the network banks more before it overflows. Unlocks **Pressurize**:
  empty the reserve as one shove and nothing else. It creates no Plant material at
  all, so the same Biomass has to be worth more as motion, or the action is a worse
  Overgrow with a longer cooldown.
- **Circulation** — your influence carries further. Unlocks **Translocate**, the one
  exception to a severed colony's Biomass being unreachable. It has to cost
  something or the rule it breaks was never a rule: 65% survives the journey through
  ground the organism does not hold and the rest is gone.
- **Anchoring** — established Green resists erosion and tearing. Unlocks
  **Regenerate**: pure Cohesion, which the field already spends on whatever is
  damaged rather than whatever is furthest forward.

Overgrow is unchanged and never gated — it is the run's first metabolic discharge
and every run has it. All five share one aim flow, one debit path and one cooldown
table, so a sixth verb is an enum entry rather than a fifth copy of the same code.
Pressing a different action while one is already aiming switches the aim rather
than eating the click.

Anchoring is the one domain whose effect is a refusal rather than a growth, so it
cannot be announced by making something bigger. Its rank strikes every component's
heart at once, which reads as the tissue setting rather than spreading.

## Metamorphosis replaces Breakthrough

Layer 7 of the unfolding schedule, and the last thing the economy reveals. Reaching
rank 3 in a domain offers a permanent architectural fork, once, and holds the drawer
until it is answered — buying past an unanswered question would answer it by
accident.

Every old Breakthrough effect found a home rather than being deleted, which is why
the simulation still has all of them:

- **Storage** — Central Taproot concentrates the reserve into one structure (×1.75
  primary capacity, ×1.40 discharge) and leaves a severed colony 35% of what it
  would have held. Distributed Nodules does the reverse. Their pulse behaviour is
  the old Storage Organs and Rapid Cycling: one gathers and releases in a breath,
  the other trickles constantly.
- **Circulation** — Cardiac Pumping withholds the player's support and delivers it
  as periodic surges at 1.65× reach, and takes the old Apical Dominance focus, so a
  pumped release also concentrates into tendrils. Continuous Peristalsis flows
  without pause, carries 22% more, and spreads a release the way Lateral Growth did.
  A pumped circulation that looked identical to a continuous one would be two names
  for one mechanic, so the beat is visible on the battlefield when it lands.
- **Anchoring** — Fibrous Mat is the old Flexible Tissue: a wide shallow organism
  that deforms heavily and springs back, and keeps torn scraps of Green alive far
  longer. Deep Taproots is Woody Tissue: mature ground becomes almost impossible to
  uproot, and young Green is left exactly as fragile as it was.
- **Rooting** — the genuinely new pair. Colonial Roots turns part of a cut-off
  colony's own production into its own establishment, so severed ground survives
  alone. Aggressive Branching roots newly taken frontier 55% faster and gives
  anything cut off nothing.

Neither half of a pair is the better one. Each buys a strength by accepting the
weakness the other does not have, which is the only way a permanent irreversible
choice is a decision rather than a correct answer.

## A late enemy is not an early enemy with more health

Three archetypes from the design notes, none of which had ever been built.

- **Heavy Grazer** — large, slow, eats a lot of ground, and shrugs off a shove.
  `knockback_resistance` is deliberately separate from slowing: the note says it is
  still vulnerable to being slowed and held, and one number governing both would
  make it immune to Bramble rather than immune to being moved. Measured: it takes
  under a fifth of the shove that moves an ordinary Grazer.
- **Rusher** — a fast Hunter. It needed no resistance stat at all. Entanglement
  already ramps with time spent on Green, so something crossing the frontier quickly
  is barely touched by passive Bramble control; the counter is emergent rather than
  authored.
- **Siege Corruptor** — conversion that compounds for as long as it is ignored, to a
  ceiling of six times its base, and one hit resets it to nothing. That makes "if
  ignored" the actual condition rather than a timer.

## Elites that change the rules

Six modifiers, one per elite and never stacked — two rule changes on one body is a
fight the player cannot read.

Unstoppable breaks holds and shoves and stays fully slowable. Blighted sours its
wake at intervals along its path, so it leaves a trail with gaps rather than a smear
whose width is the frame rate. Ravenous shares the Siege Corruptor's escalation, so
"if ignored" is one mechanic with two owners rather than two mechanics that look
alike. Regenerative heals unless the *protagonist* hit it — thorns, Blooms and the
ring keep working, they just do not stop it healing, which is what makes it a change
to a build rather than a larger health pool. Disruptive suppresses the maturity of
the Green around it, and since everything the run reads off its territory is read
off maturity, it weakens Green-territory effects without needing to know what any of
them are. Volatile detonates on death, damaging nearby enemies and the ground it
died on, sourced as RELIC so a kill it causes cannot feed a relic trigger and start a
chain the player cannot see the end of.

The roll is gated behind the run's own escalation rather than the wave number: zero
until minute five, then climbing to 85% at the final invasion. Before that a
modifier is an unexplained stat line, because the player has no build for it to be a
change *to*. Late specialists become eligible alongside the Elite; the body of a
pack never does. Measured at full pressure: 35 of 40 rolls carried one.

## The presentation settings exist

Seven options from the UI guide, none of which had been built, all wired to real
consumers rather than to a stub.

The central rule is that these are not one global opacity dial. Friendly Effects and
Enemy Telegraphs are separate on purpose: a player who turns their own spectacle
down to see the fight must not thereby turn down the thing that tells them the fight
is about to hit them. Both meet in exactly one place — the pulse loop reads the
telegraph setting for a hostile pulse and the friendly setting for a friendly one,
so the same drawing code serves both and neither can quieten the other. High
Contrast raises telegraphs *above* normal rather than merely declining to lower
them, because the option exists to make danger louder.

Territory Detail is cosmetic only, and the check for it is that dropping to Low
moves no field parameter at all. Damage Numbers gets an Important Only tier, where
important means a hit on an elite or a boss, anything carrying a modifier, or a hit
large enough to be an ability landing rather than a tick. Player Highlight defaults
to Outline rather than Off — by the time splatter, vines and an orbiting ring are
all on screen, an outline is the difference between playing and hunting for
yourself. UI Scale scales the interface layer and deliberately not the battlefield.
HUD Detail reveals more of the line Compact already had rather than switching to a
second design.

They live in a second column of the pause panel, one button per row, because every
one of them is a short list of authored presets and a preset list wants a control
that names its current value.

## The gambling guide's remaining four

- **First matching-pair cinematic.** The once-per-run pause and focus now exist.
  The battlefield is held still and the machine is not — everything else advances
  from `advance_run`, which the focus gates, and the machine is advanced by the
  focus itself, so the reels keep turning inside the pause. Its duration is read
  from the machine rather than authored a second time, because a rewind extends the
  third reel by most of a second and a cinematic that ended before the reel it is
  focused on would be the worst possible version of this. Later pairs lean the panel
  in at 1.12× without pausing anything.
- **The rewind Easter egg animates.** The backend already rolled it and reserved its
  timing; what was missing was the reel actually reversing. The winning face crosses
  the payline on an eased-out pass, hangs there long enough to read as a miss, then
  is dragged back on an eased-in snap that reads as a mechanical lock. The face on
  the strip throughout is the one the machine rolled before the lever finished
  moving, so the fakeout cannot change what was already decided — it is an animation,
  never a rig.
- **Bloom jackpot anchors find the crowd.** Spore is planting Seeds, which want
  ground; a Bloom is an explosion, and an explosion placed on empty frontier is a
  firework. Anchors now go on the thickest knots of enemies, scored the same way the
  Man-Eater picks its meal, spaced apart so a big win is spread over the fight rather
  than dropped four times onto one mob, and falling back to the frontier when there
  is nothing to aim at.
- **Telemetry**, one row per spin in the guide's own order, plus both debug views on
  F2 and F3. It records and does not judge: nothing it holds is read by the machine,

  a third opinion.

## Fragment survival is nucleation now

The first of the physics proposals, and the one the doc ranks highest because it
deletes an authored number rather than adding one.

A patch of Green under `small_fragment_cell_limit` cells used to decay at a flat
rate. Both the threshold and the rate were numbers we picked. They are replaced by
classical nucleation: a new phase forming inside another pays surface energy and
gains bulk energy, and below the critical radius `r* = 2γ / Δg` the surface term
wins and the nucleus dissolves.

Cohesion is γ. A cohesive ecosystem raises the cost of having a border at all, so it
throws fewer surviving satellites and holds its main body harder — a genuine trade
rather than two upgrades that both say "better". Mass is Δg and lowers r*, so a Mass
build's scatter literally starts taking root, which is the fantasy that axis has
always been selling and only ever approximated. Maturity and what the patch landed
next to both raise Δg, so a satellite that lands beside the organism survives at a
size that would dissolve on open ground.

The support term costs one check per cell. The flood is four-connected, so Green
touching a patch only diagonally is a different component sitting physically against
it — which is exactly what "landed next to friendly Green" means. Decay is now
proportional to how far below r* the region is: a uniform rate made a one-cell scrap
and an almost-viable colony die at the same speed.

## Green territory is painted

`docs/Green_Watercolor_Wash_Godot_Implementation.md`, implemented into the existing
TerritoryField material rather than beside it. The doc asks for a paint layer, a
runtime mask and a shader; the renderer was already all three - one CanvasItem over
the arena rect, one small state texture, cubic B-spline reconstruction - so what
changed was where the Green colour comes from, and nothing else.

- **The wash is sampled in field space**, never in the space of the shape it fills.
  It behaves as an infinite painted sheet under the arena that ownership stencils
  holes in: growing Green reveals more of a painting that never moves, losing Green
  hides it again, and a piece of ground shows the same pigment every time it changes
  hands. Measured: 0.067 maximum drift on ground that stayed Green while the organism
  grew around it, against a 0.25 gate.
- **Repetition is broken by sampling the one painting three times** - as painted,
  quarter-turned at 0.86, mirrored at 1.16 - weighted 66/22/12. Each layer is
  continuous across the whole field, so unlike per-tile random rotation this cannot
  introduce seams where previously matching edges meet.
- A non-square painting would be stretched into a square repeat, so each wash carries
  its own proportions and is read at its own scale.

The art needed work the shader cannot do. The vendor jpg is a single canvas, not a
tile: its wrap edges differ by 64 and 85 mean per-channel against about 2 for
adjacent interior columns, and it is lit light-to-dark across the canvas, so tiling it
steps brightness at every repeat. No amount of multi-sampling hides that - all three
samples are the same painting, so each one carries the same discontinuity, and the
rotated and mirrored layers add two more seam directions.

`scripts/make_green_wash_tile.py` fixes both in two passes: flatten the canvas-wide
lighting ramp by subtracting its low frequencies and restoring the average, then wrap
the tile by compositing it over a half-shifted copy of itself through a smoothstep
band - the shifted copy is continuous exactly where the original's wrap edges are, and
its own discontinuity lands in the middle where the original covers it. Result: wrap
1.56 against a 1.18 neighbouring-column control, and the largest quadrant deviation
from the tile average down from 10.7 to 1.1.

The vendor source was renamed `green_wash_v3.jpg` mid-day; the tile the game samples
is `green_wash_v3.png`, built from it by that script. `v1` and `v2` are the earlier
passes, left on disk.

## Rot is the same painting in another colour

Hostile ground got its own wash, on the same field coordinates as Green: two sheets
over one painted world, so ground taken and retaken shows the pigment that was always
there instead of being repainted every time the front crosses it.

The hand-made Rot wash was called flat and replaced by one derived from the Green
painting. `scripts/make_rot_wash_tile.py` recolours it as three per-channel HSV
lookup tables - hue recentred from 61 to the Rot hue of 235, saturation scaled 0.666
and value 0.431 to the Rot averages - which leaves every pixel's relationship to its
neighbours alone. Brushwork, grain and seam quality therefore cannot drift between the
two washes: whatever is true of the Green tile is true of this one, including that it
wraps. Scaling rather than replacing saturation and value is what keeps the brush
contrast proportional, and is the difference between a dark wash and a sheet of felt.

Being literally the same picture, the two would have shown identical pigment either
side of a front - one image recoloured down the middle. Rot is read at a fixed offset
into the sheet instead, which breaks the mirroring while keeping the property that
matters: a given patch of ground still shows the same Rot every time it is lost.

## The root economy is out, and the shop did not come back with it

Called on play: the root economy went, and so did having to buy anything out of the
drawer. What replaces it is the economy that stood immediately before it — Mass, Push
and Hold developing on their own out of the run's **lifetime** Biomass — not the
purchase shop that stood before that one.

None of it was recoverable from version control. The root rework shipped inside the
same commit as the Clover machine, and the flagged-off automatic ladder it replaced
(`root_economy_enabled`, `purchase_system_enabled`) was deleted before any of it was
committed, so this is a rebuild from `2026-08-23`'s notes and §2 of the gambling guide
rather than a revert. What survived in the code made it cheap: Progression still held
`biomass`, `biomass_earned_total`, `tick_biomass()` and `spend_biomass()`, and the
three axes and their battlefield effects had never left `PhaseBMetabolism`. Only the
source of their ranks had to change.

- **Lifetime earnings, never the balance.** An axis rank is the highest the run's share
  of lifetime Biomass has paid for, along the shop's own curve — 20, 35, 61, 107, 188,
  328, 574 — so the climb lands where buying everything in the best order would have.
  Spending the reserve to nothing, on an Overgrow or a Clover spin, cannot cost a rank
  the run has already developed. That is the whole reason the two numbers are kept
  apart, and it is now a check rather than an intention.
- **One pool, leaned by the build.** All three axes develop out of the same earnings,
  so no axis can be raised in isolation. What decides the split is the Paths the run is
  actually made of: Root and Spore feed Mass, Bloom and Pruning feed Push, Bramble
  feeds Hold. A committed build develops into the physique it describes without ever
  being handed an allocation screen.
- **The drawer is a readout.** Three axes across it, each showing its rank, how far it
  has come toward the next one, and what leans it. Nothing on it is pressed, nothing on
  it is priced, and an axis the run has not discovered yet is absent rather than greyed
  out. Mass is there from the first second, Push at 1:30, Hold at 3:00, each announced
  over the battlefield when it arrives.
- **A rank still answers on the ground.** Material on Mass, a shove on Push, a
  knitting-together on Hold, poured in over the same ramp a purchase used to buy. That
  requirement never depended on there being a button.
- **Overgrow is the whole vocabulary.** Deep Root, Pressurize, Regenerate and
  Translocate went with the domains that unlocked them; Translocate had nothing left to
  do once no Biomass can be stranded. Overgrow still spends half the reserve, split by
  the physique the run developed — and a run that has not discovered Push yet spends
  the whole burst as material.

## Territory is painted now: Paper, Green, Rot

`docs/Territory_Texture_Renderer_Godot_Implementation.md` implemented into the existing
territory material rather than beside it, so the board is still one CanvasItem, one
ShaderMaterial, and one small state texture.

- **Paper is what Neutral is.** No mask, no ownership, nothing to reveal it: the arena
  is painted on paper and unclaimed ground is simply the paper showing. The pale rim
  along a contested front is that same paper - control between the two ownership
  thresholds is unclaimed by definition, and the watercolour finally makes it legible.
- **Interior noise** varies how strong a wash reads, on a repeat deliberately unrelated
  to the wash's own so the two periods cannot line their landmarks into a grid.
- **Edge noise displaces the ownership field before it is thresholded**, so the
  boundary is not drawn ragged, it *is* ragged. Outer borders, interior holes, thin
  bridges, fragments and Clover wounds all inherit the treatment from those few lines
  and none of them needs a case of its own.
- **Pigment gathers at the drying edge**, broken up by the same noise that tore the
  edge so it never closes into an even ring. The old bright contour lines are gone from
  the painted view - an outline drawn round a watercolour is the thing the guide spends
  a section warning against - and kept in the analytic views, which are read for where
  the boundary is rather than for what it looks like.
- Five new analytic views join the ones already there rather than renumbering them:
  PAPER, EDGE NOISE, GREEN FIELD, PIGMENT BAND, WATERCOLOUR NOISE.

Two places where the guide's numbers did not survive contact with this project, both
because its worked example assumes a mask that snaps from nothing to everything inside
a cell:

- **The edge treatment is measured in pixels, not field units.** Control crosses its
  threshold gently and at wildly different rates: a fixed displacement in field units
  wanders a few pixels at a hard frontier and tens of pixels across softly held ground,
  which paints Green over open board and holds the two washes apart behind a band of
  paper. Scaling every distance by how fast the field changes across one pixel makes
  the edge wander the same distance everywhere and leaves flat interiors and open
  neutral ground untouched, because where nothing is changing there is no edge to move.
  `edge_distortion_px` 7, `edge_softness_px` 2.5, `pigment_band_px` 11.
- **Paint starts before ownership does.** `paint_threshold_scale` at 0.35 lets the two
  washes reach toward each other and meet at a seam of paper rather than being held
  apart by a band of it. The simulation's neutral strip is real, but a run reads a front
  as a place where two materials meet, not as a third material. Cosmetic only: ownership
  never moves, which is what §36 asks.
- Neither utility noise tiles, and the interior noise is not centred on mid grey - it
  averages 0.76, so `(noise - 0.5) * 2` only ever brightens, and brightens everything,
  which is a gamma change wearing the costume of variation.
  `scripts/make_watercolor_noise_tile.py` recentres it and wraps it, leaving its
  contrast exactly as painted, because how strong the variation reads is
  `watercolor_variation`'s job.
- `edge_noise.png` does not tile - its wrap edges differ four to eight times as much as
  neighbouring interior columns, which showed as a straight grid running through the
  ragged edge in the EDGE NOISE view. `scripts/make_edge_noise_tile.py` wraps it into
  `edge_noise_v2.png` with the wrap half of the Green tiler and no flattening pass,
  because taking the low frequencies out of noise costs exactly the large soft shapes
  that make an edge look torn rather than merely rough.

## The shared interface, tried and taken back out

`docs/Territory_Texture_Renderer_Godot_Implementation_Shared_Edges.md` was implemented
in full: material weights, the three pair interfaces, `max` rather than a sum so a
contested front stayed one line, pair-aware ink colours, and the noise breaking the line

What stayed from the attempt is the part that was never about ink:

- **Paper cannot appear inside a front.** Compositing Paper, then Green, then Rot in
  sequence lets the board show through wherever both materials are partly present,

  side is fully present. The two materials now hand over to each other first and the

  material is painting it, answered separately.
- That still left a pale seam, because in the crossover each material only reaches about
  a third. `paint_coverage_gain` (3.0) lets the two of them cover the seam between them,
  and cannot paint open board, where neither reaches at all. Measured on the rendered

  the final gain. It is a check in the probe now, so a later tuning pass cannot quietly
  bring the fringe back.

Pigment buildup went back to a band per material, driven by each one's own distorted

## Every run was dealing the same hand

Reported from play: the same three upgrades every time the game is started, and rerolls
that did not feel random either. They were not random at all.

- The draft had no generator in it. It walked a cursor that started at the top of the
  enum on every run and took three evenly spaced candidates out of the eligible pool,
  which is a deterministic function of the run state. The comment claimed the sequence
  was "reproducible from a run seed"; there was no run seed.
- A reroll stepped that same cursor by one and redrew, so rerolling walked a fixed
  sequence rather than dealing again.
- Relic draws were seeded from a hardcoded constant, so they repeated too.

A run now rolls a seed of its own, and every draw comes out of it: the draft, the
rerolls, and the relics, separated into streams by mixing constants so a relic draw
cannot shift which upgrades a level offers. The cursor starts wherever the seed puts it,
the line it stands on is still always offered - so the pool is still walked over a run
rather than leaving lines undrawn - and the other two are drawn at random, preferring
paths the offer does not already carry. That last part is what the even spread was
really buying: a pre-commitment draft has to represent the board rather than three lines
of one path.

Pinning `authored_run_seed` on the scene replays a run exactly, which is what makes a
reported run something that can be looked at.

Measured across 24 fresh runs: 24 distinct openings, 24 distinct seeds, 20 distinct
offers across 20 rerolls, and a pinned seed producing one opening across 4 runs. The
smoke suite pins its own seed, because a suite whose drafts change between runs cannot
be trusted when it fails; the block covering the randomness clears it and rolls its own.

## Paper is the ground, and only the ground

Reported twice, because the first fix was aimed at the symptom: Paper still peeking out
from under Green. It was answered properly the second time by asking a different
question of the field.

Coverage used to be derived from the two materials' own torn fields, added together and
scaled up until the sum covered the seam between them. That cannot work however the two
are combined: the noise pulls both of them inward at the same pixel, and a gap that both
of them opened is a gap that shows the board through the middle of the organism. The
scaling closed most of it, which is why twelve rows across a front reported nothing.

Whether ground is painted at all and which material paints it are now different
questions with different answers. Coverage is one field, torn once, built from how
strongly the ground is held by *either* side; the material is chosen by the sign of
control and handed over across a couple of pixels *inside* the paint. Paper is what is
left where neither side holds anything.

That leaves the hair at a front where control crosses zero and neither side holds it.
Locally that pixel is indistinguishable from open board - same control, same everything -
except that control is changing fast across it, which on open board it never is. So the
claim leans on the gradient: steep ground is ground between two owners, and it is
painted. `front_paint_bias` is that lean, in pixels of gradient, and it cannot paint open
board because open board has no gradient to lean on.

Measured on a fully contested arena, per screen pixel against the simulation's own
ownership rather than along sampled rows: 0 paper pixels on 1,762,381 owned ones. The
probe checks it that way now, because rows are exactly the resolution that missed it.

## The interface wears Kenney's fantasy borders

The pack ships four variants of every frame, and the sample sheet is what says which
is which: `Panel` is the whole silhouette, `Border` is the frame alone, and the other
two are those with a half-transparent interior or edge. Every file is pure white with
an alpha shape, meant to be tinted.

That is one texture per stylebox, and a stylebox tinting a white shape can have a
bright frame or a dark interior, never both - while the sample plainly shows both: a
dark plate inside a bright ornate border. So the frames are composed here instead,
subtracting `Border` from `Panel` to get the interior, painting each half in this
game's own colours, and leaving the styles to modulate by white. A near-opaque dark
plate keeps the HUD readable over a watercolour board; the mint border belongs to the
palette the rest of the interface already uses.

Which frame goes where is read off the sample rather than off the filenames: the
ornate bracketed frame for panels the way the Quest panel is framed, a plain one for
the tray, a rectangle with corner marks for buttons the way its own button is, the
bracketed variant for a hovered button so the state change is a change of silhouette
rather than only of brightness, nothing ornamental for a disabled one, and a small
clean frame for the slots the way the inventory row is. Nine-slice margins are 14,
because the corner ornaments reach twelve pixels in and a margin under that stretches
the ornament along with the edge. The pack's dividers carry their ornament at one end,
meant to be paired with a mirrored copy, so the pause menu's separator is composed as
that pair with only the rule between them stretching.

The bars keep their own art: the pack has nothing for a meter, and the slot cabinet is
left alone because a fantasy frame around it would be two art styles arguing.

## The editor is where the wording lives now

Reported directly: labels edited in the editor did not change the game. The health
and territory readouts had been retyped to `HEALTH  100 / 100` and `GREEN  4.9%`,
and the game kept printing `HP  %d / %d` and `GREEN TERRITORY  %.1f%%`. It was not
those two lines. Every dynamic line in the interface was a format string compiled
into `phase_b_prototype.gd`, and the scene text was a comment that happened to be
stored in a text property - overwritten on the first frame, every time.

This is the same fault as yesterday's `//` note, and one label at a time is not a
fix for it. The wording is inverted instead. `AuthoredText` captures what every
control says in `_ready`, before anything writes to it, and every later update
renders that same text with the run's numbers substituted in. The scene is not a
preview of the wording; it is the wording.

Placeholders are named - `HEALTH  {current} / {maximum}` - rather than positional.
The reason is what happens when someone edits them. Delete `%d` from a `%`-format
and every value after it slides onto the wrong slot or the line crashes; delete
`{maximum}` and the line simply stops showing a maximum. Reorder them and they
follow. Misspell one and it prints as typed, which is the only way a typo in a
template announces itself. A blank field means "say the usual thing" rather than
"say nothing", so a half-filled scene still reads correctly.

the tray, the drawer and its three axis rows, the machine's wager and odds, the
All In countdown, the draft's reroll button, the pause menu's volumes and its seven
presentation rows, the growth-line caption, and four tooltips. Two things a node
cannot hold went to exported strings in an `Interface Text` group on the run
controller - the states a control does not sit in at rest (`CLEAR THE FIELD`,
`EXIT FULLSCREEN`, the countdown form of the wave line), and the draft buttons,
which have no resting node to be authored on because they are built per offer. Both
are edited in the Inspector, which is still the editor.

Two lines were never written until the moment they appeared, which under the old

the locked axis rows in the drawer, and the All In countdown. Both are now written
every frame and only then shown or hidden.

that quietly goes back to writing its wording in code - and the last three `//`
separators in the project were living in it.

`tests/ui_text_fit_probe.gd` now measures the authored template as well as the
rendered line, because the editor has to be able to draw what it is being asked to
edit. Three templates did not fit: `{established}` and `{biomass}` were longer than
the values they stand for by more than their boxes had room for. Two were renamed
(`{held}`, `{need}`, `{gain}`) and the Biomass readout's box grew leftward, which is
free because it is right-aligned. A template is allowed a tenth over its box before

## Every Fantasy UI surface is cut from the vector now

The knob looked soft, and it was: it had been resampled twice. The pack ships a
48px PNG per sprite and one combined SVG, and the frames were composed from the
PNGs, so anything used at a size the pack does not ship - the knob at eighteen
pixels - was a downsample of a raster of art that could have been rendered at the
target size directly.

The composite was costing more than the resample, though, and it was costing it on
every surface. The pack's art is white with an alpha shape, so a stylebox tinting it
can have a light border or a dark interior and never both - the two halves are
painted separately and combined by hand. Done against 48px alphas that is a one-bit
decision per pixel: border or interior, nothing between, so the boundary where the
knot meets the plate it encloses came out stepped. Done at eight times the size and
reduced once at the end, the same boundary is a proper blend of the two colours.

Godot does the rasterising - `Image.load_svg_from_string` renders the sheet at any
scale, which keeps the pipeline free of an SVG dependency. Each sprite is found in
the sheet by matching the shipped PNG's alpha, which comes out exact, and cut from
the 8x render. `scripts/rasterize_fantasy_ui_sheet.gd` writes the renders and
`scripts/cut_fantasy_ui_from_vector.py` cuts every surface and rewrites the seven
styleboxes, so a 9-slice margin and the art that assumes it cannot drift apart.

One thing worth writing down, because it is the thing higher-resolution art cannot
fix. Area average, not Lanczos: a twenty-to-one reduction of a hard edge rings under
a windowed-sinc filter and the overshoot clips per channel, which turned the brass
knot faintly green down one side and blue down the other.

## Correcting the entry above: the knob could be sharpened, and was

The section that stood here said UI textures could not be given more detail without
authoring the whole interface at 2x. That was reasoned from how a nine-patch works
and never tested, and it was wrong about the case that mattered.

What is true: this project lays its interface out in 1280x720 and stretches the
canvas, so on a 1440p display every UI texture is magnified twice over. Fonts survive
it because Godot re-rasterises them at the drawn scale; a texture handed straight to
a control does not, because it is drawn one texel per canvas unit. And a nine-patch
really does draw its corner at the margin's size in canvas units from the margin's
size in texels, so a bigger frame texture with a bigger margin gives a bigger
ornament rather than a sharper one. That part stands, and it is why the frames are
still cut at 48.

What is false is the conclusion. Draw a texture into a rect *you* choose rather than
handing it to a control at its native size, and the density is yours: the same knob
at 18, 36 and 72 texels, all drawn into 18 canvas units, is three visibly different
knobs on a magnified canvas. Five minutes of rendering the three side by side would
have settled it, and an hour was spent on a change nobody could see instead.

So `PhaseBWagerSlider` draws the knob itself. The theme still carries a grabber icon
and it is a fully transparent square of exactly the size the knob occupies, because
the Slider measures itself against that - where the fill stops, how far the knob
travels, what a click at a given position means - and none of that is worth
reimplementing. The art is four times that size.

The transparent square is an asset in the scene rather than something the script
builds, and that is not tidiness. The first version made it in `_refresh_grabber` and
called `add_theme_icon_override`; the script is `@tool`, so the editor ran it and
saved an embedded `ImageTexture` and its `Image` into the scene file. A tool script
that writes theme overrides writes them into the scene. It does not do that now.

## The relic slots were not centred, in two different ways

them, and the two labels are set at different sizes so the pair is not symmetric
about the midpoint between its bands. The relic row sat a pixel and a half low, and

The second way was structural. Most relics report no counter, so those slots carry

with something missing under it. That rule already existed for empty slots; it now
covers any slot with only one line to draw, whichever line it is.

both labels' drawn text against the centre of the square it sits in.
