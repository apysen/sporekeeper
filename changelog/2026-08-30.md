# Changelog: 2026-08-30

## The 72-relic catalog, Vine and Root reworks, prebaked enemy art, and a peak-density performance pass

This entry covers work completed after `2026-08-29.md`. It records the final
behavior of systems that went through multiple revisions rather than preserving
superseded intermediate versions.

---

## 1. `docs/relics.md` became the master document for relics

- The catalog was rebuilt to exactly **72 relics in nine pools of eight** — Plant plus
  one pool per Adaptation (Spore, Bloom, Bramble, Root, Pruning, Thistle, Vine, Fruit) —
  each pool holding three Common, two Uncommon, two Rare, and one Legendary.
- Adopting the document deleted 27 relics that predated it, along with their behavior:
  all 12 Universal relics, five curated Synergies, five extra Plant-element relics,
  Patient Zero, and all four Clover relics. **Clover deliberately has no relic pool.**
  The removed code remains recoverable from history before the migration.
- Rarity is now rolled **per offered slot** against a run-state table, falling
  **downward only** when a rarity is exhausted. The four run states are single
  Adaptation, two Adaptations pre-Evolution, post-Evolution, and support/late.
- Offers contain only Plant relics and relics of Adaptations the run **permanently
  owns**. Eligibility reads `has_path`, never `has_path_active`, so a Clover jackpot
  loan cannot open a pool the run does not actually hold.
- One copy per run, and a Campaign run receives 10-14 relics.
- All 72 relic behaviors are implemented, together with the document's global
  generated-event scheduler: 12 events per frame, 48 queued, a 0.05s stagger between
  same-frame accents, and rarity-priority dropping when the queue is full.
- Relic visual cues carry the document's two mechanical rules: echoes render at 70-85%
  of the source intensity, and same-frame accents are staggered rather than stacked.

---

## 2. The Vine became a persistent simulated rope

- Added `phase_b_vine_whip.gd`: a Verlet chain of 9-18 points at 12px segments, four
  constraint iterations per step, with separate damping for the anchor and the tail.
- While Vine is held, the Cane resolves **through the simulated rope** rather than
  through its authored sector, so hits land across the sweep window instead of
  instantly. Swept-segment collision resolves what the rope actually crossed.
- The Apical zone is measured by arc length along the rope rather than by radius.
- Climber uses a line-of-sight `get_wrap_index()` rather than firing on any sharp bend,
  which previously triggered on ordinary slack.
- The guide is applied as a **rate**, not as a position correction. An earlier form
  scaled a position error by `delta * delta`, which made the guide vanish at frame
  rate; two Vine smoke cases caught it.

---

## 3. Root lost its player-facing Biomass layer and gained Lateral Roots

- **Biomass Yield became Lateral Roots**: +24px of projected Root reach per rank,
  measured through contiguous owned Green so support never crosses a wall, void, or
  basalt.
- The player-facing Biomass and Metabolism layer is gone — the drawer, the three axis
  readouts, the Biomass counter, and Overgrow. Metabolism itself still runs, developing
  out of lifetime earnings as hidden territory strength.
- Biomass therefore still accrues and is still paid out by relic-overflow and
  exhausted-draft rewards; the player simply cannot see it. Whether those consolation
  payouts should become something visible is an open design question rather than a bug.

---

## 4. Adaptation Paths are no longer offered as rewards

A reward screen presented bare Adaptation Paths instead of upgrades. The mechanism
behind it was redundant: drafting a line from an uncommitted Path already commits that
Path, filling the next open slot.

- Removed the `path_choice_requested` signal, `request_primary_path_choice`,
  `request_secondary_path_choice`, `choose_path`, and the three pending-path readers.
- Removed `ChoiceKind.PATH`, its button template, and the primary and secondary Path
  headers from the run prototype.
- Removed the `primary_path_choice_seconds` and `secondary_path_choice_seconds`
  milestones from the wave director, and the now-unused `get_path_description`.
- The one scheduled choice that survives is the evolved run's **support Path**
  (`tertiary_path_choice_seconds`), and that one is itself a draft of real upgrade
  lines rather than a list of Paths.

## 5. Campaign difficulty and density

Campaign scaled too slowly and felt empty in early waves. Measurement showed enemies
reach the 130 concurrent cap at 4.5 minutes and stay pinned there, so the cap — not the
spawn rate — is the difficulty ceiling, and the cap exists because of renderer cost.

- Pressure now builds on its own 900-second clock (`pressure_ramp_seconds`) rather than
  across the whole timeline, so a thirty-minute run is a bigger run rather than a slower
  one. It reaches full pressure around the boss and holds.
- The opening is denser than Quick's rather than sparser: early spawn interval 1.35s to
  **0.95s**, and late pack size arrives at 45% progress instead of 62%.
- Specialist introductions were pulled far forward: Saboteur 188s to **115s**, Corruptor
  450s to **270s**, Colonizer 713s to **430s**, Warden 1050s to **630s**.

---

## 6. Enemy art is prebaked per role instead of dropped at high density

Rendering enemies without their art at high densities read as wrong and distracting.
The crowd LOD was replaced rather than tuned.

- `_draw_enemy` now takes a canvas, so one art definition serves both live play and the
  bake. There is no second copy to drift.
- Each role bakes once into a `SubViewport` and becomes an `ImageTexture`, at **six
  variants per role** because the Heavy Grazer rotates its plating by `id % 6` — baking
  one would have made every Heavy Grazer identical.
- Drawing is now a single textured quad per enemy: a Hunter goes from two canvas
  commands to one, a Rusher from about ten to one. The Rusher bakes facing +X and the
  quad is rotated, so heading still reads.
- **The flat-shape LOD is deleted.** There is no longer a density at which enemies stop
  looking like themselves. `crowd_visual_lod_enemy_threshold` now governs only pickup
  and projectile ornamentation.
- Overgrown and hit-flash moved into a shared `_draw_enemy_state_overlay`. Both are
  drawn *inside* the art, so a baked quad would have silently dropped the Overgrown
  marker on any infected enemy.
- Warden and Siege Corruptor are excluded from the bake — they already always draw in
  full detail.
- If the bake fails, the previous immediate-mode rendering is used. `enemy_art_bake_probe.gd`
  verifies that fallback: headless has no rendering driver, so the bake correctly fails
  there and 120 enemies still draw without error.

Baked output has **not** been visually confirmed — the available binary is headless-only,
so sprite alignment, scale, padding, and Rusher heading still need checking in a real
run. `enemy_art_bake_padding` and `enemy_art_variants` are exported for that.

## 7. Placeholder VFX art

- Added `assets/placeholder_art/` with eight soft primitives — particle, glow, ring,
  shockwave, streak, spark, burst, swirl — regenerated by
  `generate_placeholder_art.py` beside them.
- These are **effect** sprites, not UI icons: soft alpha falloff instead of a hard
  silhouette, white with a shaped alpha channel so color comes from the draw-time tint.
  That is what lets one streak serve Bramble's purple and Vine's green from one file.
- Directional forms point along +X so the overlay's rotation aims them; radial forms are
  centered so they can be scaled up as a wave.
- Anything with structure — a spray fan, a thorny strand, a row of stubble — is drawn by
  repeating one primitive along a path at draw time rather than baking a bespoke sprite
  per relic, which keeps the whole pool looking like one family.
- The sixteen older per-relic `cue_*.png` files were removed.

## 9. Peak-density performance pass

### The Root Network projection was flood-filling per enemy per frame

`TerritoryField.get_mature_projection_at` ran one breadth-first search **per query**,
outward from the queried cell until it found Mature Green. It has two early exits —
reach is zero without Lateral Roots, and it returns immediately on unowned or
already-mature ground — and fresh Green defeats both.

The result: every enemy standing on young owned Green ran its own flood fill every
frame. With a crowd on a freshly painted Fruit patch that is 130 flood fills per frame,
measured at **38ms inside `_update_enemy_spore`**.

- It now runs **one** pass outward from every Mature cell, filling a projection value
  for the whole field, cached against a `_field_revision` counter. Distance through
  Green is symmetric, so one inward pass answers every query the outward passes did.
- Visited tracking moved from a `Dictionary` to a `PackedByteArray`; the hashing was a
  real share of the old cost.
- `_mark_field_changed()` is called from the ten places that write control or maturity.
  `step_simulation` and `analyze_topology_now` bump at **both** ends, because they write
  throughout their bodies — a single bump at entry would let a query made partway
  through cache half-written state and then survive the write.

**Root+Fruit at the enemy cap: p99 45.8ms to 23.1ms, combat p99 40.9ms to 3.95ms.**

### What that did not fix

The pass was scoped to one outlier. Twelve of 28 Adaptation pairings remain over the
16.67ms p99 budget at peak density, and every one of them contains Thistle or Fruit:

- **Thistle** costs 10-30ms in the adaptation director and spikes on a volley cadence —
  median 4-5ms, p95 around 11ms, then p99 doubles. Seven to 28 frames per 600 miss.
- **Fruit** runs broadly heavy: about 15ms in the adaptation director, and it inflates
  territory from a baseline 8.4ms to 12-16ms because it trails Green and so writes to
  the field far more than other paths. 25-68 frames per 600 miss.
- Worst rows are Thistle+Fruit at ~34.8ms and Thistle+Vine at ~30.0ms p99.

Median frame time is healthy everywhere (3.4-9.0ms), so this is tail latency — it reads
as stutter rather than as low frame rate. **All of these numbers are simulation only**;
headless has no rendering driver, so real frames are worse, not better.

### Investigation notes worth keeping

Two hypotheses were measured and disproven before the real cause was found, and both are
worth not re-testing:

- **Fruit count is not the cost.** Root Potency does raise a release from 6 to 11 Fruit
  on mature ground, and a Clover jackpot raises it to **16** (the ceiling is 16, not 15,
  because the release is `1 + effective(Fruit Set)`). Combat cost is flat across 6, 11,
  and 16 — if anything it drops.
- **Relics are not the cost.** The spike is identical with zero relics held, and the
  relic director itself measures 0.018ms, so the generated-event scheduler is doing its
  job.

# Second pass, 2026-08-30: density headroom

Picks up the thread the section above left open — Thistle's volley cadence, then
Fruit's territory writes — and closes it. **All 28 Adaptation pairings now hold p99
within 16.67ms at the 130 cap**, from twelve failing. The worst pairing went from
34.9ms to about 15ms.

Almost none of that came from the architecture the optimization guide schedules. No
spatial grid, no data-oriented enemy rewrite, no MultiMesh, no GDExtension. Seven of
the eight fixes were the same defect in different clothes, and finding them needed
measurement before each one rather than a plan drawn up in advance.

## 11. A harness first, because several diagnoses turned out to be wrong

`docs/performance_optimization_implementation_guide.md` describes the intended work.
Its frame numbers reproduced almost exactly — Thistle+Fruit measured 34.3ms p99
against its stated 34.8 — but three of its diagnoses did not survive contact with
per-subsystem timing, and acting on them would have built the wrong things first:

- It attributes Fruit's cost to repeated Green trail writes. The trail enqueue is
  **0.03ms**. The cost was collision, and then the hit cascade.
- It puts the Thistle spike in target acquisition and swept collision — the two
  things a shared spatial grid fixes. Those measured **1.3ms and 1.6ms of a 15.9ms
  spike**. The spike was one unbounded ricochet search.
- It schedules the spatial grid first. Nothing at the 130 cap needed it. At 200
  enemies `thistle_targeting_ms` was the first bar that would genuinely have
  benefited, so that justification exists now where it did not before.

Fixture A is the guide's central claim confirmed rather than contradicted: going
from 130 to 200 enemies costs **0.56ms**. Raw density is very nearly free. What does
not scale is the per-Adaptation work run against the crowd.

`PhaseBPerfCounters` holds the named timers and volume counters. It is off by
default — a disabled counter is one static bool read — and hot loops accumulate into
a local and report once per pass. `density_ladder_probe.gd` runs the guide's six
fixtures up a density ladder with those counters and writes CSV.

Three things about the probe were wrong at first and are worth not repeating:

- **The worst frame in every sample was frame #1** — first volley, pools reaching
  working size. It now discards 30 warm-up frames, so the worst-frame breakdown
  describes combat instead of start-up.
- **Per-subsystem p99s cannot say what a slow frame did**, because each is the 99th
  percentile of its own distribution and they need not come from the same frame. The
  probe now captures the worst frame's own breakdown alongside them.
- **In-game counters only cover subsystems already suspected**, so anything
  unsuspected showed up as an unattributed remainder — 23.45ms of a 36.53ms frame at
  one point. The probe now times each of the six phases it drives, directly.

## 12. Twelve failing pairings became none

Seven fixes, all the same shape: a hot loop asking a question it already had the
answer to, or calling a by-id accessor that was secretly a scan of the enemy pool.

| site | what it was doing |
| --- | --- |
| `_find_fruit_segment_hit` | a position and a radius per enemy, per Fruit |
| `_find_ricochet_target` | a position per enemy, plus a field line walk per enemy, per ricochet, per thorn |
| `_collect_enemy_targets` | a position per enemy, on every Thistle fire and every mounted head of every Cane sweep |
| `_fire_mounted_head` | a position per surviving candidate |
| `_begin_normal_fruit_release` | an identical roster-wide aim query per Fruit, up to sixteen times for one origin |
| `_stamp_growth` / `_stamp_bloom_surge` | a source-to-cell ray walk per cell, when the whole stamp was over open ground and every ray was 1.0 |

The territory one is worth spelling out. `_claimable_line_transmission` returns
exactly 1.0 when its ray crosses nothing blocked, and a Bresenham ray between two
points in an axis-aligned box never leaves that box. So one scan of the box proves
every ray inside it is 1.0. That is exact rather than approximate, and
`green_wash_probe` returns byte-identical output across the change.

**The eighth fix is the Cane's densest-target search**, the only genuine algorithm
change. Its sector sweep tested every candidate against every target: 585 x 195
`atan2` calls in one frame. It now sorts target angles once, lays them out three
times so a window is contiguous with no wrap-around case to get wrong, and
summarises each candidate from prefix sums — splitting the window at the candidate's
own angle turns the sum of `|candidate - target|` into two prefix-sum differences.

That search decides where the sweep goes, through a four-stage tie-breaker at 0.0001
thresholds, so `cane_targeting_reference_probe.gd` compares the fast paths against
the original over 1,760 randomized crowds — both routing modes, targets placed on
the sector edge and collinear to force ties. **It caught a real bug on its first
run**: the stored "directions" are not unit vectors, because gathering divides by
the routed path length, so a dot product read them as wider angles than they were.
`angle_to` never noticed, being scale invariant.

Cane at 200 enemies: `cane_total_ms` p99 **23.99ms to 7.19ms**, sector sweep
**12.09ms to 2.10ms**.

## 13. Enemy lookup by id was a linear scan, sixteen times over

`_resolve_fruit_enemy_hit` asks combat about the same enemy eight to ten times —
`is_enemy_active`, `get_enemy_spore`, `get_enemy_entanglement`, `damage_enemy_by_id`
and more — and every one of those walked the enemy pool. `_resolve_thorn_hit` does
it six times.

`PhaseBCombatDirector` now keeps `_enemy_slot_by_id`, and all sixteen accessors
funnel through `_find_enemy`. **The map is a shortcut, never the authority**: a slot
is accepted only if it still holds an active enemy with the id asked for, and
anything else falls through to the original scan, so a stale entry can make a lookup
slower but never wrong. `enemy_lookup_fallbacks` counts that and reads zero across a
600-frame 200-enemy fixture. Three more accessors that already routed through
`_find_enemy` became O(1) without being touched.

## 14. Damage numbers, which turned out to be the largest rendered cost

Finding a slot for one scanned the whole pool, then scanned it again for the oldest
when the pool was full — a blast landing on a hundred enemies paid that a hundred
times. The free-slot half is now a cursor: everything below it is active, so the
first inactive slot at or above it is the lowest in the pool, which is exactly the
slot the scan returned.

**The full-pool half is still the original scan, deliberately.** A queue of slots in
activation order was tried first, reasoning that uniform ageing makes activation
order the same as age order. `damage_number_pool_probe.gd` disproved that under
sustained saturation: the replacement recycled a different slot than the scan would
have. Which number survives a saturated frame is visible, so it stays exact until a
fast version provably agrees.

Then `draw_string` turned out to be re-shaping every number's text on every frame,
for text fixed at spawn — 3.49ms per frame for a screenful of 160. The measurement
and point size now live on the number. `tools/bake_damage_digit_atlas.gd` bakes the
ten digits of Beholden Regular into one strip with per-digit advances written beside
it, and drawing steps through the string issuing one quad per digit out of that one
texture: no shaping, no font-atlas switching, and the whole layer batches. The text
path remains as a fallback if the asset is missing, and the texture and metrics load
as a pair.

Damage-number draw, 160 on screen at 2560x1440: median **3.24ms to 1.38ms**.

Two gameplay-visible changes came out of the same work.

**Thistle appeared to have no damage numbers at all.** Three values met: the saved
display setting is DAMAGE NUMBERS = IMPORTANT ONLY, `important_damage_number_threshold`
is 25, and a thorn hits for 12 — doubling to exactly 24 on a critical, one point
under the line. So the whole Adaptation was silent on ordinary enemies while the
Cane and Fruit, which hit harder, still showed. A critical now counts as important
whatever it hit for, which is what that setting's own help text says it keeps.

**Criticals draw larger and in their own colour** — 26pt against 14, in a hot
saturated gold. Picking that needed care rather than the obvious: every entry in
`_get_damage_feedback_color` sits in one yellow-green to warm-orange band, with
Bloom already a soft gold and Fruit a soft orange, so a conventional gold critical
would have read as another damage source. Both sizes come out of the same strip, so
the second size is free rather than a second glyph cache. The enemy's hit flash
keeps the source colour; only the number changes.

## 15. The rendered frame had never been measured

`_draw` does not run headless. Every draw timer in the suite read exactly zero while
the simulation was taken apart seven times over — the guide's definition-of-done
requirement for rendered validation going unmet rather than merely deferred.

`rendered_density_probe.gd` lets the engine run the frame with a drafted evolved
build, disables vsync so the frame column measures work rather than the wait for
vblank, and attributes the combat director's `_draw` and the layers inside it.

| n | frame p50 | frame p99 | combat draw p99 | enemy draw p99 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 130 | 16.04 | 30.02 | 5.67 | 4.57 |
| 200 | 22.89 | 41.93 | 8.11 | 7.17 |

Two readings. The combat director's own drawing is a **small and near-linear** part
of the frame — the prebaked enemy art is doing its job, so the enemy MultiMesh work
the guide schedules is not where the rendered cost lives. And the frame is far
heavier than the headless gate implies: the gate passes every pairing at 130 while
the rendered p99 there is 30ms. That is a debug build at one window size on one
machine, and exported release builds are substantially faster, so it is not a
verdict on shipping performance — but **the headless table alone cannot answer
whether the game holds 60fps**, and until now nothing else was asking.

## 16. Where the density ladder stands

Headless simulation, frame p99, after everything above:

| fixture | 130 | 160 | 200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A generic saturation | 9.02 | 9.42 | 9.32 |
| C max Fruit | 16.70 | 21.35 | 26.38 |
| D Thistle + Fruit | 15.05 | 15.73 | 19.16 |
| E Thistle + Vine | 11.86 | 13.85 | 15.30 |

**Thistle + Vine, the second-worst pairing at the start, now holds 200 enemies with
zero frames over budget in 600.** Thistle + Fruit clears 160. Milestone A — every
fixture under budget at 160 — is met by three of the four, and **max Fruit is the
only one out**, at 21.35.

### Correction: the probes were running territory at half rate

These numbers are the second set. The first set was measured with the territory
field stepped one frame in four, copied from `peak_density_probe` without checking
it against the field's own cadence.

`TerritoryField` is tuned at 30Hz simulation and 30Hz render. At 60fps both fall
due every other frame, and its `_process` alternates them when both are due, which
lands **one territory operation on every frame** — twice what the probes were doing.
Every territory-heavy number in the first set was therefore optimistic, medians
most of all: fixture C's median went from 3.81ms to 7.14ms once corrected.

Both probes now alternate every frame. **The gate still passes at the true cadence**,
but with far less room than the first measurement suggested: Thistle + Fruit lands
at **16.59ms against the 16.67ms budget**, and medians across the 28 pairings roughly
doubled, from 3.4-5.5ms to 7.2-8.2ms.

The lesson is the one this pass keeps relearning: a fixture that does not reproduce
the cadence the game runs at is measuring a different game.

## 18. What still needs attention

**Max Fruit is the remaining headless problem**, and what is left in it is genuine
gameplay work rather than accessor waste. At 200 enemies: `fruit_salad_ms` 13.30,
`blast_damage_ms` 13.20, `fruitseeds_ms` 10.10, `fruit_bloom_ms` 9.24. These are AoE
cascades — `_try_fruit_salad`, `_create_fruit_bloom` and `_germinate_fruitseed` all
funnel into `blast_enemies_in_radius`, and the cost is `_damage_enemy` and what a
kill sets off, not the search that found the targets. The guide's §12 event buffers
are the shape of the fix, and it is a larger change than anything above.

**Territory batching is still untouched.** `territory_batch_ms` runs 6-12ms on its
worst frames with a measured **88% duplicate write ratio** — 5,266 cells touched for
601 unique on one sampled frame. The guide's §8.3 dirty-cell coalescing is well
supported by that number and has not been attempted.

**The territory and ricochet fixes are exact by argument plus probe evidence, not by
a differential test.** The guide's §24 `territory_batch_reference_probe.gd` — old
implementation against new, cell by cell — does not exist and would be the rigorous
check.

**Rendered performance needs a release build.** Everything above is a debug build on
one machine at 2560x1440. The rendered numbers are the first hard data on drawing
cost this project has had, and they are not yet a shipping verdict.

**Rendering is not where the rendered frame goes.** With render CPU and GPU measured
separately — `viewport_set_measure_render_time`, verified to report real, load-responsive
numbers on both GL Compatibility and Forward+ by `render_timing_support_probe.gd` —
the drafted Thistle + Fruit fixture splits as:

Roughly **70% of the rendered frame is not rendering**, and the renderer's share
grows only gently with density. Part of that gap was the territory cadence bug above.
Whatever remains is the live scene doing work the headless fixtures do not step —
other nodes' `_process`, the HUD, the aftergrowth layer — and that is where a
rendered investigation should look first, not at draw calls.

**Three probes cannot run headless** and fail in a way that reads like an assertion
failure rather than a missing driver: `green_wash_probe`, `root_stage_probe` and
`enemy_role_probe` all read the framebuffer. All three pass with a display. The two
new rendered probes have the same requirement and say so in their headers.

**Two open questions were left rather than answered.** Fixture E is faster at 200
than at 160 in an earlier run — the layouts are not scaled copies, since enemies sit
on `index % 7` rings, so it is more likely a real property of the two geometries
than noise, but it was not investigated. And making criticals important pushed peak
damage numbers on screen from 17 to 147 in a 60-enemy fixture, which makes IMPORTANT
ONLY behave close to FULL for a fully ranked Thistle; that may be more than the
setting is meant to show, and narrowing what counts as critical is a one-line change
if so.

**Beholden Regular is a light face at 14pt.** It reads cleanly in captures, but
small light numerals over a busy fight is the case a still image flatters most.
`Beholden-Medium.ttf` is already in the project — changing `FONT_PATH` in the bake
tool and re-running is the whole change.
